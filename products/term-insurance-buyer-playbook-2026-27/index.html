Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

At what age should I buy term insurance?

As early as you have a dependant or a liability. Premiums are lowest in your twenties and rise sharply with age — a Rs. 1 crore cover costs roughly 2.7 times more at 40 than at 25 for the same term. Buying young locks in the low price for the whole term.

How much term insurance cover do I actually need?

Use the playbook's formula: (monthly household expenses x 12 x years of support) + all outstanding loans + future goals - existing assets and cover. Most salaried professionals with a home loan and dependants land between Rs. 1.5 crore and Rs. 3 crore, well above the old 10-times-income rule.

Do I pay GST on a term insurance premium now?

No. Individual life insurance policies, including term plans, have been exempt from GST since 22 September 2025, following the 56th GST Council decision. Group and employer-sponsored policies still carry 18%.

Is the death benefit from a term plan taxable?

No. The sum assured paid to your nominee on death is fully exempt from income tax, regardless of the premium-to-cover ratio. Only maturity payouts on savings-type policies face thresholds.

Can the insurer reject my claim?

Within the first three years the insurer can question the policy if a material fact was misstated or hidden, and it must give written reasons. After three years, Section 45 of the Insurance Act bars the insurer from calling the policy into question on any ground at all. Full disclosure at the start removes the risk.

Is Saral Jeevan Bima the best plan for me?

It is a strong, simple, comparable plan, but its standard sum assured is capped at Rs. 25 lakh unless the insurer offers more. If your cover need is larger — which it usually is once you have a home loan or children — buy a regular term plan. Either way, check the claims record.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a general information guide that explains the rules, the costs and the process in plain English. It does not recommend any specific insurer or product and does not replace a qualified, fee-only financial adviser or a chartered accountant for your own situation.
