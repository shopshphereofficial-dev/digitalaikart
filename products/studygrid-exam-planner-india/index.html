The Problem It Solves

A syllabus is a list. A plan is a calendar. Most aspirants only ever have the list.

Every serious aspirant knows the shape of the problem. There are twenty-eight chapters, a hundred and ten days, and a fixed number of hours in each one. Somewhere between the first week's enthusiasm and the last month's panic, the arithmetic gets abandoned: the easy subject gets done twice, the hard one gets avoided, and the topics finished in August are not opened again until the mock tests show they have evaporated.

The usual fix is a timetable written on the back of a notebook — which is out of date by Thursday, because a class got cancelled or three revision sessions landed on the same day. StudyGrid keeps the arithmetic for you. It knows how many days are left, what is left in the syllabus, and which topics are due for revision today, and it redoes the sums whenever your syllabus or your available hours change. Regenerating takes one click; the alternative is an hour with a calendar and a calculator.

How the Plan Is Built

Simple enough to explain, because you have to trust it enough to follow it

1 · Count the days

From today — or a start date you choose — to the day before your exam, minus your weekly rest day. Multiply by your daily hours for total capacity, which the app shows as a percentage used.

2 · Interleave the subjects

Topics are taken in turns across subjects, weighted so a heavier subject comes up more often. The bigger topics in each subject are scheduled first, while there is still time to do them properly.

3 · Pack the first pass early

Each day is filled to at most 80% of your hours, leaving a fifth free for revision. That is why the plan front-loads new material and keeps the back half of the calendar for consolidating it.

4 · Book the revisions

Every finished topic gets its spaced-repetition dates, 30 minutes each by default. If a topic does not fit before the exam, the app counts it and tells you — it never quietly drops anything.

If the numbers do not work, StudyGrid says so in one line: how many topics did not fit at your current hours. The honest answers are to raise the daily hours, drop the rest day, or cut topics. There is no fourth option, and an app that hides that arithmetic is not helping you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Is this an ebook or a working app?

A working app. The ZIP contains one HTML file that opens in your browser as a complete study planner — syllabus, generated plan, revision schedule, mock-test scoring and a study log. There is nothing to install and no account to create.

Does it contain study material or question papers?

No. StudyGrid is the planning and tracking layer only. You type in your own subjects and topics from your official syllabus, and you use your own books, coaching material and test series. It does not include notes, question banks or practice papers, and it does not pretend to.

Does it know my exam's official syllabus?

No, deliberately. Exam bodies change syllabi and notifications, and a built-in list would be wrong the moment it shipped. You enter your topics yourself — about ten minutes for a typical paper — and edit them whenever the notification changes. The sample data that ships with the app is labelled Sample and its topic names are illustrative, not official.

How does the plan actually get built?

It counts the study days from today to the day before your exam, skipping your weekly rest day, and multiplies by your daily hours for total capacity. Topics are interleaved across subjects by weight — a subject at weight 2 comes up twice as often as one at weight 1 — and packed into the earliest days using at most 80% of each day, leaving room for revision. Every first-pass session then creates revision sessions 1, 3, 7 and 21 days later, moved to the next study day if the target falls on a rest day.

What if my syllabus does not fit before the exam?

The app says so plainly, with a count of the topics that did not fit. It never silently drops a topic or pretends a plan exists when it does not. Your options are to raise hours per day, remove the rest day, or cut topics — then regenerate. That is the whole arithmetic, and hiding it would not help you.

Does it work without the internet?

Yes. Once the folder is on your device the app works with the internet switched off. The file loads nothing from the web, and your syllabus and scores never leave your computer.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. Copy the file to the phone, open it in Chrome, and use the browser menu to add it to your home screen. The layout adapts to a small screen and the Today tab is comfortable on a phone, though typing a whole syllabus is faster on a laptop.

How are mock tests scored?

Enter correct and wrong answers per section. Score is correct minus wrong multiplied by your negative-marking value (0.25 by default, adjustable). Unfinished sessions from earlier days stay at the top of the Today tab; nothing is quietly deleted.
