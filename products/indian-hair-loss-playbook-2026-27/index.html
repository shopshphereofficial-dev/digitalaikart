What You Get

Six things almost no free Indian hair-loss article gives you

📊 The real trial numbers

Finasteride improved vertex photographs in 48 per cent of men at one year and 66 per cent at two, against 7 per cent on placebo. Microneedling added 91.4 hairs per sq cm against 22.2 for minoxidil alone. Every claim carries its number.

🇮🇳 Indian data, not American blogs

Telogen effluvium is 40.7 per cent of Indian hair-loss diagnoses; male pattern loss is 7.49 per cent. The loudest online advice is aimed at the smallest group. This book is aimed at the largest.

💰 2026 rupee prices

Minoxidil at ₹250 to ₹1,400 a bottle. PRP at ₹3,000 to ₹12,000 a session. Transplants at ₹40 to ₹150 per graft, with a graft-count table by Norwood stage and the extras clinics leave out of the quote.

⚖️ Evidence graded honestly

PRP gets moderate evidence for density and none for thickness. Laser therapy beats placebo but not minoxidil. Biotin is useless unless you are deficient — and it distorts your blood tests.

The ten phrases that should make you leave a clinic, the ASCI rulings that already struck down "stem cell" and "100 per cent success" claims, and Schedule J of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Is this a medical book? Does it replace a doctor?

No. It is an educational guide. Every dose, drug and procedure in it belongs in a conversation with a registered doctor who has examined your scalp. What the book does is make that conversation shorter, sharper and much cheaper — you arrive with a diagnosis question, a blood test list and a written cost plan instead of a brochure.

Will it work for women, or is it only about male balding?

It is written for women as much as men. In the HAILO database of 24,595 Indian patients with hair loss, about 74.8 per cent were women, and the most common diagnosis was telogen effluvium, not male pattern balding. There are dedicated sections on female pattern hair loss, PCOS, postpartum shedding, spironolactone and pregnancy rules.

Where do the numbers come from?

From primary sources: the American Academy of Dermatology's patient guidance, Indian multicentre and population studies, randomised trials and meta-analyses on minoxidil, finasteride, microneedling, PRP and laser therapy, the National Medical Commission's 2022 guidelines on hair transplant, Tamil Nadu's 2026 clinic regulation, ASCI rulings, and Schedule J of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules. The full source appendix is in the book so you can check every claim yourself.

Are the prices in the book current?

The rupee figures are 2026 Indian market ranges for medicines, consultations, PRP sessions and per-graft transplant pricing, gathered from publicly listed pharmacy prices and clinic price lists. They are labelled as ranges, not rate cards, because medicine prices vary by brand and city and procedure prices vary by surgeon and graft count.

I have already bought oils and serums that did not work. Will this help?

That is exactly who it is written for. One chapter is a decoder of the twenty beliefs that cost Indians the most money, and another explains what the law says about "cure baldness" claims. The first useful spend in every worked example in the book is a consultation — not a product.
