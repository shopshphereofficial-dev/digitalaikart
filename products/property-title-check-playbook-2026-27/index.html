Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Is this a substitute for a lawyer's title certificate?

No, and the book says so on its first page. It is a working manual that makes you competent enough to run the checks yourself and to ask a lawyer the three questions that actually matter. An advocate who has read the originals and signed an opinion is still the only person who can give you a title certificate.

Does it cover my state?

The law it explains — the RERA Act, the Registration Act, the Transfer of Property Act, the consumer commission route — is national. The portal chapter names the registration and land-record portals for Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Uttar Pradesh, Odisha, Bihar, Haryana, Punjab, West Bengal, Gujarat, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh, with the year each state's online data begins, so you know exactly which years still need a manual search at the sub-registrar's office.

What if I am buying a plot, not a flat?

Plots get their own chapters. Chapter 5 covers layout approvals, Chapter 19 covers agricultural land and conversion orders, and Chapter 27 takes apart the "panchayat approved" plot, which is the single most counterfeited product in Indian real estate.

Is the information current?

Every rule, section number, portal and judgment was checked in November 2026. The RERA text is the Act as amended, the Supreme Court judgments run to February 2026, and the land-records figures come from the Department of Land Resources releases of January and September 2026. The appendix lists every source with its date so you can re-check it.

What are the eight worksheets?

A twelve-document checklist, a 30-minute online verification log, an encumbrance-certificate entry decoder, a title chain chart, twenty questions for the seller, a nine-clause agreement checklist, a payment and retention tracker, and a red-flag score sheet with a go or no-go decision box. They are designed to be printed and filled in by hand.
