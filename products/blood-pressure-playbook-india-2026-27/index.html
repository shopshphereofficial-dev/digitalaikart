The Problem It Solves

Most people never find out their real number until something breaks.

🩺

One reading, and nobody measured again

An insurance test said 148/94 three years ago. You felt fine, so you forgot it. Chapter 2 explains why a single clinic reading is the weakest evidence there is, and Chapter 4 replaces it with a seven-day average from 24 readings.

🧂

You cut the salt spoon, nothing changed

Added salt is usually less than half of what you eat. The pickle, the papad, the namkeen, the bread and the restaurant gravy carry the rest. Chapter 6 is an audit you can run on your own kitchen in one week, with the label arithmetic that turns sodium milligrams into grams of salt.

💊

The tablets are not working

Four out of five treated adults are not at target, and it is rarely the molecule. It is an unmeasured home average, a painkiller for the knee, a salt intake of 10 grams, or a dose taken whenever you remember. Chapters 10, 11 and 12 deal with each one.

👨‍👩‍👧

A parent, or a spouse, who will not go to a doctor

You cannot argue someone into a clinic. You can hand them a device and a printed log. Chapter 4's protocol and the worksheet at the back turn a family argument into a number, and a number into an appointment.

What Makes It Different

Indian protocols, Indian prices, and a page your doctor will actually read

📏 The protocol, not a paraphrase

Two to three readings a minute apart, morning within an hour of waking and evening before dinner, three days minimum and seven preferred, day one discarded, the last two averaged. The rules that decide your number, in the order you do them.

🇮🇳 Built on Indian guidance

IGH-V 2025-26 from JAPI, the InSH consensus 2025, the Indian home monitoring guideline of March 2026, WHO 2021 and the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline — plus NP-NCD programme data up to July 2026.

🧂 The salt cut, in your kitchen

Nine hidden-salt items ranked with the practical cut for each, the sodium-to-salt conversion you can do on a packet in five seconds, and a four-week taper that replaces salt with lime, tamarind and amchur instead of with boredom.

₹ Real Indian prices

Validated monitors from ₹1,900, ABPM at ₹1,800–4,000, and the tablet maths that matters: telmisartan plus amlodipine at about ₹45 a month from a Jan Aushadhi Kendra against about ₹405 for the branded strip.

🚩 The red lines, with the symptoms

180/120 is not automatically an emergency and 150/95 is not automatically fine. Chapter 13 draws the line by symptom, adds the 30-second F.A.S.T. stroke test and a wallet card you can cut out.

🖨️ Nine printable worksheets

Questions Before You Buy

Straight answers, including the limits of a book like this

My clinic reading is 150/95 but I feel fine. Do I need this?

That is exactly the case this book is built for. High blood pressure has no reliable symptom, and 150/95 is grade 2 pressure that rarely settles without treatment. Chapter 2 explains why the clinic and home thresholds differ, Chapter 4 gives you the seven-day protocol, and Chapter 5 tells you how fast to act on the result.

Which guideline is this book based on?

The Indian Guidelines on Hypertension (IGH)-V 2025-26 published in the Journal of the Association of Physicians of India, the Indian Society of Hypertension consensus guideline 2025, the Indian home blood pressure monitoring guideline published in March 2026, the WHO 2021 guideline for the pharmacological treatment of hypertension, the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline and National Health Mission documents. Every price and threshold was checked in the first week of November 2026, and the source log is printed at the back.

Will it tell me which medicine to take?

No, and no honest book should. It explains the five drug families, why single-pill combinations are now standard, the two or three side effects that have easy fixes, how to take the tablets correctly, and what they cost at a Jan Aushadhi Kendra against branded strips. Every prescription decision stays with your doctor.

Do I need to buy a BP machine to use it?

You need a validated upper-arm monitor for the seven-day audit, and the book tells you which specification to look for and what Indian models cost in November 2026 — about ₹1,900 for a basic validated device, ₹4,500 and up for an app-connected one. If you only want to understand your numbers, the book still works without one.

Is this useful if I already take tablets every day?

That is the main audience. Roughly four out of five treated Indian adults are not at target, and the reasons are usually boring: the home average is never measured, salt stays at 8 to 11 grams, a painkiller is raising the pressure, or the tablet is missed. Chapters 4, 6, 10 and 11 are written for people already on treatment.

Is it safe if I am pregnant or have kidney disease?

Chapter 14 flags exactly where the standard advice changes for pregnancy, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, older adults and young-onset hypertension. In pregnancy and kidney disease several recommendations in this book must not be followed without your specialist's agreement, and the book says so at the point where it matters.
