Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

How much can a recruitment agent legally charge me for a Gulf job?

The Ministry of External Affairs caps a registered recruiting agent's service charge at ₹30,000 plus GST per placement. Passport fees, medicals, attestation, insurance and your air ticket sit outside that cap. Anything charged above it, without an itemised GST invoice naming the service, is a complaint you can file on the eMigrate portal or with the Protector of Emigrants.

Do I need emigration clearance for a UAE or Saudi job?

It depends on the stamp in your passport. If it shows ECR (Emigration Check Required), you need clearance from a Protector of Emigrants before flying to any notified ECR country for work — and all six GCC states are on that list. If your passport is ECNR, emigration clearance does not apply to you, though the contract and insurance checks are still worth doing.

How is gratuity calculated in the UAE?

Under Federal Decree-Law 33 of 2021, a full-time expatriate who completes one year of service is owed 21 days' basic wage for each of the first five years and 30 days' basic wage for each year after, capped at two years' wage and paid within 14 days of the contract ending. Only basic salary counts, not allowances — which is why the basic-allowance split in your contract decides your payout.

Is the book useful if I am already working abroad?

Yes. Chapters 18 to 20 cover the eleven scams, a step-by-step rescue sequence for unpaid wages, a withheld passport or an absconding report, and five ready-to-send complaint letters. Chapters 26 and 27 cover remittance, NRE and NRO accounts, the 182-day tax line and the return plan.

Is this legal advice for my case?

No. It is a research-based guide to the published rules, with the official page cited for every country so you can check the current version before you act. Gulf rules change by decree, sometimes overnight, so always verify fees, thresholds and fines at the source.
