Why This Book Exists

The numbers from November 2026, not from a blog written in 2021

India's pre-owned vehicle market is heading from about USD 36.4 billion in 2025 to over USD 73.5 billion by 2030, and roughly four out of five transactions still happen through unorganised channels with no vehicle history report. The Ease of Vehicle Ownership Index 2026, a 15-state study released in November 2026, surveyed people who had already completed a vehicle transaction. 39 per cent said their RC transfer was still pending. 61.5 per cent did not know a physical RTO appearance was required until they got there. 63 per cent could not estimate the total cost, and 63 per cent gave up and hired an agent. In Bihar, a single transfer was found to cost Rs 13,000 to Rs 14,000 over the fee shown on VAHAN. At Tamil Nadu's border RTOs, an Rs 800 fee becomes Rs 7,000 to Rs 8,000 on the ground.

That gap between the printed fee and the real cost is not a mystery. It is the cost of not knowing the sequence. This book is the sequence.

What Changed In 2026

Five facts that most used-vehicle advice still gets wrong

1. Thirty days, not fourteen

Section 157(2) of the Motor Vehicles Act was amended by the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2026, in force from 15 August 2026. The insurance transfer window is now thirty days. IRDAI's consumer handbook still warns about fourteen for own-damage claims, so fourteen is the deadline to work to.

2. Uninsured driving is priced differently

From 15 August 2026, three times the base premium or Rs 5,000, whichever is higher, for a first contravention, and five times or Rs 10,000 for a repeat. No jail term.

3. The loan charge can now vanish by itself

From 1 March 2026, MoRTH and the Reserve Bank Innovation Hub connected hypothecation termination to the Unified Lending Interface, starting with State Bank of India and five NBFCs. If the lender is not on the system, Form 35 is still the only route.

4. PUC validity depends on Bharat Stage

After one year from first registration, a PUC is mandatory: six months' validity, or twelve months for BS-IV and BS-VI vehicles, under Rule 115(7) of the CMVR. Getting this wrong means a fine of up to Rs 10,000 and suspension of the RC.

5. Delhi-NCR is its own regime

The Supreme Court's 17 December 2025 interim order allowed action against overage BS-III and older vehicles while protecting BS-IV and newer ones from action based on age alone. Once a vehicle is classified end-of-life, PUC and transfer services are blocked in the system.

And the market itself

In November 2026 the government confirmed it is examining simplification of used vehicle ownership transfer. The rules in this book are current, and the book tells you which part is law, which is state practice, and which is in motion.
