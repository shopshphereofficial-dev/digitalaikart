The Problem With a Monthly View

Profitable on paper, short of cash on Friday

A business can be profitable and still miss payroll. The two things that decide whether you make it through the month are timing and lags: when customers actually pay you, and when you actually pay everyone else. A monthly profit and loss statement averages all of that into one number and quietly hides the two weeks where the account is thin.

Most small businesses run the forecast in their head. The result is a decision made on a bad morning — a supplier paid late, a discount given away, an overdraft drawn at the worst possible rate. Thirteen weeks is the horizon that fixes this. It is one quarter: long enough to see a squeeze coming, short enough that the numbers are still close to true. You can plan for week 4 on the 1st of the month; you cannot do much about week 4 once week 4 has arrived.

CashFlow Compass exists to make that quarter visible in about ten minutes of setup, using the numbers you already know — your monthly sales, your monthly costs, and how long each of them takes to move through the bank.

How It Works

Setup in ten minutes, ten minutes every Monday after that

The maths, so you can trust it. Each monthly figure is divided by 4.33 to reach a weekly figure, then grown at your annual rate week by week. A revenue line with a three-week collection lag contributes nothing in weeks 1 to 3, and in week 4 collects what was invoiced in week 1, less the bad-debt percentage you set. Costs work the same way with the payment lag. GST is output tax on invoiced sales minus input credit on the cost lines you marked claimable, with the positive part paid after your filing lag. Headroom is closing cash plus undrawn OD limit minus your buffer — the amount you could absorb before you break your own floor.

The weekly ritual. Every Monday: type last week's real money in and out on the actuals tab, glance at the accuracy score, move the start date forward one Monday, and read the new signal line. Four weeks of that and the forecast starts being right, which is when it becomes worth something. The manual says this too, because a forecast you do not update is a decoration.

The order of moves when it says WATCH or ALERT. Chase the three largest overdue invoices. Offer a small early-payment discount on the biggest one. Shift a non-critical payment run by seven days — never statutory dues, never payroll. Draw the OD line before you need it rather than after. Delay growth spend last. The workbook names the week and the gap; the manual lists the levers; the decision stays yours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this an ebook or a working file?

A working file. The ZIP contains two macro-free .xlsx workbooks — one blank template and one filled-in example — plus a quick-start, a full manual and a licence. You open it in Excel or Google Sheets and type your own numbers.

Do I need to know Excel formulas?

No. You type into gold cells only: your opening cash, your buffer, your revenue and cost lines with their lags, and any one-off items. Every calculation, chart and colour code is already built. If you want to change a formula you can — nothing is locked or password-protected.

Why thirteen weeks and not twelve months?

Because a monthly view hides the bad week. Salaries leave on the 1st, your biggest customer pays on the 20th, GST goes out after month end — a month can close healthy while week three is empty. Thirteen weeks is one quarter: long enough to see a squeeze coming, short enough that the numbers are still roughly true.

How does it handle GST?

It computes output GST on your invoiced sales at the rate you enter, subtracts input credit on the cost lines you mark as claimable, and pays the positive net amount after the lag you set. Negative weeks are held at zero rather than carried forward, which keeps it simple. Your actual liability comes from your returns, not from this sheet.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the .xlsx to Drive, open it, then File > Save as Google Sheets. Formulas, number formats, dropdown validation and both charts carry over. After import, re-apply the custom rupee format on a few cells and re-point the chart titles if Google has renamed the ranges — the manual explains both in one line each.

What is the difference between the template and the example?

They are the same seven-tab workbook. The example is filled in for a fictional trading and service business called Sunrise Traders, so you can see a real forecast with a tight week before you type anything. The template is blank. Use the example to learn, the template to work.

The signal line names the week and the size of the gap, and the manual lists the order of moves that usually works: chase the three largest overdue invoices, offer an early-payment discount, shift a non-critical payment run by a week, draw the OD line before you need it.
