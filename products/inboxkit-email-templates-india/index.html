Why Your Emails Look Broken Somewhere

It is not your copy. It is the HTML.

An email is not a web page. Outlook for Windows renders email with Microsoft Word's layout engine, Gmail strips most of a <style> block, and Apple Mail on a phone will quietly re-scale any text under 13px. A layout that looks perfect in a browser preview can arrive as a single narrow column of text with the button turned into a blue underlined link — and you only find out because a customer says the invoice looked strange.

The fix is not a better designer. It is a boring one: tables for layout, styles inline on the elements, 600 pixels wide, one media query for phones, and buttons built from a table cell with padding rather than a styled anchor. That is what all twelve files in this pack do. They are also deliberately image-free — no logo image, no hero banner, no web font — because images are blocked by default in many inboxes and a web font simply does not load in Outlook. What you lose in decoration you get back in the email looking the same everywhere, loading instantly, and never showing an empty box where a banner should be.

And the copy is written for the Indian context rather than translated into it: rupee amounts in Indian digit grouping, UPI and IFSC details on the payment reminder, a CGST and SGST split on the invoice with a commented-out IGST row for inter-state supply, and a plain-English tone that does not pretend a reminder is a friendly hello.

How It Works

Open, replace, test, send — about five minutes for the first one

Pick and open. Start with preview.html, choose the email that matches the job, then open that file in a plain text editor — VS Code, Notepad++, Sublime, even Notepad. Not Word and not Google Docs: both rewrite the HTML and quietly break the layout.

Replace the highlighted values. Every example value from the fictional business carries a pale-yellow highlight. Search for class="ph" and work down the file. When you are done, remove the class — or leave it, and the highlight will remind you at a glance what is still a placeholder.

Map the merge fields. Anything in double curly braces is filled by your email platform. All 69 are listed in merge-fields.csv with an example. If your platform uses different syntax — Mailchimp wants *|FNAME|*, Zoho wants $[FNAME]$ — find-and-replace across the file, and the guide gives the exact syntax for six platforms.

Test before you send. Send it to yourself on Gmail and on an Outlook.com address, and check it at 390px in your browser's device toolbar. Click every link. Read the preheader. Check the amount and the date twice — in an invoice or a payment reminder, a wrong number is worse than a plain design.

Keep two pipes. Marketing templates go to your list; invoices, payment reminders and appointment reminders go out as transactional email, so an unsubscribe never blocks a bill. Section 2 of the guide maps all twelve.
