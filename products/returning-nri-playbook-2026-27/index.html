Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

I am returning to India in December 2026. Will I be a non-resident for that tax year?

Probably not. From 2 October 2026 your stay falls below the 182-day test in section 6(2) of the Income-tax Act 2025, but if you spent 365 days or more in India across FY 2022-23 to FY 2025-26, the 60 + 365 test catches you at 60 days. The playbook includes a day-count worksheet and a full arrival-date table for FY 2026-27.

Is the transfer-of-residence customs limit Rs 5 lakh or Rs 7.5 lakh?

Rs 7,50,000, if you have been abroad for two years or more immediately before arrival, your short visits to India in the preceding two years do not exceed six months in total, and you have not claimed the concession in the preceding three years. The Rs 5 lakh figure comes from the Baggage Rules 2016, which were superseded with effect from 2 February 2026.

Do I have to convert my NRE account immediately after returning?

The FEMA direction requires NRE balances to be redesignated or moved to an RFC account when you become resident, which happens on the day you land to settle. There is no statutory grace period. The playbook gives the exact order to do it in — RFC first, then the resident savings account — because the order determines whether you keep the currency and the interest exemption.

How much TDS is deducted when a non-resident sells property in India?

On the entire sale consideration, not on the gain: 12.5% for a long-term gain plus surcharge capped at 15% for LTCG and 4% cess, which is up to about 14.95% effective. A lower deduction certificate in Form 128, applied for before the first payment, brings the deduction down to your actual gain.

Is this useful if I have already returned and made a mistake?

Yes. Part 8 covers the five most common retro-fixes — an unconverted NRE account, a property sale without Form 128, a transfer-of-residence claim that falls short, a missing Form 67 and omitted foreign-asset disclosure — with what is available, what it costs and how urgent it is, plus an outline of FEMA compounding.

Is this legal or tax advice for my situation?

No. It is an information product that tells you what the rules say, which question to ask, and what the answer changes — and where the answer genuinely depends on your facts, it says so rather than inventing a number. Confirm your own position with a chartered accountant and, for property or FEMA matters, a practising lawyer.
