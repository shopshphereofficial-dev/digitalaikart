Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Do I need a lawyer to make a will in India?

No. For a simple estate you can write and sign a valid will yourself. Bring in an advocate for disputed property, business interests, agricultural land across states, a beneficiary who needs a trust, cross-border assets, or a family where a challenge is likely.

Is a will on plain paper valid?

Yes. A will needs no stamp paper, no notarisation and no registration. It needs to be in writing, signed by you, and attested by two witnesses, with you of sound mind and acting freely.

Is probate still required in India?

No. The Repealing and Amending Act, 2025 omitted Section 213 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, which had made probate compulsory in certain cases in the old presidency towns. Mandatory probate no longer exists anywhere in India. Probate remains available voluntarily if the estate is disputed, large or complex.

Does a registered will prove itself in court?

No. The Supreme Court confirmed in Pazhanathal v. Alamathal (August 2026) that a will is not valid merely because it is signed or registered. The person relying on it must prove due execution and attestation under Section 63(c). Registration is strong evidence, not proof.

Does the nominee on my bank or demat account become the owner?

Usually not. For bank accounts, demat accounts and mutual funds, the nominee is a custodian who collects the asset for the legal heirs. Life insurance is the main exception, where the nominee usually receives the payout as beneficial owner.

Does my daughter inherit equally?

Yes. A daughter is a Class I heir to her father's self-acquired property and, since the 2005 amendment, a coparcener by birth in ancestral property. Marriage does not remove her right.

Is there an inheritance tax in India?

No. Receiving property under a will or on intestacy does not create a tax bill. But when you later sell an inherited asset, your cost is the previous owner's cost and your holding period includes theirs, so keep the original purchase records.
