The Problem It Solves

A wedding is forty vendors, six functions, three opinions and one number everybody is quietly guessing at

📒

The budget lives in five places at once

A notebook for the caterer, a WhatsApp forward for the decorator, a diary for the venue, one Excel sheet someone's cousin started, and a number in everybody's head. Nobody can answer "how much have we spent" without an hour of phone calls.

🧾

The quote and the bill are never the same number

The caterer quotes ₹1,150 a plate, then adds GST. The decorator's quote excludes lighting. The photographer's travel is "extra". By the time you compare two vendors you are comparing two different sums, and the cheaper one is not always cheaper.

📅

Advances get paid twice, balances get forgotten

Forty thousand by UPI in April, fifty thousand by cheque in July, and nobody remembers which instalment that was. The vendor calls in November for a balance you thought was settled in September.

🍽️

The plate count is a guess made twice

250 invited, 180 "definitely coming", 60 maybe, and a caterer who needs a number nine days before the function. Then the Jain and non-veg counts get worked out on the back of an envelope, at the counter, on the day.

What It Does

Three numbers kept apart on purpose: planned, committed and paid

🧮 Planned, committed and paid are separate

Planned is what the Budget Plan sheet says you intend to spend. Committed is what a vendor has actually quoted and you have booked. Paid is what has left your bank. The Dashboard shows all three, plus what is still owed — so an "on budget" wedding that is short of cash this month is visible before it happens.

🎪 Budget by function, not just by category

Roka, engagement, haldi, mehendi, sangeet, wedding, reception — up to ten functions, each with its own date, venue, expected guests and plate rates. Every expense line and every vendor sits against a function, so the Dashboard shows what each function actually costs.

🧾 Quotes compared with GST and extras

Type the quote, the GST percentage and any other charges; the workbook shows the tax-inclusive quote and the true total cost beside it. Two vendors quoted differently finally become two comparable numbers, and the booked amount is what feeds every total.

💰 Every instalment with its due date

One row per advance or balance, with the mode and a reference. Mark Paid = Yes when the money goes out. Unpaid rows past their date turn red, rows due within fourteen days turn amber, and "paid so far" per vendor is calculated from that flag alone — not typed twice.

🍽️ Plates counted from your RSVP

One row per family with veg, Jain and non-veg headcounts. The Dashboard takes the smaller of the function's expected guests and the confirmed people, splits it in your confirmed guest mix, and prices it at that function's plate rates. You are not catering for 250 when 84 have confirmed.

🎁 Shagun register, both sides

Who gave what, on which function, cash or UPI, and whether the thank-you went out. Totals split by bride side and groom side, so the "who gave what" conversation has a number behind it instead of a memory.

✅ Checklist dated from the wedding

Forty tasks with "months before" — book the venue at nine months, print the cards at four, confirm the rooming list at one. The do-by date is calculated from the wedding date on Setup, so changing the date moves every task with it, and anything past its date and not done is flagged LATE.

🚨 Six health checks, not silent zeros

The Dashboard tells you when the budget is empty, when a vendor is booked in a category with nothing planned, when a function has guests but no plate rate, and when a booked vendor has no payment logged. A spreadsheet that says "check this" is worth more than one that says ₹0.

📊 One chart the family can read

Planned against committed, category by category, on the Dashboard. It prints with everything else on one A4 page for the meeting where the budget gets decided.

How to Use It

Six steps, about fifteen minutes to a working budget

  1. Open the template in Excel, or upload it to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets. There is nothing to install and nothing is locked.
  2. Fill Setup. Wedding date, city, total budget, what each side is contributing, then your functions with a per-plate rate for veg, Jain and non-veg. Fifteen expense categories come with a suggested split you can move.
  3. Build the Budget Plan. One row per expense line — function, category, item, planned amount. The header total and the Dashboard update as you type.
  4. Add your vendors. Quote, GST %, other charges, and the amount you finally booked. The status column keeps track of who is an enquiry, who has quoted and who is booked.
  5. Log the payments. Every advance and instalment with its due date and mode. Change Paid? to Yes when the money leaves — that single flag drives paid-so-far, balance due and the overdue list.
  6. Keep the guests, shagun and checklist current, then open the Dashboard. If the six health checks say OK, the workbook is wired correctly and you can stop maintaining it and start booking things.

Type in the pale gold cells; the blue-grey cells are formulas. Use the dropdown values for category, function, side and status — free text is allowed, but a near-match will not be added to the right total.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this an ebook?

No. You get two working spreadsheets — a blank one and a filled example — plus a guide, a quick start and a licence. Nothing to read before you can use it: open the workbook and start typing.

Do I need Excel, or does Google Sheets work?

Both work, and so does LibreOffice Calc. In Google Sheets, upload the file to Drive and use File → Save as Google Sheets. Every formula in the workbook exists in Sheets with the same name, so nothing needs rewriting. There are no macros, so nothing gets blocked either.

What exactly is the difference between planned, committed and paid?

Planned is your intention, from the Budget Plan sheet. Committed is what a vendor has quoted and you have booked, from the Vendors sheet. Paid is money that has actually left, from the Payments sheet where Paid = Yes. The Dashboard shows all three and the gaps between them, which is how you see an overspend before the bill arrives.

Does it handle GST on vendor quotes?

It multiplies the quote by whatever GST percentage you type, and adds any other charges, so you can compare two vendors on the same basis. It does not know the correct rate for a given service, and it does not calculate your GST liability or file anything — that is between you, the vendor and your CA.

How does the plate estimate work?

For each function it takes the expected guests from Setup and the confirmed people from the Guests sheet and uses the smaller of the two. It splits those plates in your confirmed veg / Jain / non-veg mix and prices them at that function's plate rates. It is a sanity check against a caterer's quotation, not a replacement for it.

Can my family edit it too?

Yes. In Google Sheets, share the file with them as Editors — the licence covers everyone working on the same wedding. On a phone, the Google Sheets app can edit any cell, so a payment status can be updated at the vendor's counter.

How many functions, vendors and guests can it hold?

Ten functions, 160 budget lines, 70 vendors, 160 payments, 220 guest families, 120 shagun entries and 80 checklist rows. That covers a large Indian wedding comfortably; if you genuinely need more rows, insert them above the total row and the formulas follow.

Does it calculate TDS or file my taxes?

No. It is a planning aid. TDS under sections 194C and 194J, the ₹2 lakh cash-receipt restriction under 269ST, and anything you need to file are yours to confirm with a chartered accountant. The guide explains where each of these shows up in a wedding budget.

Will it tell me what my wedding should cost?

No, and be careful of anything that claims to. Costs depend on your city, the season, the guest count and the menu. Every amount in the worked example is fictional and labelled as such. What the workbook does is hold the numbers you bring back from real quotations.

Can I use it for more than one wedding?

The licence covers one wedding, with unlimited edits and printing. If you are a planner running several weddings, buy one copy per wedding you run — that keeps the terms simple and the file honest.

What if a formula does not behave?

Page 4 of the guide lists the seven things that usually look like a bug — a category typed instead of picked, a vendor name spelled two ways, a date typed as text — with the fix for each. If it is genuinely broken, email within 7 days and a working copy or a fix comes back.
