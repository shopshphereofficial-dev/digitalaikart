What It Stops You Paying For

Part 6 audits the shelf, so you do not fund it

Black cohosh

Cochrane reviews have repeatedly found insufficient evidence over placebo, with rare liver concerns. The playbook says so plainly.

Compounded "bioidentical" hormones

The word has no regulatory meaning in India, and the preparations are not standardised for dose or purity. Approved estradiol and micronized progesterone are what the guidelines refer to.

Repeated hormone panels

FSH can read 12 this month and 48 the next. Testing it repeatedly in your forties buys anxiety, not information.

"Hormone balance" powders

Evening primrose, DHEA and most proprietary blends have no trial evidence for menopausal symptoms. The book tells you where the money actually works: calcium, vitamin D, protein and load.

Everything you need to know before buying

Is this a medical book or a self-help book?

It is a plain-language handbook built directly from published guidance — the Indian Menopause Society's 2026 clinical practice guidelines, the WHO menopause fact sheet, ICMR-NIN dietary guidelines and India's national disease-screening programme. It explains what the evidence says and what to ask for, and it says clearly that diagnosis and prescriptions belong to you and your doctor.

How is this different from the menopause content available free online?

Almost all free material is written for Western women, where hot flushes dominate and the average age at menopause is around 51. Indian studies find joint and muscle pain to be the most reported symptom, and the average Indian age at natural menopause is about 46. This playbook is built around the Indian symptom profile, Indian food, Indian costs and India's screening system.

Does it tell me whether to take hormone therapy?

No — that decision needs your history and examination. What it does is lay out the current position honestly: what the 2026 Indian guidelines recommend, what the 2002 Women's Health Initiative actually found, what changed at the US FDA in November 2025 and February 2026, the absolute risks, the regimens used in India, the contraindications, and an eight-question worksheet to complete before your consultation.

I am 41 and my periods are still regular. Is it too early to read this?

It is the right time. Symptoms often begin before cycles change, and the Indian average start of the transition is around 44.7 years. The earlier parts cover what to get measured in your forties, which is when the numbers that matter — blood pressure, HbA1c, lipids, vitamin D, bone density — are still easy to change.
