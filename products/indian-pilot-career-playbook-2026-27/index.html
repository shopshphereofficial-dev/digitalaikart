Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

I haven't decided yet — is this playbook for me?

That is exactly who Part One is written for. It covers what a licence is and is not, the true all-in cost of Rs 55 lakh to Rs 1 crore, the four routes compared side by side, and a twelve-question self-check designed to either strengthen your decision or save you the cost of it — before you pay a flying school anything.

Does it cover the newest DGCA rules?

Yes. Every rule, fee and deadline was verified against primary sources in October 2026, including DGCA's Flying Training Circular 02 of 2026 issued on 5 October 2026 on agreements between flying schools and trainees, the April 2026 flying school ranking, the RTR(A) takeover by DGCA from December 2025, and the electronic pilot licence.

I did not take Physics and Maths in Class 12. Is this still useful?

Yes, and section 6 covers your route in detail. DGCA currently requires Physics and Mathematics at 10+2 level, and the National Institute of Open Schooling is accepted by DGCA and by airline cadet programmes. The playbook also explains the status of the draft rules that would remove that requirement, and why you should not plan on them yet.

How is this better than the free information online?

Most free material is out of date, quotes figures without a source, or is written by a flying school selling a course. This playbook is built from DGCA Civil Aviation Requirements and circulars, Ministry of Civil Aviation and PIB releases, parliamentary replies, airline career pages and bank scheme documents — and it says plainly when a number is a market estimate rather than an official figure. Section 40 lists every source so you can re-verify anything.

Is this a replacement for a flying school's guidance?

No, and it is not meant to be. It is the independent brief you read before you sit in front of one — the questions to ask, the contract clauses to check, the red flags, and a due-diligence scorecard you can take to the visit. Treat it as the counterpart to a school's sales pitch, not a substitute for it.
