Why Flashcards Beat Another Tutorial

The gap is recall, not information

Every Excel problem has a written answer somewhere, and that is exactly the problem: the answer is in a tab you closed, not in your head. Watching a video builds recognition — you nod at INDEX/MATCH and feel like you know it. Then a real sheet arrives with a code in column F and prices in column G, and the formula will not come.

A flashcard forces the other thing. You see the task, you attempt the formula in your head or on paper, and only then do you check. That attempt is what builds the memory, and getting it wrong is not a failure — it is the moment the card earns its place in the next session. ExcelPower pairs every card with a note that names the trap: that SUMIFS takes the sum range first while SUMIF takes it last, that a VLOOKUP with TRUE needs a sorted column, that dividing by 1.18 is not the same as subtracting 18%, that RANK ties share a number.

The deck is deliberately narrow. Formulas only. No PivotTables, no Power Query, no charts, no macros — because a deck that tries to cover everything teaches nothing well.

Maths & Stats — 22 cards

A Look at Three Cards

Exactly as they appear in the deck and the app

DATA ANALYSIS · INTERMEDIATE

Total the sales in D where region is "North" AND status is "Paid".

=SUMIFS($D$2:$D$500,$B$2:$B$500,"North",$E$2:$E$500,"Paid")

SUMIFS puts the sum range first — the opposite of SUMIF. This trips up almost everyone.

DATE & TIME · INTERMEDIATE

Label the Indian financial year for the date in A2, e.g. FY 2026-27.

="FY "&IF(MONTH(A2)>=4,YEAR(A2),YEAR(A2)-1)&"-"&RIGHT(IF(MONTH(A2)>=4,YEAR(A2)+1,YEAR(A2)),2)

April to March. Change the 4 if your year starts in a different month.

MONEY & BUSINESS · INTERMEDIATE

Work out the taxable value when the invoice total in A2 already includes 18% GST.

=A2/1.18

Divide, do not subtract 18%. The tax is on the base, not on the gross.

These three cards ship exactly as shown. Nothing in the deck is a placeholder.

How It Works

Three minutes to start, ten minutes a day

Day one. Unzip the folder, double-click app/index.html, and press Drill. Read the task, say the formula out loud, press Space to check, then grade yourself honestly with 1, 2 or 3. That is the whole method. Ten minutes gets you through thirty to forty new cards.

Every day after. The app only shows what is due. A card you got right moves up a box and comes back in 1, 3, 7 or 14 days; a card you got wrong returns to box 1 and comes back in ten minutes. Nothing is scheduled by a calendar you have to keep — the queue rebuilds itself from what you have actually answered.

The practice layer. Open the workbook on the weekend and do five tasks. Typing =SUMIFS( into a real cell is a different skill from reciting it, and the answer key is there the moment you want it. The Sales sheet has 40 rows of fictional data, so nothing you do can damage real records.

The print layer. Print the deck at 100% scale, cut on the frames, and keep the eight or ten cards you keep forgetting on the desk. The card number in the corner tells you where it belongs when it drifts.
