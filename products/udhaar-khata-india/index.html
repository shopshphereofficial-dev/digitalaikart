The Problem It Solves

Most small businesses do not lose money on sales. They lose it on collections.

The notebook works until the notebook gets full. Then the udhaar page is somewhere in the middle, half the payments are written in the margin, and nobody can say which customer crossed 60 days last month. The result is predictable: money that is technically yours sits with someone else for months, and the follow-up happens only when cash gets tight — which is exactly when it is hardest to chase politely.

Udhaar Khata puts the same information in one place and makes the question answerable in one glance: who owes how much, since when, and what is the one message you should send today. It runs from a single HTML file on your own laptop or phone, so there is no monthly bill, no login, and no customer list leaving your device.

What You Can Do With It

A complete, working credit ledger — not a template

👥 Customer-wise khata

Add each customer once with a phone number, a note and any opening balance carried over from your notebook. Their running balance sits at the top of their ledger.

🧾 Four kinds of entry

Credit sale, payment received, and adjustment in either direction — so part payments, discounts and a forgotten old bill all land in the right place.

📅 Due-date ageing

Give every credit sale a due date and the app sorts the unpaid money into five buckets: not due, 1–15, 16–30, 31–60 and 60+ days. Payments clear the oldest dues first, the way an accountant would.

💬 One-click WhatsApp reminder

Opens WhatsApp with a polite, ready-to-send message: customer name, business name, exact amount, today's date and your UPI ID. Rewrite the wording once in Settings.

🖨️ Printable A4 statement

A clean account statement for any customer — every entry, credit and paid columns, closing balance and the amount written out in words. Save as PDF and send it on WhatsApp.

📊 Dashboard and reports

Total outstanding, how much is overdue, collections this month, ageing distribution across all customers, top ten dues and a six-month credit-vs-collection view.

⬇️ CSV for Excel or Sheets

Export a balances sheet or a full entries sheet — the one to hand your accountant at the end of the quarter. Opens directly in Excel and Google Sheets.

💾 Backup and restore

One click downloads a single JSON file with everything. Restore it on a new phone or laptop in seconds. Your data never touches a server.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Is this a book or a working app?

A working app, not a book. You get a ZIP with a single index.html file — double-click it and it opens in your browser as a complete credit ledger. There is nothing to install and no account to create.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once the folder is on your device, the ledger works with the internet switched off. The file loads nothing from the web.

Where is my data stored, and can I lose it?

In your browser's local storage on that device. Clearing browsing data, using a cleaner app or resetting the device will remove it, so the app has a one-click JSON backup and a matching restore. Download a backup every week and keep it in Google Drive — that is the whole safety net, and it takes ten seconds.

How is ageing calculated?

Every credit sale carries a due date (the entry date if you leave it blank). Payments are applied to the oldest dues first, the way most accountants do it. Whatever is still unpaid is then bucketed by how far past its due date it is: not due, 1–15, 16–30, 31–60 and 60+ days. The Reports screen totals every customer into the same five buckets.

Does it create GST invoices?

No. Udhaar Khata is a customer credit ledger and a statement printer. It does not create tax invoices, file GSTR-1 or GSTR-3B, or compute GST. If you need GST invoicing, use a proper invoice tool alongside it.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. Copy index.html to your phone, open it with Chrome, and use the browser menu to add it to your home screen. The layout adapts to a small screen, though entering many customers is faster on a laptop.

What if I have two shops?

One purchase covers one business. For two separate sets of books, buy a second copy — or keep one file per shop, each with its own backup, and never open the same file from two different folders.
