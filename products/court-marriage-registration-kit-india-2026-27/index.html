Six Questions This Answers in One Sitting

Every one of these is a search somebody makes at midnight, three weeks before a date they cannot move

Which law registers us?

Hindu Marriage Act, Special Marriage Act, the Indian Christian Marriage Act, a state Muslim registration statute, the Parsi Act, or the Uttarakhand UCC. One decision table, then the route behind each answer — including the section 16 route that registers an existing ceremony under the SMA.

Which documents, exactly?

The published lists for Delhi, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Kerala and Tamil Nadu, with the differences that get files returned — Karnataka treats the wedding invitation card as mandatory, Delhi wants separate affidavits from husband and wife, UP wants both notarised before upload.

How long will it take?

Registration of an existing marriage has no notice period; Delhi publishes 14 days for the certificate, Karnataka notifies one working day under Sakala, Maharashtra notifies one hour for an office solemnisation after a 30-day notice. Court marriage is 30 days plus solemnisation, and the notice lapses after three months.

What does it cost?

Verified figures: Delhi ₹100 (₹150 if the officer solemnises), Maharashtra ₹100 notice plus ₹150 and ₹25 for the certificate, Kerala ₹110 plus ₹1,103 plus ₹220, Tamil Nadu ₹100. Plus a realistic all-in budget that includes the notary, the stamp paper and the travel nobody counts.

What if somebody objects?

An objection must allege a section 4 disqualification — a living spouse, age, prohibited degrees, consent. "The family does not approve" is not a ground. The kit gives you the reply template, the inquiry and appeal route, and the sentence to say when you ask for the refusal in writing.

What happens after the certificate?

Six certified copies, DigiLocker where your state pushes a signed copy, the name-change sequence that avoids a gazette detour, passport and visa, and the 2026 apostille chain — including the February 2026 change in which agencies are authorised to handle it.

The Ten Templates

Working drafts, not statutory forms — the state's own form still governs where one exists

Fifteen lines, printed once per application, to tick at the door of the office instead of in the queue.

T2 · Joint affidavit

For registering a marriage already solemnised — deponents, ceremony, registration sought, marital status, age, prohibited degrees, consent, children.

T3 · SMA eligibility affidavit

One for each party, filed with the notice: no living spouse, not within prohibited degrees, age, sound mind, thirty-day residence, nationality.

T4 · Notice cover letter

A one-page index of the file that goes in with the Second Schedule notice — parties, residence period, documents enclosed, fee, date sought.

The most useful page in the kit: a written request under sections 5 to 7, with the Allahabad High Court's reasoning set out and space for the particulars of risk.

For collecting copies or filing documents when one of you cannot travel, with the limit stated plainly: it does not extend to attending for the marriage.

Questions

Can I skip the 30-day notice period?

Not by paying anyone. The Delhi High Court refused to relax the 30-day waiting period in June 2026, holding that a court cannot direct an authority to act against a statutory mandate. What you can do, in Uttar Pradesh, is file a written request that the notice not be published — the Allahabad High Court held in 2021 that publication and objections are directory and take effect only if the parties ask for them. The kit gives you that request as a template, with the reasoning already set out.

Is this only about court marriage?

No. Route A is registering a marriage that has already been solemnised — no notice period, no objections, nine steps. Route B is court marriage under the Special Marriage Act with its 30-day calendar. Most people who buy this need Route A, which is why it comes first.

Does it cover my state?

The central law chapters apply everywhere. Six states get a full published document list — Delhi, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Kerala and Tamil Nadu.
