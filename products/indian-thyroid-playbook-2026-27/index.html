Six Facts That Change What You Do This Week

All verified from Indian and international sources, current to November 2026

10.95% of Indian adults

The eight-city prevalence study found hypothyroidism in 10.95% of adults — 15.86% of women against 5.02% of men. Nearly one in three of those cases was undetected until the study tested them.

17% in pregnancy

A 2026 meta-analysis of 60 Indian studies pooled hypothyroidism in pregnancy at 17%. Among women with subclinical disease, 18% had a preterm birth and 17% a low-birth-weight baby.

The subclinical evidence is thinner than the prescription

A JAMA meta-analysis of 21 trials in 2,192 adults found no symptom or quality-of-life benefit from treating subclinical hypothyroidism. Indian guidance still asks for a repeat test in 8–12 weeks first. Both are in the book.

One in five is over-replaced

The Indian Thyroid Society puts over-treatment at about 20% of people on levothyroxine. That means palpitations, atrial fibrillation and bone loss — quietly, for years.

Four hours, not four minutes

Calcium, iron, antacids and multivitamins must be four hours away from your tablet. Most Indian households swallow them together after dinner, then wonder why the TSH will not settle.

Your pink salt is not iodised

India is iodine-sufficient — 94% of households use iodised salt and the 2026 ICMR schoolchildren study found adequate iodine. Rock and pink salts are outside that protection, and extra iodine supplements make autoimmunity worse.

No, and the book says so on page two. Who is this written for?

Four groups: people handed a report with a high TSH and told to "just start the tablet"; people who have been on levothyroxine for years and still feel unwell; women who are pregnant, planning a pregnancy or recently delivered; and anyone whose ultrasound mentions a thyroid nodule. Never on your own.
