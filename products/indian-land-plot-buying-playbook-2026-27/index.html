Why Land Needs Its Own Playbook

The fraud is usually an identity fraud, not a document fraud

In 2026 the reported cases followed the same shape. In Barabanki, a buyer paid Rs 45 lakh and discovered the person who signed at the tehsil office was an impostor. In Ahmedabad, a land trader allegedly lost Rs 4.70 crore to sellers using forged Aadhaar and PAN cards in the real owners' names — the fraud surfaced only when he applied for mutation and notices reached the recorded owners. In Hyderabad, the Central Crime Station reported 54 victims and about Rs 4.5 crore in overlapping agreements on a single property.

None of these buyers was careless. They relied on a document and a broker, and never independently confirmed three free facts: who the recorded owner is today, whether that owner is the person in front of them, and whether anyone else holds an interest in the same parcel. This playbook is that confirmation, written as a sequence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Is this legal advice?

No. It is a due-diligence playbook: general information on how Indian land records, registration, RERA and tax work, with every figure traced to an official source. Land is a State subject and rules change, so you should still have a local lawyer review your specific file. The book is written to make that review faster and cheaper — you arrive with the documents collected and the questions framed.

Which states does it cover?

All of them. The central law applies everywhere — the Transfer of Property Act, the Registration Act, RERA, the Benami Act and the Income-tax Act 2025. The book explains the state variations that matter: what your Record of Rights is called in ten states, where to find the guidance value and stamp duty in your state, and a fully worked cost example using the Telangana registration department's own published rates.

Is it updated for 2026?

Yes. This edition covers DILRMP 3.0 and Bhu-Aadhaar (ULPIN), the parliamentary committee findings of August 2026, auto-triggered mutation in 17 states, the 1% TDS now under section 393(1) of the Income-tax Act 2025 with Form 141, the divergent RERA readings on the 500 sq m / 8-plot threshold, and police-reported fraud patterns from February to November 2026.

Is it about plots or flats?

Plots and land. A flat comes with a builder, a project and a bank that has already done the homework. A plot does not, and almost every protection has to be built by the buyer. If you are buying a flat, the RERA and registration chapters still apply, but the title-chain and layout chapters are written for land.

I have never bought property before. Is it too technical?

No. Every term is explained the first time it appears, and there is a 60-term glossary at the back. The chapters are written as sequences of actions — what to open, what to look for, what to say — rather than as commentary on the law.

Can I print the worksheets?

Yes, and you should. Part 7 has eight printable sheets: the 30-point pre-payment checklist, a document request letter to the seller, the title chain worksheet, an encumbrance certificate reading sheet, a cost calculator, a site visit checklist, ten questions for your lawyer, and a seller conversation script.

I am an NRI buying land in India. Is that covered?

Chapter 21 covers the FEMA rules on what an NRI or OCI may and may not buy, the banking-channel requirement, the countries whose citizens need prior RBI permission, and the much heavier TDS that applies when your seller is a non-resident.
