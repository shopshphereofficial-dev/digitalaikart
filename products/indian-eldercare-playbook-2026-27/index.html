Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

My parents are still independent. Is this book too early for me?

That is the best time to read it. Chapter two takes two hours and builds the eldercare file: documents, a one-page medical sheet, a money map and a list of people to call. Chapter four checks whether your parent is already entitled to free hospital cover worth Rs 5 lakh a year, which most families never claim. Families who do this work while nothing is on fire spend far less later.

Does it cover the free Ayushman cover for people over 70?

Yes, in detail. All senior citizens aged 70 or above are eligible irrespective of income, with cover of Rs 5 lakh a year, about 6 crore people across roughly 4.5 crore families. The book explains that Aadhaar is the only document needed, how age is determined when Aadhaar records only a birth year, how a couple both over 70 gets separate cover, where to enrol, and exactly what the scheme does not pay for so you do not cancel a private policy on the assumption that it does.

My father transferred the house to my brother and the care stopped. Does the book help?

Chapter ten explains section 23 of the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007, under which a tribunal can declare a property transfer void where it was made on the condition that the transferee would provide maintenance and that maintenance has not been provided. It also explains the tribunal’s real limits: it sits at the SDM level, costs nothing, must decide within 90 days of notice, can order up to Rs 10,000 a month, and cannot divide property or settle a title dispute.

Are the pension and scheme names current?

Yes, and this is where most free material is out of date. On 14 October 2026 the Union Cabinet renamed the National Social Assistance Programme as the Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana, and the old age pension as the Pradhan Mantri Vridhavastha Pension Yojana, continuing for 2026-27 to 2030-31. The book gives both the old and new names, because forms and office staff still use both, along with reported state top-ups and the 2026 rates for the Senior Citizens’ Savings Scheme.

What does care actually cost, according to the book?

Chapter three sets out five rungs, from monitoring at no cost to a facility at Rs 25,000 to Rs 80,000 a month and above. Attendants are quoted at Rs 12,000 to Rs 25,000 a month live-in; trained nurses at Rs 25,000 to Rs 60,000. It then works three full household budgets, from Rs 17,000 a month for an independent 68-year-old to Rs 75,500 a month for an 84-year-old with high needs, and shows the funding order that wastes the least money.

Is this medical or legal advice?

No. It is a planning and administration manual. It explains what the schemes, statutes, regulator rules and clinical guidance say, and which questions to ask, but every diagnosis, prescription, contract and filing belongs to a qualified doctor, advocate or chartered accountant who knows your family. The book states this plainly in its opening pages and again in the appendix.

How current are the facts, and can I check them?

This edition is dated November 2026 and every figure is traced in the appendix to a primary source: NITI Aayog’s 2024 senior care paper, the National Health Authority’s PM-JAY senior citizen FAQs, IRDAI’s May 2024 master circular, the Department of Economic Affairs memorandum of 30 September 2026, the Income-tax Act 2025 renumbering, the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007, RBI’s September 2025 directions on deceased customers’ claims, PIB releases of September and October 2026, the LASI-derived national dementia estimates, and HelpAge India’s 2026 survey. Where a rate will move, the appendix names the document to check rather than a news summary of it.

Can I print the templates?

Yes, chapter fourteen is seven working documents designed to be printed and filled in by hand: a wallet-sized emergency card, a sibling care agreement, an attendant engagement letter, a monthly care review, a hospital admission checklist, a home safety audit and a quarterly money check. There is also a 72-hour crisis card near the front, written to be read by someone who is frightened and in a hurry.
