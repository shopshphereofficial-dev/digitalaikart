The Problem It Solves

Most sellers know their revenue. Very few know their stock.

You sell on two or three marketplaces, keep a WhatsApp group for orders and a notebook for stock. Then a sale event lands: something sells out on Friday and you find out on Monday, something else has sat in the corner for five months, and a SKU you were sure was profitable turns out to be paying 22% commission plus ₹85 shipping on a ₹349 price. None of this is a selling problem. It is a bookkeeping problem, and a notebook cannot calculate it.

StockSense does the four calculations a notebook cannot: what is left, what it is worth, what it earns after fees, and what to buy next — from one movement log you keep anyway.

What It Does

A working system, not a printable template

📦 Closing stock that calculates itself

Type your opening count once. After that, every purchase, sale, return and write-off is one line on the Movements sheet, and closing stock is opening + stock in − stock out. You never overwrite a stock number, so you always know why a figure changed.

💰 Margin after GST and commission

Selling price is split into net price and GST, then the marketplace fee percentage, shipping and packing, and your unit cost are deducted. You get margin in rupees and as a percentage of net price — the number that actually reaches your bank, not the flattering gross figure.

📅 Days of cover, per SKU

Units sold in the last 30 days divided by 30 gives your daily pace; closing stock divided by that pace gives how many days the shelf lasts. A moving 30-day window, so it never needs resetting.

🚦 Six stock statuses, colour-coded

OUT OF STOCK, REORDER NOW, LOW, DEAD, OVERSTOCK and OK — decided by your own reorder level, lead time and thresholds. The Products sheet reads like a traffic light instead of a wall of numbers.

🛒 A reorder list with quantities and rupees

The Reorder sheet suggests how many units to buy so stock covers your supplier's lead time plus safety days, then multiplies by unit cost. Filter Priority = ORDER NOW and that is your purchase order for the week.

🕸️ Dead stock, named

Anything with stock on hand and no sale for 90 days is flagged DEAD, and the Dashboard shows the rupees locked in it. That is the cash you can free this month — the single most useful number for a seller carrying too much stock.

📊 A dashboard that needs no setup

Thirteen live figures — stock value at cost, retail value, unrealised margin, SKUs to reorder, out of stock, units sold in 30 days, return rate, average margin, dead-stock value, cash needed for this week's reorder, and a negative-stock check that catches a data error — plus two charts, stock value by category and SKUs by status.

🔁 Return rate and write-offs tracked

Sales returns and damage are their own movement types, so the return rate on the Dashboard is real. On cash-on-delivery heavy channels this is the figure that quietly decides whether a 20% margin survives.

🧾 Dropdowns instead of typos

Category, channel, movement type, GST slab and SKU are all validated lists. A SKU that does not exist in Products cannot be logged in Movements, which is the single most common reason inventory sheets go wrong.

📖 A manual that documents the formulas

Every column, every status rule and every formula is written out in plain English and in full text in the guide — so you can extend the workbook, add rows or change the logic without hunting for help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Is this an app or a spreadsheet?

A spreadsheet — one Excel workbook with six tabs. It opens in Microsoft Excel 2016 or later, in Google Sheets and in LibreOffice Calc. There is nothing to install, no account to create and no browser extension.

Does it connect to Amazon, Flipkart or Meesho?

No. Nothing is fetched automatically and no seller account is connected. You enter or paste your own numbers. That is deliberate: no API keys, no permissions to grant, no monthly fee, and no chance of a price or stock change being pushed to a live listing by a spreadsheet.

How is the margin calculated?

Selling price ÷ (1 + GST rate) gives the net price — the part of the price that is yours before tax is remitted. From that the workbook subtracts the marketplace commission percentage you enter, your shipping and packing cost per order, and your unit cost. The result is margin in rupees, and as a percentage of the net price. If you would rather model it differently, every formula is documented and editable.

Do I have to type my stock level every day?

No — and you should not. You enter opening stock once, then log movements: stock in, sales, sales returns and write-offs. Closing stock is calculated as opening + stock in − stock out. A physical recount becomes a write-off line with the note "recount", so the history stays honest.

How does it decide when to reorder?

From your own numbers. Days of cover is closing stock divided by average daily sales over the last 30 days. Status becomes REORDER NOW when stock falls to your reorder level, LOW when cover falls inside lead time plus a seven-day buffer, and DEAD when there is stock but no sale for 90 days. The suggested quantity covers lead time plus 14 safety days at the current pace — both thresholds are editable on the Settings tab.

How many SKUs does it hold?

300 SKUs and 5,000 movement lines out of the box. Beyond that you copy the last formatted row down and the formulas follow; the manual also explains which Dashboard ranges to widen. Most sellers with 20 to 150 live SKUs never touch the limit.

Does it file GST or create tax invoices?

No. It uses the GST rate to show you the net price and the true margin, and it tracks stock and cost. Tax invoices, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B belong in your billing software or with your accountant.

What if I use Google Sheets, not Excel?

Upload the .xlsx to Google Drive, then open it with Google Sheets. The formulas, dropdowns and conditional formatting convert. Charts occasionally lose their styling on import — re-insert one from Insert → Chart and the numbers behind it are already there.

Can I use it for a shop as well as online?

Yes. Set the marketplace fee to 0 for counter sales, and the channel list already includes Offline / Shop. The reorder, cover and dead-stock logic does not care where the sale happened.
