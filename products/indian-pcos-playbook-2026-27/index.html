Is this a medical treatment guide?

No. This is an educational guide, not medical advice and not a diagnosis. It explains PCOS and what the evidence-based options are, so you can have a better conversation with your own doctor. It does not replace a consultation, and you should never start, stop or change any medicine based on it. Please see a qualified doctor for your own care.

Is the information specific to Indian women?

Yes. Prevalence and metabolic figures come from the ICMR national PCOS task-force study of nearly 10,000 Indian women, the diagnostic approach follows FOGSI's India recommendations, and the diet and meal template use everyday Indian foods. The management advice follows the 2023 International Evidence-Based PCOS Guideline that Indian clinicians use.

Are the facts and statistics sourced?

Every figure and claim is drawn from the World Health Organization, the ICMR-PCOS study (JAMA Network Open, 2024), FOGSI's 2024 recommendations, the 2023 International Evidence-Based PCOS Guideline, NICE, NICHD and the Government of India's Tele-MANAS programme. The full source list is inside the guide.

Does it include a diet plan?

Yes. It explains the evidence on diet in PCOS, gives you a simple four-moves way to build any Indian meal, lists smarter Indian swaps and label-reading tips, and includes a full 7-day Indian PCOS meal template plus a plan for eating out, festivals and weddings.

Does it cover fertility and pregnancy?

Yes. There is a dedicated section on PCOS and fertility — what helps and in what order, pre-conception checks, and why pregnancy with PCOS should be planned with your doctor. It is information to discuss with your specialist, not a substitute for one.

Is it useful for teenagers?

There is a dedicated section on PCOS in adolescents and families, which explains why diagnosis is deliberately cautious in teenagers and what parents should know. For any teenager's care, the guide is a starting point to discuss with a doctor, not a self-treatment plan.
