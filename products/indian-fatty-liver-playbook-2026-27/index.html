Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

I was told my ultrasound shows grade 1 fatty liver. Is this book for me?

Yes. Grade 1 is the most common report in India and the one people are given the least information about. Chapter 1 explains what the three grades actually measure and why the grade does not tell you whether there is scarring. Chapter 6 then shows you how to compute your own FIB-4 score in four minutes from a blood report you probably already have.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is educational material that explains what your reports mean, what Indian and international guidance says, and which questions to ask. Every decision about diagnosis, scans and medicines belongs to a qualified doctor who has examined you. The book says so plainly on its first page and again at the end.

Are the facts current, and where do they come from?

Every figure was checked against primary sources in November 2026, and the full source list is printed at the back of the book, with the specific study, guideline or notification behind each number. Sources include the ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, INASL guidance, the Ministry of Health's NP-NCD operational guidelines for fatty liver, the 2024 EASL-EASD-EASO clinical practice guidelines, AASLD guidance, the ESSENCE and MAESTRO-NASH trials, CDSCO approvals, CGHS notified test rates and IRDAI's 2024 insurance framework.

Does it cover medicines like saroglitazar and semaglutide?

Yes. Chapter 17 sets out the real regulatory position in India as of November 2026: saroglitazar, approved by the DCGI for NASH with F1 to F3 fibrosis in 2020 and available only in India; semaglutide 2.4 mg, approved by CDSCO for non-cirrhotic MASH with F2 to F3 fibrosis in July 2026; resmetirom, US FDA approved but not marketed in India; pioglitazone and vitamin E with their trade-offs. It also states clearly that none of these is approved for simple fatty liver without fibrosis, and that in every trial the placebo group also improved on diet and exercise.

Will it tell me what to eat? I am vegetarian and I eat rice.

That is exactly who it was written for. Chapter 11 rebuilds the thali using the ICMR-NIN 2024 numbers: cereals at no more than 45 per cent of energy, pulses at 14 to 15 per cent, 400 g of vegetables, oil at 25 to 30 g, added sugar under about 25 g, salt 5 g. Chapter 13 gives six breakfasts, ten lunches and dinners, a full seven-day menu, snack options and rules for eating out, including biryani, dosa and a festival day. Rice is not banned; quantity, frequency and what it is eaten with are what change.
