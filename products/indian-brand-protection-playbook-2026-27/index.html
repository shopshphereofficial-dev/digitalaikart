Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Do I need a trademark to sell in India?

Legally, no. Commercially, you need one before you build a brand you would mind losing. Without a registered mark you cannot sue for infringement, cannot enrol in Flipkart's Brand Assure, and cannot use Amazon Transparency. Amazon Brand Registry does accept a pending application — that is the fastest partial protection available, and Part 13 shows how to use it.

Is a company name or GST registration the same as a trademark?

No. A company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs only stops another company registering the identical name at MCA level — it does nothing to stop a different business using a similar brand on products or a marketplace. GST registration is a tax registration. Only a registered trademark gives exclusive rights in a name for specific goods or services.

What is the government fee for a trademark in India?

On Form TM-A filed online, ₹4,500 per class for individuals, DPIIT-recognised startups and micro, small or medium enterprises with a Udyam certificate, and ₹9,000 per class for everyone else. The fee is per class and per mark, and it is non-refundable. Renewal every ten years is ₹9,000 per class.

How long does registration take?

The registry does not promise a fixed total duration. The stages are examination, publication in the Trade Marks Journal, a four-month opposition window, and registration if no opposition is filed. Expedited examination under Rule 34 is ordinarily completed within three months of the request, at ₹20,000 per class for individuals, startups and small enterprises, or ₹40,000 for others.

Does it cover copyright and design registration too?

Yes. Part 10 covers copyright — the Form XIV filing, the ₹2,000 rate for an artistic work used with goods and services, the ₹9,000 TM-C search certificate that logo filings require, and the 30-day objection window. Part 11 covers design registration for shapes and packaging at ₹1,000 on Form 1 for individuals, startups and small entities.

I have already launched. Is it still useful?

Yes, and more urgently. Part 3 is the search you should have run, Part 5 is the filing you can still make, Part 7 is the examination report you will receive, and Part 14 is what to do when someone is already copying you. The templates in Part 18 include a retroactive IP assignment for work already delivered.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is a researched, source-verified working manual, not legal advice, and it does not create a lawyer-client relationship. For an opposition, a live dispute, a high-value brand or anything already in litigation, instruct an advocate. The playbook says so plainly in its own front matter.

Are the fees and rules current?

Every figure was checked against primary sources in October 2026 — the First Schedule to the Trade Marks Rules, 2017, the Copyright Rules, 2013, the Designs Rules as amended in 2021, WIPO's Madrid schedule, and the live Amazon and Flipkart programme pages. Fees change by notification, so the playbook tells you where to confirm before filing.
