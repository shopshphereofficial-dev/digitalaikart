Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Is this useful if I only have one spare room?

Yes. Chapter 4 models a single spare room at 40 per cent occupancy: 146 guest-nights, about Rs 2.6 lakh of bookings and roughly Rs 88,000 net before tax, for about eight hours of work a week. The licensing chapters apply from your first paying guest.

Which states does it cover?

It explains the national Ministry of Tourism classification standards, which apply where a state has no scheme of its own, and gives the documented room caps and incentives for Uttarakhand, Goa, Kerala and Uttar Pradesh from NITI Aayog's 2025 policy brief. Chapter 45 gives the ten questions to ask your own state tourism office, so you get your state's current answer rather than a stale table.

Is the law in it current?

It reflects the position published as of November 2026: the Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025 with Form III in 24 hours and a Rs 50,000 compounding penalty, the Income-tax Act 2025 with the presumptive scheme now in Section 58, GST at 5 per cent without credit for rooms up to Rs 7,500 a night since 22 September 2025, and FSSAI's turnover thresholds revised from 1 April 2026.

I already host on Airbnb. What will I learn?

The parts most hosts skip: whether your registration matches your room count, how the per-unit GST test actually works, the Form III duty that applies to homestays and serviced apartments including OCI cardholders, and a monthly model that shows what you really earn after the 15.5 per cent fee, laundry and breakfast.

Is this legal or tax advice for my case?

No. It is a research-backed guide with every legal figure traced to a named government or platform source, so you can check it before you file. Registration goes through your state tourism department and tax positions through a chartered accountant.
