Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Is this for a chartered accountant or for a business owner?

Both, and it is written so neither has to translate for the other. A proprietor or partner can follow Parts 1 to 3 and the 48-day plan with no GST background. An accountant gets the table-by-table walkthrough, the FY 2025-26 relaxations, the IMS mechanics and the GSTR-9C bridges. Where a row depends on the portal's own label, the kit says so rather than guessing.

Do I even need to file GSTR-9 this year?

If aggregate turnover for FY 2025-26 is up to ₹2 crore, the annual return is exempt under Notification 15/2025-Central Tax dated 17 September 2025 — and you may still file voluntarily. Above ₹2 crore it is mandatory for every GSTIN; above ₹5 crore you also file the self-certified GSTR-9C. Part 2 walks the test properly, because aggregate turnover is computed at PAN level across all your registrations, not from one State's books.

What is the 30 November 2026 deadline in the title?

Five provisions share one cut-off: 30 November following the end of the financial year, or the date the annual return is filed, whichever is earlier. That covers input tax credit under section 16(4), credit notes under 34(2), GSTR-1 amendments under 37(3), corrections under 39(9) and Rule 37A reversals. For FY 2025-26 the date is 30 November 2026 — and filing GSTR-9 before it closes the credit window early. Part 1 and Part 9 both work through the sequencing.

Is the information current?

Every position was checked on 13 November 2026: the 31 December 2026 due date under Rule 80, Notification 15/2025 for the ₹2 crore exemption, Notification 07/2023 for the turnover-slab late fee, CBIC Circular 246/03/2025 on GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C together, Notification 16/2025 for the IMS-based section 38 framework, and the 56th GST Council's 5% / 18% / 40% structure effective 22 September 2025. Where a portal parameter is not published — such as the DRC-01B and DRC-01C thresholds — the kit says so instead of quoting a number as law.

Does it cover the 22 September 2025 GST rate change?

Yes, in Part 7. FY 2025-26 is the first financial year with two rate regimes, and the kit shows the five places that lands in the annual return: splitting Table 17 by HSN and by rate, credit notes issued after the change, invoice-date discipline for September 2025, corrected inverted-duty structures, and the services that moved to 5%. It also tabulates where each rate now sits, including the 12% slab retained only for bricks and the 40% demerit rate.
