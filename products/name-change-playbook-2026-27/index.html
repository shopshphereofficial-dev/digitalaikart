Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before buying

Does this cover the new UIDAI name change rules?

Yes, and that is why the book exists now. UIDAI issued a revised Standard Operating Procedure for name updates on 18 September 2026, superseding the 2021 process. Part 2 puts all eight categories in one table — minor change, data-entry error, marriage or divorce, gender change, prefix removal, alias removal and major change — with the exact annexure (B, C, D, E or F) or Gazette notification each one needs. It also covers the two corrections that now need no document at all, and the rule that the name is updated to match the document you submit, not what you write on the form.

Do I need a Gazette notification to take my husband's surname?

No. A marriage certificate is enough for Aadhaar, PAN, the passport and the bank. The Gazette is for a genuinely new name or a full change of identity, and Part 1 explains which of the four creating documents applies to your case. Most people who believe they need the Gazette do not, and the ones who do need it usually try to skip it — both mistakes are expensive.

Is the information current, or recycled from older guides?

Every rule, form and fee was checked on 11 November 2026 against the issuing department's own source: the UIDAI circular of 18 September 2026 and its October 2026 acceptable-documents list, the UIDAI fee schedule, Protean's PAN change-request guidelines and Form PAN CR-01, the Passport (Amendment) Rules of 2016 and the Passport Seva document tables, ECI Form 8, Parivahan Sarathi, the EPFO joint declaration document rules, CBSE Examination Bye-laws Rule 69.1, and the Department of Publication's rate order of 19 September 2023. Where a state or portal sets its own charge, the book says so instead of inventing a number.

I only have a spelling mistake. Is a 38-page book overkill?

Part 3 finds your case in two minutes, Part 5 is the Aadhaar fix and Part 6 is PAN. If you read only those three, you still get the order right — and the order is what stops you paying twice. The rest of the book exists for the records nobody thinks about until a claim is rejected: the bank's CKYC record, the folio with the old name, the EPF profile created by an employer a decade ago.

Will it help if a board or university has refused my correction?

Part 11 is the honest chapter. It sets out what CBSE Rule 69.1 actually says: a spelling or typographical correction within a year of the result being declared, and a change of name or surname only where the change was admitted by a court and notified in the Gazette before the result was published. It then explains the route that does work for an adult name change — a civil suit for a declaration — and why an agent promising the Gazette shortcut cannot deliver it.

What exactly are the nine templates?

An affidavit for a surname change after marriage, one for a spelling or data-entry error, one for a full change of name as an adult, the Department of Publication undertaking, the Gazette proforma, the newspaper advertisement, the covering letter to the Department, a bank name-change request, and an EPF joint declaration covering letter. Each carries a note telling you when to use the authority's own prescribed form instead.
