Why Food Cost % Lies to You

A dish at 32% food cost can still leave you 6%

The usual test of a menu is food cost percentage: ingredient cost divided by selling price. Keep it under a third and the dish is "fine". That test was written for a dine-in restaurant where the customer pays at the counter and walks home. It falls apart the moment an order arrives through an app, because the platform is paid out of the same bill before you are.

Neither Swiggy nor Zomato publishes a commission rate card. The only range either platform has put on the record is Swiggy's 10–24% to the Competition Commission of India, and Zomato has said publicly that its all-in take is capped at 30%. Commission is charged on the gross order value — which includes the GST and the packaging charge — and 18% GST is then charged on the commission itself. At the 5% no-ITC rate that a standalone café or cloud kitchen must use, none of that is recoverable. Add a payment gateway fee of about 1.84% and the platform is taking roughly a quarter to a third of the bill before a single ingredient is bought.

So the only number worth managing is contribution: what lands in your account after the platform, the tax on the platform, the gateway and the packaging, minus what the food cost. That is what pays rent, salaries and you. MenuMargin OS computes it per dish, in the same row, and then tells you which dishes are carrying the menu and which are quietly costing you money.

Menu Engineering, in One Picture

Popularity against contribution — the four classes and what each one means

Star

Sold often and earns well. Protect the recipe, protect the price, never cut it in a discount campaign.

Plowhorse

Sold often, thin margin. Raise the price 5–8%, trim one costly ingredient, or renegotiate the packaging.

Puzzle

Good margin, few orders. Reposition it on the menu, rename it, photograph it better, push it in the first three slots.

Dog

Neither. Rebuild the recipe cheaper or take it off the menu — every dog on the list dilutes the kitchen's speed.

A dish counts as popular when its share of orders is at least 70% of an even share — with four dishes that is 17.5% — and profitable when its contribution per order beats the menu average. Both thresholds are the industry-standard version of the test, computed live from your own sales, not from a benchmark someone else published.

How It Works

Fifteen minutes to set up, five minutes whenever a price changes

The set-up order. Settings first — your commission, GST, gateway and packaging. Then Ingredients, one row per pack you buy, with the pack size in grams or millilitres and the wastage percentage. Then Recipe Lines, one row per ingredient per dish. Then Dishes: portions per batch, packaging, menu price. Then Menu Engineering with last month's units sold, and Break-Even with your fixed costs. The guide walks each step with the example numbers still in place.

What is calculated. Cost per usable gram carries the wastage. Batch cost is a SUMIF over the recipe lines for that dish code. Cost per portion divides by portions per batch. Gross order value is menu price plus GST plus packaging. Commission, GST on commission and gateway are deducted to give what you receive; food cost and packaging are then deducted to give contribution. Food cost % is measured against the menu price excluding GST, as is contribution %.

The five-minute job afterwards. Butter moves ₹50 a pack, or your platform renegotiates to 23%. Change one cell and every dish that touches it re-prices, the verdicts update, the menu ranking shifts and the break-even point moves. That is an afternoon in a spreadsheet built from scratch, and five seconds here.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this an ebook or a working file?

A working file. The ZIP contains one macro-free .xlsx workbook with seven sheets, a printable start-here guide, a readme and a licence. You open it in Excel, LibreOffice, WPS Office or Google Sheets and type your own pack prices and menu prices.

Do I need to know Excel formulas?

No. You type into the cream cells only: settings, ingredient pack prices, recipe quantities, menu prices, units sold and fixed costs. Every cost per gram, contribution, food cost percentage, dish class and break-even figure is already calculated.

How does it handle Swiggy and Zomato commission?

Commission is a percentage you enter per dish, from your own contract. It is charged on the gross order value — menu price plus GST plus packaging — with 18% GST on that commission and the payment gateway fee deducted. The workbook then shows what you actually receive and what contribution is left after food and packaging cost. It does not guess a rate for you, because neither platform publishes one.

Which GST rate should I enter?

5% for a standalone restaurant, café, bakery, takeaway or cloud kitchen under SAC 9963, without input tax credit. The 18%-with-ITC option applies only to a restaurant inside a specified premises — a hotel where a room was supplied above ₹7,500 a night in the previous year. A standalone outlet cannot declare itself one.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the .xlsx to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets, or use File > Save as Google Sheets. Every function used — SUMIF, AVERAGEIF, COUNTIF, VLOOKUP, IFERROR — behaves the same. No macros and no add-ins, so nothing is stripped on import.

How many dishes can I cost?

Fifty dishes, 200 ingredients and 400 recipe lines out of the box. The guide explains how to extend the lookup ranges if your menu is bigger.

I deleted the example rows and a dish shows food cost ₹0. What broke?

Nothing. That dish's code on the Recipe Lines sheet does not match the code on the Dishes sheet, or it has no recipe lines yet. A trailing space is the usual culprit. "Not found" in the ingredient column means that ingredient code is not on the Ingredients sheet.

Does it handle dine-in and delivery pricing separately?

Yes. Commission and GST are per-dish columns that default to your Settings values, so you can set commission to 0% on dine-in rows and your contract rate on delivery rows, and compare the same dish both ways in one file.
