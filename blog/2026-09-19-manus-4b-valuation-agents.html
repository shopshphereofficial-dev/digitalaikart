Manus AI Seeks a $4 Billion Valuation — Its First Big Round Since Beijing Broke Up Its Meta Deal

#ManusAI#AIBusiness#AIAgents#StartupValuation

TL;DR

  • Chinese AI agent startup Manus is in talks to raise $500 million at a $4 billion valuation, per a Wall Street Journal report
  • The round would be its first since Beijing blocked its $2 billion acquisition by Meta on regulatory grounds
  • Tencent may become its largest shareholder, alongside IDG Capital, Boyu Capital and CATL
  • A Hong Kong IPO is on the table — and the agent economy it plays in keeps getting more valuable

If you enjoy startup drama, the Manus AI story has had everything: a viral agent demo, a $2 billion Meta acquisition, regulators killing the deal, founders effectively grounded, and now — a comeback round. According to a Wall Street Journal report picked up by TechCrunch on September 18, the Chinese AI agent startup is in discussions to raise $500 million at a $4 billion valuation as it resumes life as an independent company.

01.Quick recap: how we got here

Manus went viral last year off a demo of its AI agent — software that doesn't just answer questions but plans and executes multi-step work inside its own virtual computer. It relocated staff to Singapore in mid-2025 and by December had agreed to a $2 billion acquisition by Meta, reportedly while pulling in annual recurring revenue north of $100 million.

Then came the twist. Beijing blocked the deal, citing potential violations of export controls and foreign investment rules — China was visibly nervous about losing AI talent and research to the West. Manus has spent this year untangling itself from Meta, with early backers reportedly helping it buy back its shares at roughly the $2 billion valuation. In August it told users to export and back up their data because it had to delete everything generated after the Meta acquisition to comply with regulations. This month, it confirmed it has resumed independent operations with the founding team still in charge.

02.The new $500 million round

The fresh financing talks, first reported by the Wall Street Journal citing anonymous sources, would value Manus at $4 billion — double what Meta agreed to pay. Potential investors include IDG Capital, Boyu Capital, battery giant Contemporary Amperex Technology, and existing backers Tencent, HSG and Zhenfund. Nothing is signed yet, and terms could still shift, but the direction is clear: Chinese capital is rallying around the country's most-watched agent startup.

Manus is also reportedly weighing a restructuring to prepare for an IPO in Hong Kong — a comeback path that would have seemed far-fetched six months ago when the company was stuck between a blocked deal and a forced separation.

03.Why the price doubled

Here's the thing nobody's talking about: the valuation isn't really about Manus' revenue (impressive as $100 million-plus ARR is). It's about scarcity and independence. Every major player — Meta included, as we covered when Meta went all-in on AI agents and subscriptions — is racing to own the agent layer. A proven, agent-native team that is now permanently out of reach of American buyers is worth a geopolitical premium to Chinese investors.

For context on how hot this category is: US-based Sierra hit a $15.8 billion valuation in May, and Cognition crossed $10 billion last year. Manus at $4 billion would make it China's most valuable autonomous agent startup, and reportedly the largest shareholder position would go to Tencent — giving the WeChat giant a flagship asset in the agent race.

04.What this means for the agent economy

The bigger picture for those of us watching from India: agents are becoming the platform businesses of this cycle, the way food-delivery apps were in 2015. Manus' products — a chatbot, vibe-coding tools for building apps and websites, a browser assistant — overlap with what OpenAI, Lovable and Replit offer, and the technology underneath keeps getting cheaper and faster, as we saw recently when TypeSafe's Jev model made agent-scale decisions drastically cheaper. What's new in the Manus saga is the reminder that in AI, regulation is now as decisive as technology. A deal can be perfect on paper and still die at the border.

Key Takeaways

  • Manus is in talks to raise $500 million at a $4 billion valuation — double its blocked $2 billion Meta deal price
  • Beijing killed the Meta acquisition over export control and foreign investment concerns; Manus bought back its shares at ~$2 billion
  • Tencent, IDG Capital, Boyu Capital and CATL are reportedly circling the new round
