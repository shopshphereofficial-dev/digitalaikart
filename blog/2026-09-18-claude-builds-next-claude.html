Claude Is Now Helping Build the Next Claude — Anthropic Explains

#Claude#Anthropic#AIAgents#RecursiveAI

TL;DR

  • Anthropic says Claude now leads 26% of its model research and development, up from zero in February
  • About 90% of the company's R&D happens "in collaboration" with Claude, with humans supervising throughout
  • Around 30,000 AI agents were doing research and engineering work at Anthropic as of August
  • Anthropic published these numbers to measure how close the industry is to recursive self-improvement — AI autonomously building its successor

Anthropic made an announcement on Thursday that would have sounded absurd a year ago: Claude, the company's own chatbot, is now helping develop the next, more intelligent version of itself. And unlike the usual startup hype, this one came with actual numbers, an explanation of what "helping" means, and a warning about where it all leads. Claude is leading 26% of Anthropic's model research and development — and the company wants the whole industry to report the same metric publicly.

01.What the numbers actually say

Two statistics carry the story. First: Claude leads 26% of Anthropic's model research and development, which the company says means the model can complete most of a given task "end-to-end from a high-level prompt" while remaining under human supervision. Second: about 90% of the company's R&D is done "in collaboration" with Claude — the model handles "large chunks of work under close human direction". The distinction matters. Leading a task is not the same as doing everything, and Anthropic is careful to state the model is not yet working completely autonomously.

The pace, though, is what made me sit up. The portion of work Claude leads was zero in February. Six months later, in August, it hit a quarter of all research and development. That is a steep curve by any standard, and Anthropic itself flagged where it points.

02.30,000 agents inside one AI lab

Anthropic also disclosed that approximately 30,000 AI agents were doing research and engineering work at the company as of August. These aren't chatbots answering questions — they're automated workers running experiments, writing code and completing engineering tasks, with oversight systems tracking how often agent misbehaviour gets caught by monitoring. The company says these oversight measures are crucial, and it has committed to setting up external third-party evaluators embedded within the company to monitor safety efforts.

If you've been following this space, you know the timing is not accidental. An Anthropic researcher resigned last week with a dire public warning about the technology's threats. CEO Dario Amodei has been leading the call for AI development to actually slow down. Publishing these metrics is the company putting numbers behind the debate.

03.Recursive self-improvement — the technical term you should know

The phrase that matters here is recursive self-improvement: a model's ability to autonomously build its successor. Anthropic was blunt in its blog post — "Models accelerating their own development could make it more challenging for humans to understand or control these systems." Their argument for publishing the numbers is that sharing these metrics helps the world understand how close leading labs are to that threshold.

The company also urged other AI developers to report similar metrics regularly, using a public methodology so the numbers can be compared over time — and potentially across labs. Whether OpenAI and Google take up that invitation is now the interesting question.

Honestly, what surprised me most wasn't the 26% figure itself. It's that Anthropic chose to volunteer it. Companies usually publish numbers that flatter them; this one invites scrutiny of exactly the thing safety researchers are most worried about.

04.Why this lands in the middle of the slowdown debate

This announcement comes at a strange moment for the industry. Amodei, Sam Altman, Elon Musk and other tech leaders have voiced support for slowing development, while President Trump and other figures have pushed back, arguing a US slowdown hands the advantage to China. Meanwhile, Anthropic is expected to beat OpenAI to public markets with a blockbuster IPO later this year — a company calling for caution while preparing one of the biggest listings in tech history.

For Indian developers and AI-curious professionals, the practical read is simpler: the tools you use every day are increasingly built by the tools themselves. The gap between "user" and "builder" is shrinking, and the people who benefit most will be those who learn to direct AI agents well rather than just chat with them.
