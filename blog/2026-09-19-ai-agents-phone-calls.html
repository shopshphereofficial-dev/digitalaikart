AI Agents Can Now Make Phone Calls: Instinct vs Muse

#AIAgents#VoiceAI#AICalling#MetaMuse

TL;DR

  • Instinct launched "Concierge" — an AI agent that places real phone calls to book tables, join waitlists and argue your cable bill
  • Meta's Muse simultaneously gained the ability to call US businesses
  • Instinct is reportedly in talks to raise $1 billion at a $10 billion valuation
  • Calling is the new frontier: agents that act in the real world, not just chat in a text box

For the last two years we've been typing to AI assistants and asking them nicely to do things. This week, AI agents that make real phone calls went from demo videos to actual products — and not from one company, but two at almost the same time. San Francisco startup Instinct announced Concierge, while Meta quietly switched on outbound calling in its Muse assistant. If you've been waiting for the moment AI agents stop living inside a chat window, this is it.

01.Instinct Concierge: your agent picks up the phone

Instinct's founder Noah Shinn announced the feature on X on September 16. Concierge is described as a "white glove" service for the high-touch cases that apps can't handle — the restaurant that doesn't take online reservations, the dentist whose cancellation list you can never get on, the cable bill that's been quietly inflated for three months. Your Instinct agent places the actual call and sorts it out.

It's rolling out in early access to a subset of users first, with a full rollout planned. The company also gave agents their own email addresses last week, so they can handle account sign-ups on your behalf.

02.Meta's Muse is dialing too

Meta isn't watching from the sidelines. Muse, its personal AI agent that launched on phones, the web and WhatsApp earlier this month, can now place calls to US businesses. There's a neat growth hack in the rollout: the feature reaches users who ask Muse to make a call first. Meta says whenever Muse launches new capabilities, people who showed interest get them earliest. Phone calling was one of the top requests.

Both moves brought Instinct and Muse to parity with other assistants that had been advertising calling as their differentiator — which tells you how quickly "my AI can call" went from selling point to table stakes.

03.Why calling is harder than chatting

Here's the thing nobody's talking about enough: making a phone call is a much harder problem than answering a chat. A call involves a real human on the other end — background noise, Hinglish, hold music, an irritated restaurant manager who has thirty seconds for you. The agent has to speak, listen, adapt, and actually close the task. It's the difference between an AI that answers questions and one that negotiates with the world.

That's also why this matters more than another benchmark score. Bookings, waitlists and billing disputes are exactly the errands most of us postpone for weeks. An agent that handles them is selling time, not intelligence.

04.Follow the money

Investors are convinced. Instinct raised $350 million at a $2.5 billion valuation last month and is reportedly in talks to raise $1 billion at a $10 billion valuation — eye-watering numbers for a company whose product is, at heart, a text box that does things. Muse, meanwhile, crossed 730,000 US downloads in its first few days per Sensor Tower data, edging out the 707,000 that Meta's standalone AI app managed in a comparable launch window. There's a crowded field forming — Wajo, Town, Ollie — all betting that the next interface isn't an app, it's an agent.

We saw a version of this land in India recently — Amazon's Alexa+ arrived with Claude under the hood and conversational smarts that old Alexa never had (we covered the India launch in detail earlier). Calling is the natural next step for that same wave.

05.Agents talking to agents

One detail from Instinct's recent updates deserves attention: users can now let their assistant contact other people's assistants through a trusted network. Your agent negotiates with my agent, and the two of us never speak. It sounds gimmicky until you think about scheduling a meeting between four busy people. Honestly, what surprised me most this week is how quickly the agent-to-agent plumbing is arriving alongside the human-facing stuff — the two trends feed each other.

06.What about India?

Neither calling feature works in India yet — Muse is US-focused and Instinct has no local presence. But the gap won't last. India is a voice-first, phone-first market; anyone who's watched their mother book a doctor's appointment or haggle with an electrician over a call knows the demand is enormous. When this capability arrives on WhatsApp — which is where a billion Indians already live — it will be a bigger deal here than anywhere else. Google is already preparing agents that manage family life for everyone in the household (we wrote about Google CC here), and the same pattern will apply.

Until then, the practical move isn't waiting — it's learning to direct these agents well. Prompting an agent that calls, books and negotiates on your behalf is a different skill from chatting with a chatbot, and it's exactly what the GPT-6 Astra — Zero to Hero Guide drills through its AI agents and computer use chapters — including how to keep costs sane when an agent runs tasks in the background all day.

Key Takeaways

  • Instinct Concierge places real phone calls — bookings, waitlists, billing disputes — in early access now
  • Meta's Muse can call US businesses, prioritising users who asked for it
  • Instinct: $350M raised at $2.5B, reportedly in talks for $1B at a $10B valuation
  • Agents now handle email sign-ups and can contact other agents through trusted networks
  • India is a voice-first market — expect calling agents to matter here more than anywhere

What This Means for You

If you run a small business in India, read this as an early warning: your customers may soon send an agent to negotiate with you, not a phone call they make themselves. And if you're a professional or student, the skill being rewarded is shifting — from doing the errand to directing the system that does it. Start practising with the agents you already have. The people who learn to delegate to AI now, before everyone's agent is calling restaurants, will be the ones others ask for help.
