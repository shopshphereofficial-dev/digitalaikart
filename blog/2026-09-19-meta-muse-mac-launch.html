Meta Muse for Mac: AI Agent Now Runs Your Computer

#MetaMuse#Mac#AIAgents#MetaAI

TL;DR

  • Meta released a native Mac app for its Muse AI agent on September 17 — nine days after the phone launch
  • Muse can now work across your files, Messages, Calendar, Notes and Mail, with your permission
  • A separate "Sentinel" agent gates everything Muse does before it touches the internet
  • US-only for now, but computer use is the direction every AI agent is heading

Meta's Muse agent has taken its biggest step yet — out of the chat window and onto the desktop. On September 17, Meta released a native Mac app, making this the first version of Muse that can get things done directly on your computer. It works with your files, Messages, Calendar, Notes and Mail, wherever they already live. If that sentence makes you both excited and slightly nervous, you're having the correct reaction.

01.From asking to acting

A chatbot needs you to gather information and paste it into the conversation. An agent with access to your computer goes and gets the context itself. If a date is in Calendar, a detail is buried in Notes and the supporting PDF is sitting in Downloads, you just ask Muse to work across all three — no folder-opening safari required. Ask it to find a file and it doesn't need you to tell it which folder to search.

Meta is also pitching continuity: a task can begin on your phone, continue on the Mac and be nudged along through WhatsApp, with Muse keeping the context throughout. That's a fundamentally different product shape from the assistants we're used to, and it's why Muse crossed 730,000 US downloads in its first few days — slightly ahead of the 707,000 that Meta's standalone AI app managed in a comparable window, per Sensor Tower data reported by TechCrunch.

02.The Sentinel: Meta's answer to the fear

Here's the part I found most interesting. Meta knows that handing your inbox, calendar and a shell to a piece of software that runs unattended is scary — Tarek Sheasha, a VP at Meta Superintelligence Labs, admitted as much, writing that their own early experiments "didn't always work out as planned." Their answer is architectural: a separate Sentinel agent runs on the same machine, kept apart from Muse at the system level. Nothing Muse does reaches the internet unless the Sentinel approves it, and the Sentinel asks you for permission when needed.

Permissions are opt-in: you choose which apps Muse connects to and exactly how much access it gets. For email, you decide whether Muse can only read your mail or also send on your behalf. Full Disk Access is optional, and everything is changeable or revocable through Settings. Meta's safety team also says Muse was built with prompt-injection awareness — the attack where hidden text on a webpage tricks an agent into doing something it shouldn't.

03.The work laptop problem

On your personal Mac, granting an agent access to selected files and apps is one person's decision. On an office Mac, the same machine may hold customer information, internal documents and confidential conversations. The more context an agent can gather on its own, the more useful it becomes — and the more likely it is to expose something you didn't think about when approving a task. Meta's launch doesn't resolve that tension; it just surfaces it. Expect many IT departments to have strong opinions before Muse touches a managed device.

If you're curious how this agent economy is being wired together for developers, we covered Muse's connectors launch in detail earlier — it's the plumbing that makes moments like this Mac release possible.

04.A delayed product, finally shipping

Context worth remembering: Zuckerberg revealed earlier this month that Meta actually delayed Muse before launch to get the safety right (we wrote about that delay here), even as he publicly rejected the broader industry slowdown. The Mac app arriving a week after the phone version suggests the cadence is now full speed. Muse is currently available in the US only, with a free tier that has usage limits and paid plans for heavier use — Meta says wider expansion is coming, though India timings haven't been announced.

05.Why this matters for India

Honestly, what surprised me most is how quickly "computer use" went from research demo to consumer product. The Mac release puts an agent where your documents, messages and work actually live — not inside a sandboxed app. When (not if) Muse or a competitor brings this to India, the killer combination is obvious: a WhatsApp-connected agent that also operates your desktop. For students, that's research across files and notes without the copy-paste dance; for small business owners, it's an assistant that lives inside the actual machine where the business runs.

Key Takeaways

  • Muse for Mac launched September 17 — the first version that acts directly on your computer
  • It works across files, Messages, Calendar, Notes and Mail with opt-in, revocable permissions
  • A separate Sentinel agent must approve anything Muse sends to the internet
  • Muse crossed 730,000 US downloads in its first few days, beating Meta's own AI app launch
  • US-only for now; India availability is unannounced

What This Means for You

The direction of travel is clear: your next assistant won't live in a tab you visit — it will live on the machine you work on, reading what you allow and acting on what you approve. For Indian professionals and students, the practical move is to get comfortable with agent workflows now, while the stakes are low and the tools are forgiving.
