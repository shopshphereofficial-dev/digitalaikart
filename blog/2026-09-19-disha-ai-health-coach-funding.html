Disha AI Health Coach Raises ₹44 Crore in Series A

#HealthTech#AIStartups#IndiaFunding#AICoach

TL;DR

  • Disha, the AI health coaching platform from the team behind Curelink, raised ₹43.88 crore in a Series A led by General Catalyst
  • It offers 24x7 diet, fitness and chronic-care coaching in Hindi, English and Hinglish, across 15 health conditions
  • Claims 70 lakh sign-ups, 3 lakh active users and 1.4 crore messages exchanged on the platform
  • Post-money valuation jumped about 52% to roughly ₹288 crore

While the tech world argues about trillion-dollar AI valuations, a quieter — and honestly more relatable — AI funding story landed this week. Disha, the AI-powered health coaching platform built by the team behind Curelink, has raised ₹43.88 crore in a Series A round led by General Catalyst, according to regulatory filings reported by Entrackr. It's one of the clearest signals yet that global investors are betting on AI that speaks to Bharat in its own language.

01.What just happened

Disha's board approved the issuance of 12,309 Series A CCPS at an issue price of ₹35,643.64 per share to raise the ₹43.88 crore. General Catalyst led with ₹38.20 crore, existing investor Elevation Capital put in ₹5.20 crore, and Flamel Performing Creatives chipped in ₹48 lakh. Elevation had earlier co-led the company's roughly ₹26 crore seed round with Venture Highway.

Per Entrackr's estimates, the post-money valuation rose about 52% to around ₹288 crore, up from ₹190 crore in the previous round. The fresh capital is earmarked for working capital, developing the AI models and tech platform, business expansion and general corporate purposes.

02.What Disha actually does

Think of Disha as a health coach living in your phone. It offers personalised diet, fitness and chronic-care support across 15 conditions — and it does so in Hindi, English and Hinglish, with coaching available 24x7. That language detail matters more than it sounds. Most health apps in India are built for English-first users; the moment your diabetes coach talks to you the way a family doctor in Lucknow would, the product reaches a completely different hundred million people.

The traction numbers back that up: the company claims 70 lakh sign-ups, 3 lakh active users and 1.4 crore messages exchanged on the platform. Founders Aman Singla and Divyansh Jain continue to hold the largest stakes at 31.10% each after the round, with General Catalyst at 13.33% and Elevation Capital at 9.96%.

03.The numbers behind the story

Here's the honest part — this is still an early-stage business financially. Disha's revenue doubled to ₹4.91 crore in FY25 from ₹2.47 crore in FY24, while its loss narrowed 22.5% to ₹4.45 crore. A ₹288 crore valuation on under ₹5 crore of revenue tells you investors are paying for the AI thesis and the user base, not current financials. That's normal for a Series A, but it's worth saying out loud instead of pretending every funding round equals a profitable business.

The angel list is interesting too: Paytm founder Vijay Shekhar Sharma and ShareChat co-founder Ankush Sachdeva each hold around 0.16% — founders who know exactly how hard Indian-language consumer products are to build.

04.Why global money likes Hinglish health AI

What surprised me most about this round is who led it. General Catalyst is a top-tier Silicon Valley firm — the kind that used to only look at Indian startups through a Bengaluru-SaaS lens. Now it's leading a round for an app whose superpower is Hinglish health coaching. The logic is simple arithmetic: 24x7 human coaching at 70-lakh-user scale would need thousands of nutritionists. With AI doing the heavy lifting and human experts supervising, the economics finally work for a country where a gym membership is still a luxury for most families.

It also fits the broader wave we've been tracking on this blog — Indian AI in healthcare is having a moment. We covered AIIMS building an AI that reads mammograms like a second doctor, and Kardi Ai bringing long-term ECG monitoring to India. Disha's bet is the consumer end of that same spectrum. One caveat worth repeating: an AI health coach is brilliant for habits, diet and follow-ups, but for anything that feels serious, a qualified doctor still comes first.

  • AI health coaching in Hindi, English and Hinglish across 15 conditions, available 24x7
  • Claims 70 lakh sign-ups and 1.4 crore messages; revenue doubled to ₹4.91 crore in FY25

What This Means for You

If you're a consumer, expect a wave of AI-first health, fitness and coaching apps that finally speak your language — compare them on evidence, not ads, and keep real doctors in the loop for anything clinical. If you're a builder or student, this round is a lesson: you don't need a frontier model of your own to build something fundable.
