Nasscom and CAIT tie up to teach India's traders how to use AI

#AIIndia#MSME#DigitalSkilling#Retail

TL;DR

  • Nasscom's FutureSkills Prime and CAIT signed an MoU on 27 October 2026 to train traders and MSMEs in AI and digital tools.
  • The focus is practical: AI for small business tasks, digital commerce, cloud tools and protection from cyber fraud.
  • CAIT says India has more than nine crore traders, and that lack of awareness is the biggest barrier to adoption.
  • No enrolment targets, fees or rollout timeline have been announced yet.

01.A deal aimed at the shop floor

On 27 October 2026, in New Delhi, the Confederation of All India Traders signed a memorandum of understanding with FutureSkills Prime, the digital skilling platform run jointly by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology and Nasscom. The signing took place at an event called Tech Connect for Traders and MSMEs, held at The Ashok hotel, which brought government officials, technology leaders and members of the trading community into one room.

Union Minister of State for Electronics and IT and for Commerce and Industry Jitin Prasada attended, along with CAIT Secretary General and Member of Parliament Praveen Khandelwal and Nasscom President Rajesh Nambiar. The idea behind the partnership is simple: take structured technology learning, which so far has mostly gone to students and IT professionals, and point it at people who run shops, trading firms and small manufacturing units.

02.What the training will actually cover

The MoU lists four broad areas. The first is practical, business-level use of artificial intelligence and agentic AI for small enterprises, meaning tools that can do a piece of work on their own rather than just answer a question. The second is digital commerce and cloud tools, aimed at widening the market a small seller can reach. The third is cybersecurity and prevention of online fraud. The fourth is plain awareness, so that traders know these courses exist and can actually enrol.

Press Trust of India reported that the pact covers training in AI, cybersecurity and digital marketing. None of the partners have published a syllabus, a fee structure, enrolment targets or a start date, so it is not yet clear how a shopkeeper in a small town would sign up or what a course would cost in time and money.

03.Why nine crore traders are the target

Khandelwal put the scale in perspective, saying India has more than nine crore traders, alongside large numbers of people working in transport, street vending and other non-corporate businesses. His diagnosis was blunt: lack of awareness, not lack of interest, is the biggest barrier to technology adoption in that part of the economy. He said the two organisations will jointly run a nationwide campaign on AI adoption and protection against cyber fraud, and that the aim is to take it down to the village level.

Prasada argued that AI has to move past conference speeches and reach the counter. He described how localised AI applications could help a neighbourhood shopkeeper work out storage, logistics and electricity costs, plan seasonal inventory and read market trends, and said technology should be democratised and reach the last mile. He also pointed to rural 5G coverage as the infrastructure that makes this possible, and urged that the gains be measured in real productivity at Tier-2 and Tier-3 businesses rather than in event photographs.

04.The fraud warning sitting next to the AI pitch

The cybersecurity half of the deal is not a footnote. Prasada noted that wherever digitalisation grows, the risk of cyber threats grows with it, and said that graph moves in step. For a small seller, that risk is concrete: a fake payment screenshot, a fraudulent QR code, a supplier invoice that arrives from a lookalike email address, or a customer on a marketplace who claims a refund that never happened.

That is why the campaign pairs AI training with fraud prevention rather than treating them as separate subjects. Nambiar framed the wider point by saying that every business today is a technology business, and that small traders in the age of AI and agentic AI should be at the front of adoption instead of being left behind. He added that combining adoption with skilling is what improves productivity and customer reach for MSMEs.

05.What this means for you

If you run a small business, the practical value here is not the MoU itself but the training that may follow it. The tasks the minister named, cost estimation, inventory planning and demand forecasting, are the ones where a free or cheap tool can save real hours each week. Watch for announcements from FutureSkills Prime and CAIT rather than paying for a private course on day one, and check whether anything you are offered is government-backed or just marketed that way.

If you are a student or a professional, this is a useful signal about where the demand is heading. Skilling money is moving towards people who can explain AI to a non-technical business owner and set it up for them, which is a different skill from training a model. It also means the audience for AI work in India is widening well beyond the metros. The honest caveat is that an MoU is a promise of cooperation, not a programme, and nothing has been announced yet on how many people will actually be trained or when.

Frequently asked questions

Is the AI training for traders free?
Not announced. The partners have not shared fees, enrolment targets or a rollout timeline, so check FutureSkills Prime and CAIT for the actual terms before assuming it is free.

Who can join the programme?
The MoU is aimed at traders, MSMEs and small businesses, with a stated plan to reach the village level. No eligibility rules or application dates have been published yet.

Will learning AI help a small shop immediately?
It can help with cost estimates, seasonal inventory and demand trends, which is what the government cited. But an MoU is only a starting point, and no course guarantees a jump in sales.
