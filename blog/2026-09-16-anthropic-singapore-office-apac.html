Anthropic Is Opening a Singapore Office — Its Fifth in Asia-Pacific

TL;DR

  • Anthropic is setting up a Singapore office next month — its fifth location in Asia-Pacific, after Tokyo, Bengaluru, Seoul and Sydney
  • Singapore ranks 2nd of 121 countries for Claude usage per capita, with adoption running 5.81x higher than expected
  • Demand is strongest in financial services and government — the heavily regulated sectors
  • OpenAI and Google DeepMind are making similar Asia-Pacific moves, so the regional AI race is officially on

Anthropic announced on Wednesday that it will open an Anthropic Singapore office next month — the company's fifth location in Asia-Pacific and its latest bet that serious enterprise AI demand now lives in this half of the world. The move joins existing regional offices in Tokyo, Bengaluru, Seoul and Sydney, and comes with a new Asean general manager, local hiring, and a visit from senior executives in October.

01.Why Singapore? The numbers are wild

According to Anthropic's latest Economic Index, Singapore ranks second globally out of 121 countries for Claude usage per capita — and local adoption is running 5.81 times higher than you'd expect for a country of its size. That's what made it a "standout market for Claude," in the company's own words.

"As we grow across Apac, opening an office in Singapore – where Claude usage per capita is among the highest in the world – is a natural next step," said Chris Ciauri, Anthropic's managing director of international. His logic is simple: a local presence means hiring people who know the market, working side by side with customers and partners building on Claude.

Dale Finlay has been appointed general manager of Asean and will be based in Singapore, with hiring already underway across operational roles.

02.The India connection nobody should miss

Here's the part that matters for us: Bengaluru is already on that list of Anthropic's Asia-Pacific offices. While Singapore gets the headlines this week, India remains a core part of Anthropic's regional footprint — and the same forces driving the Singapore expansion (enterprise demand, regulated industries going all-in on AI) are playing out in Indian offices too.

Anthropic says demand has been particularly strong in regulated sectors like financial services and government — which, if you've watched Indian banks and public institutions adopt AI over the last year, sounds very familiar. Banks in Mumbai, fintechs in Bengaluru, GCCs in Hyderabad — they're all in the middle of the same transformation.

It's also a healthy reminder that the AI jobs map is expanding beyond San Francisco. Regional offices across APAC mean account managers, solutions engineers, policy roles and partner teams — and AI safety coordination, which the big labs are now doing together, as we covered earlier this week, keeps adding new kinds of work.

And none of this slows the bigger story — Anthropic is now drawing up plans for a $2 trillion IPO.

03.The race for Asia is heating up

Anthropic isn't alone. In May, OpenAI announced its first Applied AI Lab outside the US — also in Singapore — with plans to hire more than 200 specialised AI professionals as part of a S$300 million investment, and it has reportedly been in talks to lease around 100,000 square feet at the new Shaw Tower on Beach Road. Google DeepMind has been expanding regionally too.

Honestly, what surprised me most is the pace. Three years ago, all of this would have been unthinkable — the frontier labs treated Asia as a market to sell into, not a place to build. Now it's a hiring ground for thousands of AI professionals.

04.What enterprises in the region are actually buying

Worth noting what Anthropic says it will support: organisations "of every size — from startups and digital natives to large enterprises and the public sector." That's not accidental positioning. The money in Asia-Pacific is in regulated, compliance-heavy industries that need AI which can be trusted with sensitive data.

For Indian professionals and founders, that's the signal to read: the region's AI economy is being built around enterprise reliability, not just chatbot novelty.
