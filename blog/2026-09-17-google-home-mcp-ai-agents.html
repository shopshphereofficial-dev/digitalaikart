Google Home MCP: Any AI Agent Can Now Run Your Smart Home

TL;DR

  • Google Home now supports MCP, letting third-party AI agents like Claude securely control your devices and read your home's event history
  • Agents can do cross-camera analysis, count how many laundry loads you did last week, and even talk to you over speakers
  • It's US-only for now, limited to Google Home Premium Advanced subscribers at $20/month
  • It doesn't replace Gemini for Home — it's a layer on top, and a big developer play by Google

Here's something that would have sounded absurd two years ago: Google Home MCP is here, and it lets third-party AI agents — not Google's own assistant, but agents like Anthropic's Claude, Open Claw, Hermes and Google's own Antigravity — securely work with all the devices and event history in your Google Home ecosystem. Your smart home is no longer walled off to one company's voice assistant. Honestly, this is one of the quietest big announcements of the week, and it deserves way more attention than it's getting in India.

01.What is Google Home MCP, exactly?

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol — an open standard that lets AI agents connect to external tools and data in a standardised way. Google has now built an MCP integration for Google Home, which means any AI agent that supports MCP can plug directly into your home's control and data layer. "It allows any AI agents that support MCP, including Google Antigravity, Claude, Hermes or Open Claw, to securely work with all of the devices and event history in your Google Home ecosystem," said Taylor Lehman, group product manager at Google Home & Nest. Read that again — Google is inviting its competitors' agents into the living room.

02.What your AI agent can actually do around the house

This goes well beyond "turn off the lights." Because the agent gets access to your home's underlying data and control layer, it can reason across events, not just obey single commands. Google's examples include cross-camera analysis — asking your agent what your kid did after getting home from school — and using device state history to figure out how many loads of laundry you did last week, or how long the lights were left on. The agent can also send you an audio message over your Google Home speaker when it finishes a task, and even build you a custom dashboard for controlling your home. That's the shift: from command-and-control to something contextual and proactive.

03.No, it's not replacing Gemini for Home

Before anyone panics — Gemini for Home remains Google's own assistant inside the Home app and on Nest speakers. The MCP integration is an additional layer: it lets third-party agents interact with your Google Home devices through their own interfaces. So you could be chatting with Claude in the Claude app and have it manage your living room without touching the Google Home app at all. It's an open door, not a demolition. (If you're curious how Google's voice AI is evolving generally, we covered the Gemini 3.8 Live launch in detail earlier this week.)

04.The catch: price, region and setup

Now for the part that matters to us in India. At launch, Home MCP is limited to Google Home Premium Advanced users in the US — that's $20/month or $200/year — with access rolling out over the coming weeks. Setup isn't a one-tap affair either: you need to create a Google Cloud project and configure it to use Home MCP. That's clearly aimed at tinkerers and developers for now, not your parents. There's no announced timeline for India yet, but the direction is unmistakable — and given how many Atomberg, Qubo, Dreame and Wipro devices Indian homes already run through Google Home, this will land here eventually. When Amazon launched Alexa+ in India with Hinglish support just yesterday, it was clear the big players see Indian smart homes as prime territory.

05.The real story: Google wants to be the AWS of smart homes

Here's the thing nobody's talking about: the biggest change isn't that Claude can switch your geyser on. It's that Google is positioning itself as the infrastructure layer of the smart home. Over the past couple of years, Google has opened API access to its smart home, made Gemini for Home a full-stack offering, and now it's letting any MCP-compatible agent build on top of its device data. Developers can even use Google's Antigravity coding tool to build custom AI agents that tap into the Home control layer. The AWS analogy writes itself — Google provides the rails, everyone else builds the trains. And no matter which agent you pick, as long as it's running on Google Home, Google wins.

One thing worth flagging before you get too excited: giving an AI agent access to your cameras and your entire home's event history is a genuine privacy decision, not a feature toggle. The convenience is real, but so is the data exposure. Key Takeaways

  • Google Home MCP lets any MCP-compatible AI agent — Claude, Open Claw, Hermes, Antigravity — control devices and read event history
  • Agents can do cross-camera queries, analyse usage patterns, send voice updates and build custom dashboards
  • It's US-only for now, on the $20/month Premium Advanced tier, and requires Google Cloud project setup
  • Gemini for Home stays; MCP is an extra layer on top
  • The bigger play is Google becoming the smart home's infrastructure layer for developers

What This Means for You

If you're a smart home enthusiast in India, don't hold your breath — this is US-first and developer-heavy right now. But if you're a builder or a student watching where the industry is going, pay close attention: "agents that can act on the real world" just moved from pitch decks to a shipping Google product. And for everyone else, this is a good moment to think about what you'd actually want an AI to manage in your home, and what you'd rather keep off-limits.
