Chhattisgarh AI Mission Begins: 5 Centres, 20 Labs, 1.5 Lakh Staff

#ChhattisgarhAI#IndiaAI#AISkills#GovTech

TL;DR

  • Chhattisgarh's AI Mission held its first steering committee meeting in Raipur on 10 November 2026.
  • The first-year plan covers 20 AI data labs and five Centres of Excellence across all five divisions.
  • About 1.5 lakh state government employees will be trained in AI, starting with 15,000 officials.
  • The plan sits inside a Rs 500 crore Mukhyamantri AI Mission launched earlier this year.

01.What happened in Raipur

Chhattisgarh's AI Mission held its first steering committee meeting on 10 November 2026 at Mahanadi Bhawan in Raipur, chaired by Chief Secretary Vikas Sheel. The meeting moved the state's AI plans from announcements to a first-year work plan: 20 AI data labs, five AI Centres of Excellence, and AI training for about 1.5 lakh state government employees, according to the state's public relations department.

Secretaries from urban development, good governance, school education, finance, IT, industry, skill development, health and revenue attended the meeting. Sheel asked the Information Technology Department to work with the other departments and prepare AI action plans based on what each department actually needs, rather than one common plan for the whole state government. Department-wise plans are the step that turns a training target into tools people actually use, because each department knows its own files, forms and bottlenecks.

02.One centre in each division

The five Centres of Excellence will be set up one each in the Raipur, Durg, Surguja, Bilaspur and Bastar divisions. In Raipur, the centre's work is being taken forward with NIT Raipur and IIIT Naya Raipur. Proposals for research grants, chairs and fellowships with the state's academic institutions are also part of the plan.

Spreading the centres across all five divisions means the programme is not limited to the capital region. Alongside the centres, 20 AI data labs are to be opened in the first year to support data and technical research. States elsewhere have taken a similar route, with Karnataka approving an AI Centre of Excellence at IIIT Raichur. AI is also already visible in the state's industry, where BALCO runs an AI humanoid assistant at its smelter.

03.The biggest target is people

The largest single target in the first year is training. About 1.5 lakh state government employees will be trained to use AI in administrative work and to improve governance. In the first phase, 15,000 officials will receive specialised training through institutions such as IIIT, NIT and IIM.

The mission will also develop AI courses and digital learning material for school students and for higher and technical education, with a goal of training thousands of young people in the first year. That makes the programme wider than a staff upskilling drive, because it reaches classrooms as well as government offices. Training 1.5 lakh employees is a large number for a state with a relatively small administration, and the first phase of 15,000 is the part that will show whether the model works before it is scaled up.

04.The Rs 500 crore plan behind it

Chief Minister Vishnu Deo Sai launched the Rs 500 crore Mukhyamantri AI Mission earlier this year. A state government article published in September said the mission plans 100 AI labs and five Centres of Excellence, aims to connect about 6 lakh students and 1.5 lakh officials with AI, and seeks to make more than 50 government services more effective using AI. The 20 data labs cleared now are therefore the first slice of a much larger lab network, not the whole build-out.

The same article said an AI-based data centre and an AI special economic zone are being developed in Nava Raipur with an investment of about Rs 1,000 crore. Chhattisgarh is not alone in this. Goa announced a Rs 250 crore AI policy in October, and Andhra Pradesh recently cleared a physical AI policy, as states build their own programmes instead of waiting on central schemes.

05.What it means for Indian readers

For students, the signal is that AI money is reaching state institutions, not only metro campuses. Students and researchers at NIT Raipur and IIIT Naya Raipur are closest to the first work, and the proposed research grants, chairs and fellowships with state universities and colleges are worth tracking if you are planning a project. School students in the state can expect AI courses and learning material built for them, and the wider target of reaching about 6 lakh students suggests the classroom push will outlast the first year.

For working professionals, 1.5 lakh government employees learning AI in daily administrative work changes how files, forms and citizen services move, and that affects anyone dealing with a government office. For small businesses, departments that begin using AI tools will need trainers, software vendors and support staff, and the planned AI special economic zone in Nava Raipur is a longer-term opening. The real test will be how fast the data labs and trained officials turn into tools that citizens actually use, because a trained workforce without working software changes very little on the ground.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Chhattisgarh AI Mission?
It is the state's Rs 500 crore Mukhyamantri AI Mission, launched by Chief Minister Vishnu Deo Sai, to expand the use of AI in governance, education, research and public services. Its first steering committee meeting was held on 10 November 2026.

Where will the five AI Centres of Excellence come up?
One each in the Raipur, Durg, Surguja, Bilaspur and Bastar divisions. Work in Raipur is being advanced with NIT Raipur and IIIT Naya Raipur, and 20 AI data labs are planned in the first year.

How many government employees will be trained in AI?
