Gnani Artha: India's New Sovereign AI Stack Explained

#Gnani#Artha#SovereignAI#IndiaAI

TL;DR

  • Bengaluru's Gnani.ai has launched Artha, a sovereign AI stack for enterprises and government bodies that must keep data inside their own network
  • It pairs Evon 3.3, a 30-billion-parameter multilingual model, with Plexus, an agentic platform for real enterprise workflows
  • The stack runs inside your own data centre or VPC — data never leaves your infrastructure
  • Sovereign AI is having a moment in India, from the NTA's on-prem models to the ₹10,372-crore IndiaAI Mission

If you've been reading this blog for a while, you know the big theme of India's AI year: stop renting intelligence from California and start building our own. This week that story picked up another chapter, and honestly, a slightly unexpected one. Gnani.ai — a Bengaluru startup best known for voice AI — has launched Gnani Artha, a sovereign AI stack built for enterprises and government departments that want serious AI without their data ever leaving their own network. And the timing, as it turns out, could not be better.

01.What exactly is Gnani Artha?

Artha is a complete package, not just a model. It bundles Evon 3.3, Gnani's flagship 30-billion-parameter language model, with Plexus, an agentic platform that turns the model's intelligence into actual work — reading documents, connecting to internal systems, completing multi-step processes. The stack handles multimodal, multilingual processing across Indian regional languages, and the whole thing is built for deployment on local infrastructure. That last bit is the "sovereign" part, and it's the entire point.

02.Inside Evon 3.3, the 30-billion-parameter model

Here's where it gets technically interesting. Evon 3.3 has 30 billion total parameters, but it uses a Mixture-of-Experts architecture in which only about 3.5 billion parameters are active for each token — which keeps compute costs and hardware demands dramatically lower than the raw parameter count suggests. It's built on a hybrid Mamba-Transformer MoE architecture, the weights are open and published on Hugging Face, and it's designed around English plus 10 Indic languages. That matters more than it sounds. Most global frontier models treat Indian languages as an afterthought — bolted on through translation rather than built in. A model that's Indian-languages-first behaves very differently when the job is customer support in Tamil or processing government paperwork in Hindi.

03.Plexus: where the model becomes a workforce

A smart model on its own is a party trick. Plexus is the enterprise layer on top: a platform for building, orchestrating and deploying autonomous AI agents that plug into an organisation's systems, documents and workflows. Gnani says Artha can be deployed inside an organisation's own data centre or virtual private cloud, with customer data staying inside the organisation's network. For a bank or a PSU, that sentence is the difference between "interesting demo" and "approved purchase".

04.Sovereign AI is suddenly everywhere in India

Artha doesn't exist in a vacuum. ThePrint reported this week that the National Testing Agency ran the translation for the NEET-UG retest on sovereign AI models installed on its own GPUs — on-premise, no cloud — precisely because the translation stage had been identified as the weak point in this year's paper leak. Back in February, the government unveiled three state-backed models under the ₹10,372-crore IndiaAI Mission: Sarvam AI, the multilingual BharatGen, and Gnani's own voice model. We covered Sarvam's Indus chat app in detail earlier this week, and we also wrote about the NTA's sovereign AI push for NEET — the pattern is impossible to miss. Over 38,000 GPUs have already been deployed under the mission against an initial target of 10,000. Artha is Gnani doubling down commercially on the same national wave.

05.Who actually needs this?

Let's be real — if you're a student or a freelancer, a private AI stack is not in your near future, and it shouldn't be. Artha is aimed at regulated Indian enterprises: banks, insurers, hospitals, PSUs, defence contractors — anyone whose compliance officer has ever said "absolutely not" to a public cloud deployment. For those organisations, an Indic-language, self-hosted stack ticks a box they've been trying to tick for two years. But you don't need your own data centre to face the underlying problem. Controlling what you spend on AI is the everyday version of sovereignty, and the cost-optimization chapter in our GPT-6 Astra — Zero to Hero Guide is written for exactly that — it's the difference between AI as a business line and AI as a hobby you can no longer afford.

06.My honest take

What surprised me most isn't the model — it's the packaging. Plenty of Indian labs have released models; very few have shipped a full stack you can run behind your own firewall, agents and all, tuned for Indian languages from the ground up. Two caveats, though. There are no public benchmark comparisons against global frontier models yet, so "good enough for the enterprise" is still partly a promise. And the sovereign-enterprise space in India is about to get brutally crowded — Sarvam, Arcee, Ola Krutrim and others are all circling the same customers. But the direction of travel is unmistakable: India is graduating from AI consumer to AI builder, and stacks like Artha are what that graduation actually looks like.

Key Takeaways

  • Gnani Artha = Evon 3.3 (30B-parameter MoE model) + Plexus (enterprise agent platform)
  • Deploys inside your own data centre or VPC — data never leaves your network
  • Evon 3.3 is open-weight on Hugging Face and built for English + 10 Indic languages
  • Sovereign AI is now national policy — from the NTA's on-prem models to the ₹10,372-crore IndiaAI Mission
  • The real buyers are regulated enterprises and government, not individual developers
