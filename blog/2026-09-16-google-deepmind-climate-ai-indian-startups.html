Google DeepMind Picked 4 Indian Startups for Its First Climate AI Accelerator

TL;DR

  • Google's inaugural DeepMind Accelerator: AI for the Planet (APAC) has selected 16 organisations — and 4 of them are Indian
  • Terrastack, Varaha Climate, Farmers for Forests and Climitra Carbon get 3 months of Google's AI stack, technical support and expert mentorship
  • The programme began with a bootcamp in Singapore and wraps up with a Demo Day on December 10
  • Google paired the announcement with solar-rooftop AI for 30 crore Indian homes and a 150 MW solar project in Rajasthan

When you hear "AI startup in India", you probably picture another chatbot or a coding assistant. Here's a story from the other side — four Indian startups using AI for farming, forests and carbon removal, and Google DeepMind just gave them a global stage.

01.What this programme actually is

Google has announced the first-ever Google DeepMind Accelerator: AI for the Planet for the Asia-Pacific region — a 16-member cohort of startups, nonprofits and research teams from across APAC. The four Indian names in it: Terrastack, Varaha Climate, Farmers for Forests and Climitra Carbon.

And here's the part that makes it interesting — this isn't a funding round. It's an access programme. Over three months, participants get the latest Google AI stack, tailored technical support and mentorship from Google experts. The cohort kicked off with an in-person bootcamp in Singapore, runs virtually through September to December, and concludes with a Demo Day on December 10 in Singapore. For a small startup, that access to frontier models and Google engineers is often worth more than a cheque.

02.Meet the four Indian picks

Each one is attacking a different slice of the climate problem, and honestly, the variety is the best part:

  • Terrastack combines satellite and agronomic data to give smallholder farmers plot-level land intelligence — which sounds simple until you realise most farmers have never had precise, data-driven answers about their own land.
  • Varaha Climate helps small farmers earn carbon credits by verifying regenerative agriculture and carbon removal with remote sensing and AI. It sits in the programme's climate and carbon track.
  • Farmers for Forests uses AI-powered drones to turn smallholder agroforestry into measurable carbon and biodiversity action — attacking the measurement problem that has always plagued nature-based climate work.
  • Climitra Carbon applies Geo-AI to verify the removal of invasive plant species and their conversion into biochar, linking land restoration with scalable carbon solutions.

03.Google's quiet India climate blitz

The accelerator didn't come alone. Alongside it, Google announced a set of India-focused clean energy moves: an AI-powered solar API expanding across 300 million (30 crore) Indian rooftops to automate roof surveys and layouts, a long-term agreement with ReNew Power for a 150 MW utility solar project in Rajasthan, and an initiative with Mitti Labs to help rice farmers in Telangana adopt alternate wetting and drying — a technique that disrupts the methane-producing microbes in paddy fields. There are also fresh research and innovation opportunities opening up on the government's Manthan platform.

Put together, that's a company betting seriously on AI-for-sustainability in India — not just talk, but infrastructure-level commitments.

04.Why "boring" verification is the real story

Personally, what surprised me most about this cohort is how unglamorous the winning idea is. Nobody here is building a flashy app. Three of the four startups are essentially in the verification business — proving that carbon was removed, that forests are growing, that a farmer's field changed.

That's not boring at all once you follow the money. A carbon credit is only worth something if someone can prove the underlying activity happened. For decades, that proof required auditors walking around fields with clipboards. Satellites, drones and AI models can now do it continuously, at scale, for a fraction of the cost. Whoever verifies the carbon markets of the future will sit at a very profitable toll booth. And right now, a chunk of that infrastructure is being built in India.

Key Takeaways

  • 4 of 16 organisations in Google DeepMind's first AI for the Planet cohort are Indian
  • All four apply satellite data, remote sensing, drones and AI to farming, forests and carbon removal
  • The programme provides technical access and mentorship rather than direct funding, ending with a Demo Day on December 10 in Singapore
  • Google paired the announcement with solar-rooftop AI for 30 crore homes, a 150 MW Rajasthan solar project and rice-farming initiatives in Telangana
