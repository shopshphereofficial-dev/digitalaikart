India's Biggest AI-Only VC Fund Just Closed $105 Million — And It Wants to Bet on Ideas, Not Pitch Decks

TL;DR

  • Activate, founded by Aakrit Vaish and Pratyush Choudhury, closed its maiden fund at $105 million — roughly ₹1,000 crore
  • It plans to back 25–30 AI startups at the pre-seed or idea stage with cheques of $500K to $3 million
  • Its backers include Vinod Khosla, General Catalyst, Vijay Shekhar Sharma, Lalit Keshre and Ronnie Screwvala
  • Indian AI startups raised $676 million across 57 deals in H1 2026 — more than 4X the previous year

Here is a headline I did not expect to write this soon: India now has its largest venture capital platform focused exclusively on AI, and it has just finished raising its first fund. Activate, founded by Aakrit Vaish — the man behind chatbot pioneer Haptik — and former Together Fund partner Pratyush Choudhury, announced the final close of its maiden fund at $105 million (around ₹1,000 crore). In a funding winter where most funds are quietly shrinking their cheques, this one is betting big on the earliest, riskiest stage of Indian AI.

01.Where the $105 million sits

The fund is split into two parts: an $85 million flagship early-stage fund, plus $20 million deployed through dedicated growth investment vehicles. What caught my attention is that the flagship closed at 125% above its original target — meaning limited partners wanted to put in more money than the team initially asked for. The fund reported a first close of $75 million back in December, and has already made 10 investments in nine months: seven early-stage bets that are still in stealth, plus growth investments in Sarvam AI, ElevenLabs and Wispr Flow.

02.The cheque sizes tell you the philosophy

Activate is targeting 25–30 startups with initial equity investments between $500K and $3 million — roughly ₹4 crore to ₹25 crore. But here is the interesting part: it works with founders from as early as the ideation stage, in some cases before the company is even incorporated. Support covers product development, technical architecture, hiring, go-to-market and follow-on fundraising. The thesis is simple — in AI, the winning companies over the next decade are being dreamed up in hostels, tier-2 bedrooms and corporate cubicles right now, and by the time they have a polished pitch deck, the best funds have already missed them.

03.Look at who is bankrolling it

The limited partner list reads like a who's-who of Indian tech: more than 50 founders and AI researchers, around a dozen global VC general partners, and over 50 family offices and corporates. Named backers include Vinod Khosla, General Catalyst, Vijay Shekhar Sharma (Paytm), Lalit Keshre (Groww), Harsh Jain (Dream11), Bhavin Turakhia, Ronnie Screwvala and Ranjan Pai. When people who have built India's biggest internet companies put their own money into an AI-only fund, that tells you what they think the next decade looks like.

04.More than money

Activate is not just writing cheques. It runs a GenAI community of more than 15,000 technical practitioners across 40-plus groups, and has partnerships with NVIDIA, OpenAI, Anthropic, ElevenLabs, AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud and Notion — portfolio companies get access to up to $1 million in credits and benefits. The NVIDIA partnership, signed in February, gives founders access to the Nemotron family of open-source models along with technical training and compute resources. For a first-time founder in Indore or Kochi, that stack of credits and connections can matter almost as much as the cash.

05.The bigger wave behind this

Context matters here. Indian AI startups raised $676 million across 57 deals in the first half of 2026 — over 4X the same period last year, according to Inc42's funding report. Accel recently closed a $550 million fund for AI and deeptech; Elevation Capital launched its $500 million AI-focused Fund IX in July. Honestly, what surprises me most is not that money is flowing into Indian AI — it is how early the money is willing to arrive. The pre-seed end of the market, historically the weakest link in Indian venture capital, is exactly where the boldest bets are now being placed.

What This Means for You

If you have an AI idea sitting in your notes app, the message from this week is blunt: the excuse of "no funding for idea-stage in India" is fading fast. Funds like Activate are explicitly looking for people who understand a problem deeply — a shopkeeper's inventory mess, a clinic's paperwork, a coaching institute's doubts queue — and want to solve it with AI. You do not need IIT tags or an MBA; you need a working demo and clarity on whose problem you are solving.
