Sarvam AI's Indus App Is Here — India's Own Answer to ChatGPT

TL;DR

  • Bengaluru's Sarvam AI has launched Indus, a consumer chat app built for Indian users, now on the Google Play Store
  • It supports 10+ Indian languages, works voice-first, and follows a "sovereign" India-first data approach
  • Sarvam is already running AI at massive scale — over one crore voice calls for Mahindra Finance and a new AI layer for Aadhaar services
  • The battle won't be decided by benchmark scores — it'll be decided by who actually understands Bharat

If you've ever tried explaining something to a chatbot in Hinglish and watched it politely misunderstand every second word, this one's for you. Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI has launched Indus, a chat app built specifically for the Indian market — and it's live on the Google Play Store right now.

01.What exactly is Indus?

Indus is a consumer-facing chat application designed for the Indian landscape rather than adapted to it as an afterthought. It supports over 10 regional languages, and — this is the detail I like most — it's built voice-first. You talk, it responds. For a country where a massive chunk of internet users are more comfortable speaking than typing, that's not a nice-to-have feature. It's the whole point.

The app also follows what Sarvam calls a "sovereign" data approach — the idea being that your interactions stay within an India-first framework instead of everything routing through foreign infrastructure. For anyone who's ever wondered where their data goes when they chat with a global AI, that's a meaningful difference.

02.Voice-first is the killer detail

Here's the thing nobody talks about enough: most of India doesn't want to type in English. Ask anyone in a tier-2 or tier-3 city how they use their phone — voice notes, voice search, voice everything. Sarvam has been building for this reality for years. Its speech models are trained on Indian accents, dialects and the code-switching we all do — that seamless Hindi-English-Hinglish mix that makes global chatbots sweat.

Global AI companies treat Indian languages as a feature to bolt on later. For Sarvam, Indian languages are the foundation the product is built on. That philosophical difference shows up everywhere in how Indus works.

03.The quiet empire behind the app

Don't let the consumer app fool you into thinking Sarvam is a small player testing the waters. Just this week at the Global Fintech Fest 2026, Mahindra Finance announced an expanded voice AI partnership with Sarvam — voice agents operating in 12 Indian languages that have completed over one crore (10 million+) calls across sales, collections and employee engagement. That's one of the largest voice AI deployments in Indian financial services, full stop.

And it goes further. UIDAI — the Aadhaar authority — has partnered with Sarvam on a voice-based generative AI platform for Aadhaar services, giving users real-time alerts about potential overcharging and suspicious authentication activity. Crucially, that system runs entirely within UIDAI's own infrastructure. The company is backed by Lightspeed, Peak XV and Khosla Ventures, and works with Tata Capital, SBI Life, CRED, IDFC and LIC. This is an Indian AI company operating at population scale. Indus is just the consumer face of it.

04.So can it actually take on ChatGPT?

Honest answer: on raw, general-purpose intelligence, the global leaders are still ahead. I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But here's my take — Indus isn't trying to win a benchmark contest. It's betting that the Indian market gets decided on language depth, voice experience and price, not on who has the most parameters.

The challenges are real too. Researchers point out that quality dialect data is still patchy — accents and slang change every few hundred kilometres in India, and clean, labelled data for all 22 official languages simply doesn't exist in digital form yet. So this will be an evolution, not an overnight victory. But the direction is clear, and the fact that a homegrown company is even in this fight says a lot about how far India's AI stack has come.

05.The skill that matters more than the app

Here's something worth sitting with: whichever app wins — Indus, ChatGPT, Gemini — the people who benefit most will be those who know how to direct AI well, not those who picked the "right" app. Prompting is a genuinely transferable skill. Key Takeaways

  • Indus is live on the Google Play Store with 10+ Indian languages and a voice-first design
  • Sarvam already runs one of India's largest voice AI deployments — 1 crore+ calls for Mahindra Finance across 12 languages
  • UIDAI is using Sarvam's AI inside Aadhaar services, running on its own infrastructure
  • Indus wins or loses on language depth and voice experience, not benchmark scores
  • Learning to direct any AI well — prompting — is the skill that compounds, whatever app you use

What This Means for You

If you're a student or a working professional in India, just do one thing this week: download Indus alongside whatever you already use, and test both with the same voice queries in your own language. The difference will teach you more about where AI in India is heading than any article can.
