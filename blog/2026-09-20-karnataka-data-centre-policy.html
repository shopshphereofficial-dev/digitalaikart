Karnataka's ₹1,300 Crore Data Centre Policy Explained

TL;DR

  • Karnataka's cabinet has approved a Sustainable Data Centre Policy 2026–2031 with a ₹1,300.45 crore budget
  • The target: 1 GW of cumulative data centre IT load and 10–12% of India's AI-ready capacity by 2031
  • The trigger is "unprecedented" demand for computing from AI, cloud, 5G, fintech and global capability centres
  • Translation for readers: jobs, cheaper local compute and a bigger launchpad for AI startups in and around Bengaluru

While everyone was busy arguing about AI slowdowns and lawsuits in America this week, the Karnataka cabinet quietly did something that will matter far more to your daily life in India. On Friday, at a cabinet meeting held in Mangaluru, the state cleared two policies — one about marine biotechnology that grabbed the headlines, and one about data centres that tech folks should actually be paying attention to. It's called the Sustainable Data Centre Policy 2026–2031, and it comes with a budget of ₹1,300.45 crore.

01.What the cabinet actually approved

Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar's cabinet approved the policy to position Karnataka as a hub for next-generation digital infrastructure. The five-year policy carries a revised budget framework of ₹1,300.45 crore for implementation. In plain terms, the state is formally getting into the business of attracting and hosting the massive computing facilities that AI runs on — land, power connections, approvals, the works.

02.The numbers worth remembering

Two targets define this policy. First, Karnataka wants 1 gigawatt of cumulative data centre IT load by 2031. That "IT load" number is the power actually consumed by the servers, and 1 GW is serious scale — think of entire campuses humming with machines. Second, the state wants to corner 10–12% of India's AI-ready data centre capacity by 2031.

That second number is the more interesting one. "AI-ready" isn't just marketing — AI workloads need high-density racks, serious cooling and reliable power in a way that old-school web hosting never did. Whoever builds that capacity first gets to host the AI boom.

03.Why now? The AI compute crunch

IT Minister Priyank Kharge, who holds the Home Affairs, IT, Biotechnology and E-Governance portfolios, didn't mince words about the timing. The policy, he said, seeks to strengthen Karnataka's digital infrastructure backbone at a time when AI, cloud computing, IoT, 5G, global capability centres and fintech are driving "unprecedented demand" for computing capacity.

Anyone who works in Bengaluru knows this viscerally. The GCC story alone — global banks and retailers running their back offices out of the city — has collided with the AI wave, and every one of those companies now wants GPU capacity for their AI pilots. The state clearly doesn't want that demand met by servers sitting in someone else's state.

04.This is one piece of a much bigger puzzle

Zoom out and the pattern is unmistakable. At Semicon India 2026 in Delhi this week, the central government talked about ₹1 lakh crore of commitments under Semicon 2.0, with countries lining up to expand electronics and semiconductor work in India. And in November, the Prime Minister is set to launch the ₹1 lakh crore Research, Development and Innovation scheme, with AI and deeptech as focus areas.

Compute is becoming what land was in the last industrial era — the thing every state wants a stake in. Karnataka is simply moving early and loudly. If you want to see how deep this national push goes, we covered the Google–NVIDIA data centre partnership in detail earlier, and it's worth reading alongside this one.

05.What's in it for jobs and startups

Here's the part nobody's spelling out clearly. A gigawatt of data centre capacity doesn't just employ the engineers inside the facility — it creates ripples in construction, electrical work, cooling systems, security and facility management, plus the entire ecosystem of cloud and AI services built on top. Karnataka has also been investing in the talent pipeline separately — we wrote about Samsung's plan to train 2,000 Karnataka youth in AI earlier — and these two threads are clearly meant to meet.

For AI startups, local capacity matters more than most realise. If your agents are running heavy workloads, the distance between your servers and your users decides your latency, and where your data lives decides your compliance headaches. More AI-ready capacity inside Karnataka is good news for every founder building here.

And to be practical about it — if you're one of those founders planning to run AI agents at scale, your biggest bill after talent will be compute. 06.What to watch next

Policies are promises until implementation details land. Watch for three things: the specific incentives the state offers operators, how quickly land and power clearances actually move, and how rival states respond — several were openly pitching investors at Semicon India this week. Competition between states for data centre investment is exactly what India's AI ecosystem needs right now.

Key Takeaways

  • Karnataka approved a Sustainable Data Centre Policy 2026–2031 with a ₹1,300.45 crore budget over five years
  • Targets: 1 GW cumulative IT load and 10–12% of India's AI-ready data centre capacity by 2031
  • The driver is surging compute demand from AI, cloud, 5G, fintech and global capability centres
  • It complements national pushes like Semicon 2.0 and the ₹1 lakh crore RDI scheme launching November 3
  • Expect ripple effects in jobs, construction, power infrastructure and the local AI startup ecosystem

What This Means for You

If you're in Bengaluru or anywhere in Karnataka, this is quietly excellent news — the state that already hosts India's biggest tech workforce is now investing its own money in the physical layer of AI. For students, data centre operations and cloud infrastructure are becoming legitimate career paths that don't require you to be a machine learning researcher. For working professionals, more local compute means your company's AI projects stop dying in the "we don't have capacity" meeting.
