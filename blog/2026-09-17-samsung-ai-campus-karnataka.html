Samsung to Train 2,000 Karnataka Youth in AI This Year

#Samsung#Karnataka#AISkilling#YouthJobs

TL;DR

  • Samsung has launched Samsung Innovation Campus 2026 in Karnataka, aiming to train 2,000 young people in AI skills this year
  • It's part of Samsung's plan to skill 20,000 youth across 10 states — with hands-on AI and Generative AI training for ages 18–25
  • The programme runs with Nrupathunga University Bengaluru, plus ESSCI and TSSC sector skill councils
  • In 2025 the same programme trained over 20,000 people across India, with 48% women participation

Amid all the model launches and funding rounds, it's easy to forget that India's AI story ultimately runs on people who know how to use the stuff. So here's a quieter announcement that deserves attention: Samsung India has launched Samsung Innovation Campus 2026 in Karnataka, with a plan to train 2,000 young people in industry-relevant AI skills in the state this year.

The launch happened at the Federation of Karnataka Chambers of Industry and Commerce (FKCCI) in Bengaluru, with Basavaraj Rayareddy, Karnataka's Minister for Higher Education, as chief guest. The programme is being run in partnership with Nrupathunga University, Bengaluru — a nice touch, since it anchors the training in the local education system rather than a one-off corporate event.

01.What the AI training actually involves

This isn't a weekend webinar. Samsung Innovation Campus (SIC) is the company's flagship global skilling programme, now in its fourth year in India, and it targets youth aged 18 to 25 with hands-on AI and Generative AI training — structured learning, practical projects, mentorship from industry trainers, and career-readiness support. The 2026 edition is being implemented with the Electronics Sector Skills Council of India (ESSCI) and the Telecom Sector Skill Council (TSSC), and participants who complete it get a certificate. In Karnataka alone, the programme has already trained over 3,000 people to date.

The timing is deliberate, too. Karnataka is pushing hard to build an AI-native ecosystem, and a steady pipeline of trained 18-to-25-year-olds is exactly the raw material that pipeline needs.

02.20,000 youth, 10 states, one bigger plan

Karnataka is one chapter of a bigger book. Samsung has committed to empowering 20,000 youth across 10 states through SIC 2026, running the programme in around 30 cities across India. And the track record is real: in 2025, the programme trained more than 20,000 participants across the country, with 48% women participation — a number I genuinely wish more tech programmes could match. Since it started in India in 2022, SIC has trained over 26,500 young people in AI, coding, IoT and big data.

It slots into a pattern we've been writing about all month — India's AI skilling push is coming from every direction. IIT Madras just launched an AI venture studio to turn research into startups, and the government's IndiaAI mission is moving towards funding homegrown LLMs. Samsung's version attacks the same problem from the ground up: not founders or researchers, but the entry-level workforce.

03.Why free corporate AI training is a bigger deal than it sounds

Let's be honest about the economics. A decent generative AI course in India runs anywhere from ₹5,000 to ₹80,000 depending on the platform — and quality varies wildly. For a student in Hubballi or Mysuru with no industry contacts, a free, structured, industry-designed programme with a certificate at the end is a genuinely rare deal. The certification isn't a job guarantee, obviously, but it signals something employers increasingly scan for: has this person actually built anything with AI, or just watched videos about it?

That's the gap most self-learning leaves open, by the way — people consume content but never build. 04.The skilling-vs-jobs question

Here's my one caution, and it's the part nobody puts in press releases. Training 20,000 people in AI skills only pays off if the jobs absorb them — and right now the demand side is concentrated in bigger firms and startups in a handful of cities. Programmes like SIC work best when they're one leg of a stool that also includes hiring demand and local industry growth. Karnataka has the best odds of any state outside that equation, but the rest of the 10 states are the real test.

Still, directionally, this is the right kind of news. When the world's largest electronics company puts money into skilling Indian youth for AI — as it celebrates 30 years in the country, no less — it's a bet on India's AI workforce that smaller employers tend to follow.

Key Takeaways

  • Samsung Innovation Campus 2026 launched in Karnataka to train 2,000 youth in AI skills this year
  • Part of a national plan to train 20,000 youth across 10 states, run with ESSCI and TSSC
  • Hands-on AI and Generative AI training for ages 18–25, with certificates, mentoring and project-based learning
  • In 2025 the programme trained 20,000+ participants across India, with 48% women
  • Registration runs through the official ESSCI/SIC portals — worth checking if you or someone you know qualifies

What This Means for You

If you're 18–25 and in Karnataka, this is a no-brainer: free, industry-designed AI training with a certificate — go check the eligibility criteria on Samsung's registration portal before seats fill up. If you're outside the age bracket or the state, don't wait for a programme to reach you.
