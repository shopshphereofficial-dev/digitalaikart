Jio Haptik's Solo Gives Every Indian Small Business an AI Marketing Team

TL;DR

  • Jio Haptik's SOLO by Interakt gives solopreneurs two AI assistants — Tara for marketing, Ved for sales and support — that work end to end with almost no owner involvement
  • Businesses on the platform see average ROAS of about 2.3x, touching 4.5x with immediate WhatsApp follow-ups
  • Onboarding is as easy as photographing a product — Solo then generates a month of social content, reels included
  • A Delhi-NCR campaign found 90% of 300 participants would start a business if marketing and support were handled for them

Jio Haptik's Solo is one of those AI products that makes you go "obviously, someone should have built this years ago." The pitch: give every small business owner — the home baker, the boutique tailor, the neighbourhood gym — an AI team that markets, sells and follows up on their behalf, without hiring a single person. Built by Interakt under Jio Haptik (Reliance Jio's conversational, agentic and voice AI company), Solo is aiming squarely at the long tail of Indian business. And this week it made headlines on two fronts: real performance numbers, and a street-level campaign across Delhi-NCR.

01.Meet Tara and Ved, your new employees

Solo personifies its AI into two personas. Tara handles marketing; Ved manages customer support and sales, working independently toward the same goal. The onboarding is deliberately effortless: a business owner downloads the app, shares a website or Instagram handle, or simply photographs a product. Solo cleans up the image, studies the brand's existing tone of voice, and generates a full month of social content — video reels, captions, hashtags — posting it on the owner's behalf every single day.

Tara then goes a step further, putting part of the owner's budget directly into Meta ads that click through to WhatsApp or Instagram DM conversations, where Ved takes over — qualifying leads and nudging them toward a sale before they go cold. If you've been following how quickly agentic AI is eating real jobs, we covered how AI agents are even replacing pull requests in software teams — Solo is the small-business version of the same shift.

02.The numbers that actually matter

Return on ad spend is the metric marketers obsess over, but Jio Haptik CEO Ahshad Jussawala is candid that small business owners don't speak that language. As he puts it: a small business today doesn't know what ROAS is — they think in terms of "I will give you ten thousand, how much business will come from it." So Solo translates: it tells a home baker that a ₹5,000 marketing spend should yield fifteen to twenty high-converting leads.

Behind that plain talk sits real data. Average ROAS on the platform stands at roughly 2.3x, and it has touched 4.5x for businesses that benefit from immediate WhatsApp follow-ups. The real lift, Jussawala argues, isn't the ad — it's Ved's ability to personalise every conversation and respond before a lead goes cold, something traditional funnels (dependent on a human calling back the next day) have always struggled with.

03.Why Jio Haptik built it

Haptik's roots are in enterprise AI, but four to five years ago it turned its attention downward. That produced Interact, a WhatsApp marketing platform for small and medium businesses that worked with more than 100,000 SMBs over five years. A pattern kept repeating: owners loved the platform but struggled to use it well, because they had no time. As Jussawala says, the small business owner is doing everything himself — marketing, sales, support, and running the business. Solo is the answer to that observation: marketing and sales run end to end, with almost no involvement required from the owner.

04.The honest caveats

Worth knowing: roughly half of all the content the system produces today is AI-generated, though Jussawala says most of it doesn't get tagged as AI — "They're quite natural looking. We always have a human face." The final call on what gets published always rests with the business owner. And the scale is still early: Haptik estimates its addressable market at two to three million businesses over the next three years, in a country with roughly 60 million SMBs — for comparison, Tally, the country's largest SMB software ecosystem, serves around 2.5 million paying users. In other words, AI is arriving at India's kirana-grade businesses, but the gap between arrival and dominance is measured in years, not months. 05.The Delhi-NCR roadshow

Solo also just took its "You Bring the Idea, We Bring the Team" campaign to Delhi-NCR — a three-day activation with a glass-fronted Canter travelling across IIT Delhi, NSUT, Connaught Place and Cyber Park in Gurugram. Over 300 participants pitched business ideas to a jury for a ₹1 lakh cash prize, and roughly 10,000 people engaged with the activation. The most telling stat: 90% of the 300 participants said they would be more likely to start a business if marketing and customer support were taken care of. That's the anxiety Solo is betting on — and the opportunity.

It's a good moment for Indian AI overall. Sarvam AI just launched its Indus chat app for the masses, and enterprise AI like Mahindra Finance's deployments keeps deepening — the tools are finally being built for Indian users, in Indian contexts, at Indian price points.

Key Takeaways

  • Solo = Tara (marketing) + Ved (sales & support), two AI personas that run a small business's growth end to end
  • Average ROAS on the platform is ~2.3x, touching 4.5x with instant WhatsApp follow-ups
  • Onboarding needs only a website link, Instagram handle, or a product photo
  • About half the content is AI-generated, but the owner always approves what goes out
  • Target: 2-3 million businesses in 3 years, out of India's ~60 million SMBs

What This Means for You

If you run a small business — or you've been sitting on an idea because you can't afford a marketing person — this is your signal to experiment. Try the free tier, give Tara a product photo, and see what a month of automated content feels like. Keep your own judgement in the loop, because as Jussawala himself admits, equal access to tools doesn't guarantee equal outcomes — your product and service still decide whether customers come back.
