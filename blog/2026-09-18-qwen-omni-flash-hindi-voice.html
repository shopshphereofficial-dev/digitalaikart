Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash: Hindi Voice AI at 98% Lower Cost

TL;DR

  • Alibaba just launched Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, a native omnimodal model that handles text, audio and video together
  • Speech recognition in 74 languages — including Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu — and speech generation in 29, including Hindi
  • API cost per hour for voice input is over 98% lower than its predecessor; 1 million token context window
  • It matches or beats Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash on several audio and video benchmarks

Every few weeks a launch lands that quietly changes what normal developers in India can afford to build. Alibaba's new Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash is one of those. It's the newest model in the Qwen family — a native omnimodal model, meaning it processes audio, video and text natively rather than bolting separate models together — and the headline numbers are hard to ignore, especially if you've ever priced a voice assistant project and quietly closed the tab.

01.What Alibaba actually launched

Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash is built for workflows that live on audio and video: real-time conversation, audiovisual summarisation, video editing, film production work, and agent tasks that need to watch and listen. The model supports a context window of 1 million tokens, so it can chew through long recordings in a single session. Alibaba also improved the surrounding plumbing — the Qwen-MM plugins now handle on-demand perception and tool use for long audio and video, and the company open-sourced a Qwen-Live Harness for building real-time voice applications on top of the model's API.

Across 29 benchmarks, the average score is more than 25% higher than the previous Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus, while text performance stays at the level you'd expect from a same-size text-only model.

02.The part that made me sit up: 74 languages, including Hindi

Here's the thing nobody's talking about enough. Speech recognition covers 74 languages, and the list reads like a map of India: Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Punjabi, Malayalam, Odia, Urdu. Speech generation supports 29 languages, including Hindi and Urdu. For a country where the next few hundred million internet users think voice-first in their own language, this matters more than any leaderboard position.

And the benchmark positions aren't bad either. Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash scored 71.0 on WildClawBench-MM (up a huge 36.5 points from its predecessor) and 69.6 on UniClawBench for agent coding and long-horizon tasks. On LongAudioSpan, the benchmark for understanding long audio, it hit 82.7. In Alibaba's own testing it surpassed Gemini 3.8 Flash in multiple benchmark tests, with overall audio performance ahead — video performance lands at a comparable level. Take vendor benchmarks with the usual pinch of salt, but the direction is clear: the open Qwen line is now genuinely competing with Google's best voice models.

03.The 98% price cut

Now the number that changes business plans. The API cost per hour for voice input is over 98% lower than the previous Omni-Plus model, and voice-plus-video input is over 93% lower. For context, Google's newly launched Gemini 3.8 Live — which we covered when it landed — lists developer pricing around $0.005 per minute for audio input and $0.018 per minute for audio output. Voice AI at scale has been genuinely expensive, and that's exactly why so many Indian voice-app ideas die at the spreadsheet stage. A 98% cost drop doesn't just save money; it makes previously impossible products viable — vernacular customer support lines, voice-first education apps, meeting transcription for Indian offices. 04.Why this matters for India

Think about what a ₹500-per-month budget could power a year ago versus today. A student in Lucknow can build a Hindi voice tutor. A Jaipur D2C brand can offer voice ordering in Hindi and Urdu. A clinic in Kochi can transcribe patient conversations in Malayalam. The model is available through Alibaba's Qwen platform, and Qwen's open-weight lineage has made it a favourite among Indian developers who want strong performance without Western API pricing.

Honestly, what surprised me most is how little fanfare accompanied a launch this significant. Most coverage went to the US frontier labs' safety drama this week — understandable, but a 98% cost cut on multilingual voice AI is arguably more consequential for builders outside San Francisco.

Key Takeaways

  • Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash is Alibaba's new native omnimodal model with a 1 million token context window
  • Speech recognition in 74 languages (Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu and more); speech generation in 29, including Hindi
  • Voice input API costs over 98% lower than Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus; video-plus-voice input over 93% lower
  • Surpasses Gemini 3.8 Flash in multiple audio benchmarks; comparable on video
  • Qwen-Live Harness is open-sourced, and Qwen-MM plugins now support long audio/video agent workflows
