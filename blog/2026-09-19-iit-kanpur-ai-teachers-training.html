IIT Kanpur AI Training for 900 UP School Teachers: What's Actually Happening

#AIinEducation#IITKanpur#TeacherTraining#UttarPradesh

TL;DR

  • IIT Kanpur, the UP government and SCERT will train 900 school teachers, DIET lecturers and SCERT officials in AI, coding and digital teaching
  • It's an eight-week blended programme — five days residential at IIT Kanpur, then online classes through Google Classroom
  • The syllabus starts from zero (operating systems, MS Office) and builds up to Python, Scratch, AI tools and even a robotics challenge
  • The same curriculum is already inside SCERT's Class 6-8 textbooks, reaching an estimated 48 lakh students across UP

Here's a piece of education news that deserves more attention than it's getting. IIT Kanpur has tied up with the Government of Uttar Pradesh and the State Council of Educational Research and Training (SCERT) to train 900 participants in digital literacy, computational thinking, AI and technology-enabled teaching. The IIT Kanpur AI training programme, called DLCCAI (Digital Literacy and Computational Thinking Concepts for AI), is expanding from its earlier avatar into secondary education — and the details are more interesting than the headline suggests.

01.Who is actually getting trained?

The 900 seats aren't all going to one type of person. The batch mix is smart: secondary school teachers, lecturers from District Institutes of Education and Training (DIETs), and officials from SCERT's ancillary units. That last group matters more than people realise — when the people who design teacher training themselves understand AI, the effect multiplies down the chain. The programme runs in six batches of 150 participants each, so it's a proper production line, not a one-off workshop.

02.Eight weeks, and it starts from absolute zero

This is the part I respect most. The programme is explicitly designed for participants with limited exposure to computers, coding or AI. The opening five-day residential training at IIT Kanpur covers digital foundations, productivity tools, spreadsheets, creativity and AI, block-based programming and introductory Python. After that, the rest of the eight weeks happen online through Google Classroom, with weekly mentor sessions and live doubt-clearing sessions.

There's a proper finish line too. Complete the quizzes, assignments, projects and a final capstone presentation, and you get a joint certificate from SCERT and IIT Kanpur. For a government school teacher in, say, Barabanki or Ballia, that certificate carries real weight — it's from one of the original IITs.

03.The syllabus is built around real school work

Instead of abstract theory, teachers learn through tasks pulled from their actual week: preparing notices and lesson plans, creating marksheets and presentations, conducting assessments, and building digital teaching resources. Along the way they cover MS Office, Google Workspace, digital communication, cybersecurity, digital citizenship, Scratch, basic Python, and AI and EdTech tools. There's even robot assembly and sensor-based activities, ending in a robotics challenge.

Think about that for a second. A government school teacher in UP assembling a robot and learning Python as part of official training. Prof Ramkumar J, who coordinates the programme at IIT Kanpur, put it plainly: coding, computational thinking and AI are no longer the preserve of engineering colleges — they belong in secondary schools today.

04.The 48 lakh student connection

Here's the thing nobody is talking about: this teacher training is the visible half of a much bigger change. The DLCCAI curriculum is aligned with NEP 2020, and it has already been embedded into SCERT's Class 6-8 textbooks — reaching an estimated 48 lakh students across Uttar Pradesh. So the teachers being trained now are the ones who will deliver AI-adjacent content that's already sitting in lakhs of school bags. The textbook came first; the teacher preparation is now catching up.

It also fits a pattern we've covered before. The PM recently announced a plan to skill one crore Indians in AI (we broke that down here), and IIT Madras opened its own AI studio for students earlier this month. State-level programmes like this one are where those national ambitions actually touch classrooms.

05.Why this matters beyond UP

Uttar Pradesh has more school students than most countries have people. If AI literacy works here, at this scale, with teachers who started with limited computer exposure, it can work anywhere. And the blended model — short residential burst plus weeks of mentored online learning — is exactly the format other states can copy without massive budgets. Honestly, what surprised me most is how practical the whole thing sounds. No moonshot promises, just spreadsheets, marksheets, Python and robots.

One caution though: teacher training programmes live or die on follow-up. Eight weeks builds a foundation, but AI tools change every few months. The teachers who keep learning after the certificate ceremony are the ones who'll actually transform their classrooms — and honestly, learning to direct AI tools well is now as basic a teaching skill as lesson planning.
