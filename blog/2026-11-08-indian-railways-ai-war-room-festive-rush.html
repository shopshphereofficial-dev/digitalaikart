Inside Indian Railways' AI War Room for the Festive Rush

#AIIndia#IndianRailways#AI#PublicSafety

TL;DR

  • Railway Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw reviewed the War Room at Rail Bhawan in New Delhi on 7 November 2026, where live CCTV feeds from major stations are analysed with AI for crowd movement and incidents.
  • Around 700 AI-enabled cameras and more than 18,000 special trains are part of this year's festive arrangements, against roughly 12,000 special trains last year.
  • Reporters were given different station counts, 28 by The Indian Express and 38 by The New Indian Express quoting the minister, and no data retention policy has been published.
  • Railway officials told reporters that AI improves response times but cannot add platform space, which is why holding areas and station redevelopment still carry the load.

01.A war room that watches the platforms

On Saturday, 7 November 2026, Railway Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw reviewed the Railway War Room at Rail Bhawan in New Delhi. It is a centralised monitoring and coordination facility: live CCTV feeds from major stations sit on screen, covering platforms, foot overbridges and the area around the station, and officials can zoom into a single camera feed to look closely at one spot. Alongside the video, AI-based systems help staff analyse activity and incidents at different locations so they can respond while a situation is still small, as reported by The Indian Express.

The idea is not new on Indian Railways. The national carrier has been piloting AI-based surveillance at New Delhi and Varanasi since 2021, using computer vision to flag overcrowding and alert officials. What has changed this season is scale. The war room now covers a few dozen stations and roughly 700 AI-enabled cameras, according to The Indian Express, with the Press Information Bureau recording on 6 November that 700 additional AI cameras had been installed to strengthen surveillance and security.

02.What the software is actually doing

Three jobs stand out. The first is situational awareness: feeds are analysed for incidents and for the way a crowd is moving, and officials can ask the system about a specific location instead of scrubbing through screens. The New Indian Express reported that AI-enabled tools let officials analyse activity at a particular location through prompts, assess how a situation is developing, and act in time.

The second is forecasting. TV9 Hindi reported an AI-based safety application that estimates how many passengers are on a platform and how much the crowd is likely to grow in the next 30 to 60 minutes, then alerts railway staff so they can intervene before a crush builds. That is the most useful part of the whole system, because a crowd that is detected early can be diverted to a holding area or a different entry gate.

The third is operations. The war room tracks train movements and delays in real time, which lets officials connect a late train to the crowd building up on a platform for it and adjust resources accordingly.

03.The numbers behind this festive season

More than 18,000 special trains are running this festive season, against around 12,000 last year, the minister said. Dedicated passenger holding areas have been set up at about 20 stations, with separate ticket counters and multiple scanning facilities to keep queues moving. Vaishnaw said preparations began nearly six months in advance, with particular attention to the Chhath Puja surge towards Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh.

At New Delhi station alone, he said daily footfalls during the Chhath and Diwali period multiplied several times to around eight lakh, according to The New Indian Express.

One detail worth flagging: reports do not agree on how many stations the war room watches. The Indian Express put it at 28, listing New Delhi, Anand Vihar Terminal, Hazrat Nizamuddin, Secunderabad, SMVT Bengaluru, MGR Chennai Central, Mumbai Central, CSMT Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Surat, Pune, Varanasi, Gorakhpur, Sealdah, Howrah, Jaipur, Nagpur and Vijayawada among others. The New Indian Express, quoting the minister the next day, said 38. Until the Railways publishes its own figure, treat the exact count as unconfirmed.

04.What it means if you are travelling this week

Practically, the system changes how you will experience a station rather than what you need to do. If a holding area is in use, you will wait there until your train is called rather than on the platform, so budget extra time and keep your ticket and identity proof handy for the scanning points.

Check the National Train Enquiry System before you leave for the station. Because the war room correlates delays with crowd build-up, a train that is running late may mean a longer wait outside the platform, and the announcement pattern may differ from a normal day.

The cameras exist to spot a surge early, which only helps if passengers follow directions from Railway Protection Force and station staff when a platform is being cleared or an entry gate is closed. If you are travelling with children or elderly relatives, keep the group together at the holding area and ask staff for assistance rather than joining a queue that is already dense.

05.The limits, and the privacy question

The honest caveat came from railway officials themselves. One official, speaking to Inside AI on condition of anonymity, put it plainly: AI can help manage crowds, but it cannot create more space, and the long-term fix is station redevelopment. Another, from the Centre for Railway Information Systems, told the same outlet that AI surveillance has to come with clear data protection safeguards. Inside AI noted it could not independently verify the exact capabilities of the systems or their data retention policies.

That last point is the one to watch. Around 700 cameras watching platforms during the year's busiest weeks is a real expansion of surveillance in public spaces, and Indian Railways has not published a retention or access policy that reporters could check. The system's value is measurable, faster detection and better coordination, but so is what it collects.

This post draws on reporting by The Indian Express, The New Indian Express, the Press Information Bureau, Inside AI and TV9 Hindi.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Indian Railways War Room?
It is a centralised monitoring and coordination facility at Rail Bhawan in New Delhi where officials watch live CCTV feeds from major stations, supported by AI-based analysis of crowd movement, incidents and train delays. Railway Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw reviewed it on 7 November 2026 during the Diwali and Chhath Puja rush.

How many AI cameras has Indian Railways installed?
Around 700 AI-enabled cameras are being used for festive monitoring, according to The Indian Express, and the Press Information Bureau said on 6 November that 700 additional AI cameras had been installed to strengthen surveillance and security. The two figures are described slightly differently in the official record, so the precise total in service is not clear.

Does the AI system replace railway staff?
No. The AI flags crowd build-up, incidents and delays, and staff at the war room and at stations act on those alerts. Railway officials quoted by Inside AI said the technology improves response times, but that space constraints at major stations still need long-term solutions such as station redevelopment.
