Hostinger Agent: AI That Now Runs Your Website

#Hostinger#WebsiteBuilder#AITools#AIAgents

TL;DR

  • Hostinger has retired Kodee, its support chatbot, and replaced it with Hostinger Agent — an AI that can actually edit and manage your site
  • Kodee was already resolving 91% of ~1.5 million monthly support conversations on its own
  • The new agent handles DNS records, domain management, migrations, SEO and even builds websites from chat
  • Support stays free; deeper agentic work (marketing, SEO, analytics) comes through paid tiers

Quick question: what's the difference between a chatbot that answers hosting tickets and an AI agent that manages your live website? This week, that stopped being theoretical. Hostinger Agent is now live across the company's platforms for its 5 million-plus clients — and if you've ever built a site on a budget in India, odds are decent you've got a Hostinger tab open somewhere.

The shift is bigger than a rebrand. Kodee, the old assistant, could tell you how to fix a DNS issue. The new agent can go fix it — while you watch, or while you make chai. It's one of the clearest examples yet of "computer use" AI reaching a mass-market product, the same capability shift we explained in our GPT-6 Astra breakdown — AI that operates software instead of just describing it.

01.What is the Hostinger Agent?

Hostinger Agent merges the old hPanel support assistant (Kodee) and the company's specialized business agents into one unified assistant you talk to like a colleague. It draws on context you already have in your account — your website, store, domain, business email, newsletters and past conversations — so you don't have to re-explain your setup every time.

It's also built directly into AI Builder, Hostinger's environment for creating websites, stores and apps. That means you can be building a site and consulting the agent in the same window: ask it to change the homepage, suggest SEO improvements, plan a launch, or investigate why something broke. Under the hood, the whole thing runs on the Model Context Protocol (MCP) — the same standard Google opened up for smart homes this week, which tells you where the industry is heading.

02.What it can actually do

  • Website changes: edit pages, update content, apply changes to a live site just by describing them in chat
  • Build from scratch: describe a site and it builds it in the background, then messages you a link when it's ready
  • Technical work: add, update or delete DNS records, manage domains and subdomains, restart a VPS, start migrations
  • Business tasks: specialized agents for marketing, SEO, content and visuals, analytics, and recurring scheduled jobs
  • WordPress management: run and maintain a WordPress site by chatting with the agent in the admin panel

Read that list again with a freelancer's eyes. Half of it is work that used to go to a part-time web admin — the person you paid a few thousand rupees a month to update plugins and fix contact forms.

03.The numbers behind the switch

Kodee was no slouch — it resolved 91% of roughly 1.5 million monthly support conversations without a human. But answering a billing question and editing a live website are very different levels of risk, and Hostinger knows it. The company created a role called AI CX Engineer: human specialists who can step into an ongoing AI conversation and add context, instead of making the customer start over from scratch.

After changes introduced at the end of June, the results were striking. Specialist-guided AI resolution jumped from 41% to 72%. Live chat demand fell from 10% to 4.5% of requests. And the average specialist-guided resolution now takes about three minutes, versus more than fifty minutes for the old live chat route.

Here's the thing nobody's talking about: those AI CX Engineers are the actual template for the jobs replacing traditional support roles. Humans aren't being removed from the loop — they're being repositioned as supervisors who parachute into AI conversations when the machine hesitates. If you work in customer support or plan to, that's the job description coming your way.

04.Free versus paid: what costs money

Support and basic account management stay included with your hosting at no extra cost. The deeper agentic capabilities — marketing automation, SEO tools, analytics, recurring tasks — are sold through tiered subscriptions. So the agent is both a cost-saving move for Hostinger and a new revenue line on top of hosting itself. Expect every other big host to copy this within a year; agent interfaces are quietly replacing the dashboard across the whole industry.

One honest caveat, though. A fast resolution isn't the same as a correct one. For tricky stuff — DNS misconfigurations, security incidents, complex migrations — a confident wrong answer from an agent can do more damage than a slow right one. Use the agent for the routine 80%, and think twice before handing it anything that would hurt if it broke.

Key Takeaways

  • Hostinger Agent replaces Kodee and can edit live sites, manage DNS, run migrations and build websites from chat
  • Kodee previously resolved 91% of ~1.5M monthly support conversations autonomously
  • Human "AI CX Engineers" now step into AI conversations — specialist-guided resolution rose from 41% to 72%
  • Support remains free; specialized marketing, SEO and analytics agents sit behind paid tiers
  • The agent runs on MCP — the same open protocol standard Google adopted for smart home agents this week
