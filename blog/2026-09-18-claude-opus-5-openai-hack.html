Claude Opus 5 Hacked OpenAI: Full Story Explained

TL;DR

  • Security startup Hacktron AI used Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 to breach OpenAI — and won a $6,500 (about ₹5.4 lakh) bug bounty
  • The whole chain, from image file to internal code access, took under 72 hours, with "just a few hours of human time"
  • Two bugs did the damage: a libheif image-parsing flaw and an OpenAI single sign-on weakness
  • The researchers say the whole HEIF Heist project cost less than $3,000 (roughly ₹2.5 lakh) in tokens

Here's a story that sounds like a movie plot but is completely real: a small security startup used one AI lab's model to hack a rival AI lab, got paid by the victim for their trouble, and published the whole thing this week. Claude Opus 5 hacked OpenAI — with human researchers steering, but the AI doing a serious chunk of the exploit work. The Wall Street Journal reported it first, and Forbes, VentureBeat and Business Insider all followed this morning.

01.What actually happened?

On July 25, 2026, researchers at Hacktron AI — a San Francisco-based AI cybersecurity startup — chained two vulnerabilities to compromise ChatGPT and Codex accounts, including one belonging to an OpenAI employee. That employee's Codex was connected to OpenAI's GitHub organization, which meant a path into the company's internal code. To prove the access was real without actually reading anything sensitive, the researchers instructed Codex to open a harmless pull request in OpenAI's internal monorepo. Then they stopped, reported everything, and OpenAI fixed the SSO flaw within roughly 14 hours of the submission.

The team disclosed the operation publicly this week, and OpenAI paid them a $6,500 bounty — around ₹5.4 lakh — through its Bugcrowd program.

02.How the Claude Opus 5 attack worked

The entry point is almost boring: an image upload. OpenAI's community forum runs on Discourse, and HEIC/HEIF image files there get passed to ImageMagick for conversion, which exposes the underlying libheif parser. That parser had a heap buffer overflow. Upload one malicious image and you could get remote code execution on the forum's server.

The second bug is the one that should keep security teams up at night. OpenAI's single sign-on had a flaw that turned control of the forum into access to the ChatGPT and Codex accounts of people who had signed in through it — including employees. Forum compromise plus SSO weakness equals account takeover, and those accounts were connected to GitHub, Slack, Outlook, Gmail and more.

Where does Claude come in? The Hacktron team had access to a special version of Claude through Anthropic's Cyber Verification Program, which relaxes certain cyber restrictions for qualified security practitioners. Claude Opus 4.8 found the libheif vulnerability and built a partial exploit but, in the researchers' words, "struggled across several sessions" to make it reliable. Then Anthropic released Opus 5 that very evening. The new model produced a working ARM64 exploit within about three hours, then ported it to the x86-64 environment the forum actually used. From first probe to confirmed access: less than 72 hours.

03.The quote that should worry everyone

In their conclusion, the researchers wrote something I haven't been able to stop thinking about: "Software has long benefited from a kind of security through complexity... AI is removing that protection by turning more of this scarce expertise into compute. Work that once required a well-resourced team and months of effort can now be compressed into days... Security assumptions must catch up with attacker capabilities."

Honestly, what surprised me most wasn't that AI could write an exploit. It was the economics. The OpenAI hack took "a few days for an agent, and just a few hours of human time." The broader HEIF Heist project — which found similar issues across Slack, Zoom, Meta, GitHub Enterprise and Next.js — took two months, three researchers, and less than $3,000 (roughly ₹2.5 lakh) in tokens. That's less than what many Indian companies spend on a single training workshop.

04.Why this matters for India

For anyone building a career in cybersecurity in India, this is a loud signal. Bug bounties are real money — that ₹5.4 lakh payout came from one properly reported chain — and AI agents are rapidly becoming the standard toolkit for serious vulnerability research. The skill that pays now is knowing how to direct these models: scoping targets, verifying findings, and writing reports that companies act on. The pure memorization part of security work is getting automated; the judgment part is not.

It also matters for every Indian business running forums, upload features or SSO. If OpenAI can be breached through an image parser and a login flaw, so can a fintech app in Bengaluru or a D2C store in Surat. Patch your dependencies, sandbox your image processing, and limit what a compromised account can reach.
