OpenAI Brings Free ChatGPT Plus to Indian Researchers for 12 Months

#OpenAI#ChatGPTPlus#IndiaResearch#AIinEducation

TL;DR

  • OpenAI has opened its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers programme in India from 17 November 2026.
  • Each grant gives three free ChatGPT Plus seats for 12 months to a researcher and up to two collaborators.
  • Applicants must be faculty or postdocs with a paper on arXiv, bioRxiv or ChemRxiv in the last three years.
  • Applications are first-come, first-served, so eligible researchers should apply as soon as the window opens.

01.What OpenAI announced in India

OpenAI said on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, that its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers programme is now available in India. Under the programme, eligible research faculty and postdoctoral researchers at selected institutions can apply for free access to ChatGPT Plus for 12 months, in the fields of science, mathematics and engineering. The India launch is part of a global round in which the company is offering 50,000 new grants to researchers on a first-come, first-served basis.

Each grant is not a single login. It provides three ChatGPT Plus seats inside a dedicated research workspace, covering the recipient and up to two collaborators. OpenAI said the aim of the programme overall is to give free ChatGPT access to as many as two lakh researchers worldwide, which is double the target it announced when the pilot began in July this year.

02.Who can actually apply

The programme is aimed at people already doing research, not at everyone who is curious about AI. To be eligible, an applicant has to be a research faculty member or a postdoctoral researcher affiliated with an eligible academic institution in a supported country. They also need to have published a paper in an eligible field on arXiv, bioRxiv or ChemRxiv within the past three years, verify their institutional affiliation, and describe their research and how they plan to use ChatGPT.

OpenAI's programme page lists the eligible fields as biological sciences, chemistry and materials, computer science, earth and planetary sciences, engineering, mathematics and physics. The collaborator seats are more flexible: they can go to research students at the same institution. So an approved faculty member can bring up to two students or junior colleagues into the sponsored workspace, but a student cannot apply for a grant on their own.

03.Why India is in the first wave

OpenAI said Indian institutions showed unusually strong demand during the pilot. Raghav Gupta, Head of Education for India and Asia Pacific at OpenAI, said in the company's statement that since the pilot launched a few months ago, Indian institutions have demonstrated one of the highest levels of interest in accessing the programme. That demand is the stated reason India is part of this round rather than a later one.

The announcement also builds on OpenAI's earlier education work in the country. The company pointed to its OpenAI Learning Accelerator, which combined AI access, training and research, and to a research collaboration with IIT Madras backed by 500,000 US dollars to study how AI can support learning and teaching.

04.What it changes for students and small labs

For a research group in a state university or a small college with no institutional AI licence, three sponsored seats are a practical gain. OpenAI says researchers can use ChatGPT to work through scientific literature, develop and test hypotheses, analyse data, write and debug research code, and prepare grant applications and manuscripts. The programme also includes training and hands-on support, which matters as much as the seats themselves for researchers who have not built AI tools into their daily work before.

Data handling is the other point worth noting for Indian labs. OpenAI said that by default it does not use data shared through the programme to train its models. For researchers working on unpublished results, or on projects that need an institutional ethics committee's approval, that default is usually the first question asked, and it is now answered in writing on the programme's terms.

05.The catches to plan around

There is a date mismatch worth checking before anyone blocks time for an application. Indian reports of the announcement, based on OpenAI's India statement, said applications open on 30 November. OpenAI's own global post published on 16 November said applications open on Monday, 23 November. Researchers should confirm the date on the official programme page rather than rely on either report, since the window is first-come, first-served and closes once 50,000 eligible applications are in.

The other changes are in the details. OpenAI has moved away from the lottery system used in the summer pilot, so speed now matters more than luck. The pilot gave researchers ChatGPT Pro, while the new grants give Plus, because the company found most pilot users stayed within Plus usage limits. Access runs for 12 months, collaborators who join later get only the remaining period, and meeting the eligibility criteria does not guarantee approval. It is also access rather than funding: no cash, and no compute for training models.

Frequently asked questions

Can undergraduate or masters students apply for the free ChatGPT Plus grant?
No. The applicant must be research faculty or a postdoctoral researcher at an eligible institution with a recent paper on arXiv, bioRxiv or ChemRxiv. Students can still get access if an approved researcher adds them as one of the two collaborators.

Does OpenAI use my research data to train its models?
OpenAI says that by default it does not use data shared through the academic researchers programme to train its models. Institutional terms in your order form may add conditions, so check what your college has signed.

When do applications open and how long does the access last?
OpenAI's India announcement said 30 November 2026, while its global post mentioned 23 November, so confirm on the official programme page. Approved grants run for 12 months from when the account is set up.
