Odisha Just Got the Green Light for an AI Centre of Excellence in Bhubaneswar

TL;DR

  • MeitY has given in-principle approval to an AI Centre of Excellence (AI-CoE) in Bhubaneswar, to be implemented by STPI under the IndiaAI Mission
  • Hyderabad-based Blue Cloud Softech Solutions comes in as industry partner with a ₹4 crore contribution, plus mentorship and startup support
  • Focus areas go beyond the usual: healthcare, governance, disaster management, education — and coastal fisheries and the ocean economy
  • Startups incubated at the centre get access to Blue Cloud's AI platforms, engineers and its enterprise and government customer network

When we talk about AI in India, the conversation somehow always drifts to Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune. So it's refreshing to write about a story where the action is in Bhubaneswar — and where the government is actually putting structure, money and industry partners behind it.

01.What just got approved

On September 15, the proposed Centre of Excellence in Artificial Intelligence (AI-CoE) in Bhubaneswar received in-principle approval from the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY). The Software Technology Parks of India (STPI), an autonomous society under MeitY, will implement the centre as part of the IndiaAI Mission. STPI has also formally confirmed Hyderabad-based Blue Cloud Softech Solutions as the industry partner and asked the company to start executing a definitive memorandum of understanding.

02.What the industry partner is bringing

Under the proposed MoU, Blue Cloud will contribute ₹4 crore towards developing the centre — and, just as importantly, bring industry leadership, mentorship and strategic support for the startups incubated there. The company says it will make its AI platforms, engineering talent and its enterprise and government customer network available to those startups.

Group chairman Tejesh Kumar Kodali was blunt about why the location matters: "Odisha is exactly the kind of place where applied AI should be built. There are real problems to solve in healthcare, disaster management, coastal fisheries and the ocean economy, and a state government and STPI that want solutions deployed, not merely researched."

That last phrase stuck with me. "Deployed, not merely researched" is the gap that kills most Indian deep-tech good intentions.

03.Why Odisha's focus areas are actually different

Here's the thing nobody's talking about: this isn't a copy-paste tech park. The centre's initial application areas include healthcare, governance, disaster management, education and industrial use cases — but its priority areas lean on the state's own strengths: oceanography, coastal fisheries, tidal and ocean energy, and women and child development.

Think about that for a second. Cyclone prediction and disaster response for the Odisha coast, AI for fishing communities, tools for the ocean energy sector — these are problems Bengal Valley investors rarely think about, but they affect millions of people. A centre dedicated to them could produce genuinely original work instead of the twentieth food-delivery recommendation engine.

04.What's in Blue Cloud's toolbox

For context on what startups there can tap into: Blue Cloud's existing portfolio includes BluHealth, a camera-based, non-invasive vital-sign screening platform for healthcare; Blura SAGA and SOCEYE for public safety and governance applications; and Access Genie, its sovereign AI and 5G digital infrastructure stack. Managing director and group CEO Vinod Babu Bollikonda said the company will move quickly to execute the MoU with STPI so the centre can begin operations, and wants Bhubaneswar to become "a reference point for how industry, government and startups can build responsible AI together."

05.The bigger picture

This fits into a broader pattern we've been tracking: the IndiaAI Mission quietly building AI infrastructure beyond the metros — data labs, GPU access, and now applied centres of excellence. For a state like Odisha, a CoE is a magnet effect: it gives local students and founders a reason to build where they are, and gives the ecosystem compute, mentors and government pilots in one place. The ₹4 crore is the headline number, but the institutional pipeline matters more.

Key Takeaways

  • MeitY gave in-principle approval to an AI Centre of Excellence in Bhubaneswar, implemented by STPI under the IndiaAI Mission
  • Blue Cloud Softech Solutions joins as industry partner with a ₹4 crore contribution plus platforms, engineers and mentorship

What This Means for You

A government-backed AI centre with real industry partners means local pilots, local mentors and local customers. If you're anywhere in India, the pattern is clear: applied AI for real problems — health, disasters, fisheries, governance — is where the support and the money are flowing.
