Pocket FM Sherpa: AI That Writes 100-Episode Stories

#PocketFM#AIFiction#AudioSeries#IndianAI

TL;DR

  • Bengaluru's Pocket FM launched Sherpa, an AI fiction-writing platform that keeps stories consistent across hundreds of episodes
  • It uses a squad of specialised agents plus a "Narrative World Model" that remembers every character, secret and plot thread
  • Content production on the platform grew 75x to 2.5+ million hours a year, and ARR doubled from $250 million to $500 million
  • Sherpa is free for writers — and 10% of eligible Pocket FM writers earn over $200,000 a year

If you've ever tried to get ChatGPT to remember what happened in chapter 4 of your novel, you'll immediately get why Pocket FM Sherpa is interesting. On Saturday, Bengaluru-based Pocket FM pulled its AI fiction-writing platform out of the lab and made it public. The pitch is simple: generic AI can write decent sentences, but it cannot keep a story straight across two hundred episodes. Sherpa was built to do exactly that — and the numbers behind it are worth your attention whether you're a writer, a founder, or just someone who watches the Indian startup scene.

01.The problem: AI writes sentences, not stories

Long-form fiction is brutal. Characters have to evolve without losing who they are. A conflict introduced in episode 12 has to stay unresolved until episode 90 — and the story has to remember it. Ask any LLM to do this and it starts hallucinating its own plot within a few chapters, because it treats every episode as an isolated piece of text. Pocket FM's co-founder Prateek Dixit put it well in the launch: "The next breakthrough in generative AI for entertainment isn't simply going to come from models that can write better sentences. It will come from systems that understand stories." That one line is basically the whole product thesis.

02.Inside Sherpa: a team of AI agents, not one chatbot

Sherpa's answer is what the company calls Narrative Intelligence. Instead of one model responding to prompts, it runs a squad of specialised agents across the fiction pipeline: an Ideation Agent that develops story concepts, a Planning Agent that works on structure and sequences, a Research Agent that brings in factual context, a prose agent that writes scenes, and a Feedback Agent that evaluates story quality and suggests revisions.

The heart of the system is the Narrative World Model — a long-term story memory that grows as the story develops. It maintains a living picture of characters, relationships, conflicts and events, so something that happened 50 or 100 episodes ago still shapes what happens next. CEO Rohan Nayak described it as an evolving knowledge graph of characters, secrets and plot dependencies. The creator stays in control throughout: you set the vision, make the big calls, and Sherpa handles the scaffolding. If you're new to how multi-agent AI systems like this actually work, we explained the building blocks in our GPT-6 Astra beginner's guide.

03.The numbers are genuinely wild

Here's where it stops sounding like a demo. Pocket FM says annualised content production has grown 75x to more than 2.5 million hours, with a 10x increase in blockbuster titles. The platform now has over 770,000 titles, more than 550,000 creators, and — this is the moat — 5.5 billion hours of playtime data with minute-by-minute engagement and retention signals, plus 1.4 million hours of text and voice content. Every story that flops or hooks teaches the models something about pacing and story structure.

The business results followed. Nayak says content production rose 1,200% in a year, and Pocket FM's annual recurring revenue doubled from $250 million to $500 million in the same period. The company is also targeting an EBITDA margin of 15-20%, up from around 5%, with AI already powering about 90% of its production. Next stop: Japan and South Korea in the next six to eight months.

04.Writers are actually making money

This is the part I didn't expect. According to Nayak, 10% of eligible writers on Pocket FM earn more than $200,000 a year, and three writers have become millionaires in under two years. One property on the platform has generated over $100 million in revenue. Early Sherpa user Ajit Kumar, author of Brahmyodha: The Destroyer, has a story with 275.2 million plays as of August 2026. His take on the tool is refreshingly grounded: "It does not take the place of the writer; it helps the writer keep going."

Vasu Sharma, Pocket FM's Head of AI, frames Sherpa as giving a single creator what previously took entire studios, specialised teams and production budgets. If that sounds familiar, it's the same one-person-studio logic driving AI video and biopic experiments — we covered India's first AI-assisted OTT biopic when it dropped earlier this week.

05.Why I'm paying attention

Honestly, what surprised me most isn't the tech — it's that an Indian startup built a defensible AI product on top of data no frontier lab has. OpenAI and Anthropic can generate prose, but they don't have billions of hours of audience retention data telling them where listeners lean in and where they drop off. Pocket FM turned that into a flywheel: more stories produce richer signals, which improve the models, which help creators write better stories. And Sherpa is currently free for writers, which tells you they see it as an ecosystem play, not a revenue line.

The company's stated ambition is "a billion creators creating a billion stories for a billion people." Big talk, but the direction is real. For anyone in India sitting on creative ambition — especially in languages that global platforms ignore — the cost of building a fictional universe just collapsed.

One caution, though: tools like Sherpa reward people who understand how to direct AI well — clear vision, good prompts, tight feedback loops.
