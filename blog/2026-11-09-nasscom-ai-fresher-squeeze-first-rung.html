Nasscom's Fresher Warning: 1.35 Lakh Jobs, 1.5 Million Graduates

#AIJobs#Nasscom#IndiaAI#TechCareers

TL;DR

  • Nasscom president Rajesh Nambiar told Nikkei Asia on 6 November 2026 that fresh graduates could struggle to find tech jobs as AI takes over entry-level coding and software testing.
  • Nasscom's own review projects about 6 million direct tech jobs in FY26 with a net addition of just 1,35,000, against roughly 2.6 million engineering enrolments and about 1.5 million computer science graduates a year.
  • Nasscom is co-designing a joint AI undergraduate curriculum with the UGC and expects approval in about six months, which is slower than this year's placement season.
  • For students the practical signal is that employers screen for evidence of working with AI tools, not for how fast you write boilerplate code.

01.The industry's own lobby body said it plainly

On 6 November 2026, Nikkei Asia published an interview with Rajesh Nambiar, president of Nasscom, in which he said recent college graduates could struggle to land technology jobs because companies are automating entry-level coding and software testing with AI. Those are the tasks that absorbed lakhs of engineering graduates into their first jobs at firms like TCS and Infosys. "From a fresher's point of view, it is a concern, and I will not sugarcoat it any differently," he said.

What gives the warning weight is who is saying it: Nasscom is the industry body for the companies that do the hiring, so this is not an outside critic predicting doom. "If our education system is unable to make that transition very quickly, students will come out with skill sets which may be very obsolete already," he said, adding that "the level of transformations required in the curriculum has never been this significant." He called the present phase a period of rebalancing, and named cybersecurity, AI consulting and AI implementation as the areas firms must move into.

02.The arithmetic: 1.35 lakh jobs against 1.5 million graduates

Nasscom's Strategic Review 2026 projects direct tech employment of about 6 million in FY26, but a net addition of only 1,35,000, a rise of 2.3 per cent and barely 2,000 more than the 1,33,000 added in FY25. The review expects the sector to cross 315 billion dollars in revenue and notes the shift from billing by headcount to billing for outcomes. Supply keeps growing: India had 5,903 engineering and technology institutions with enrolments close to 2.6 million in the year ending March 2026, and about 1.5 million computer science graduates enter the market every year, per Informeia's account of the interview.

The old equation was simple: win a large contract, hire thousands of freshers, bill for the people deployed. AI has broken the link between revenue and headcount. TCS reported 0.5 per cent sequential revenue growth in the July to September quarter while annualised AI revenue rose 19 per cent to 3.1 billion dollars, and Infosys grew AI revenue 29 per cent quarter on quarter while trimming its FY27 revenue growth forecast to 1 to 2 per cent. AI work is growing quickly, but it is not creating fresher jobs at the old rate.

03.Why the bottom rung is the one AI takes first

The tasks AI handles best are the ones we used to hand to beginners: standard code, test cases, a summarised document, a routine reply. Those tasks paid a junior a salary and also taught the job. A payroll study by Stanford economists led by Erik Brynjolfsson, summarised by Indian Masterminds, found employment of workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed occupations fell 16 per cent relative to other groups, while employment for workers over 30 kept rising. Keep seniors to review AI output and stop hiring juniors, and the pipeline that produces tomorrow's seniors thins from the bottom.

The stakes are larger in India than the exposure numbers suggest. IT services and entry-level cognitive work built the post-liberalisation middle class, and the country has the world's largest youth population to absorb. NITI Aayog's Roadmap for Job Creation in the AI Economy puts both outcomes on the table: about 1.5 million tech jobs lost by 2031, or up to 4 million created, depending on what government, industry and colleges do now. India Today, reporting the same interview, cited estimates that unemployed graduates rose nearly fourfold to about 11 million between 2004 and 2023.

04.Six months for a curriculum, and what to do before it lands

Nasscom is co-designing a joint AI undergraduate curriculum with the University Grants Commission and other agencies, and Nambiar expects approval in about six months. A curriculum approved in six months still has to reach classrooms. Some groundwork exists: the government has begun teaching AI in schools and says 1.3 million people have completed online courses in AI, data analytics and cybersecurity, as reported by CIO Elets. A BCG assessment cited there found AI could reshape 200 billion dollars of IT services work by 2030, and that redeployment depends on retraining people for applied AI.

For a student the reading is narrower. Employers screen for evidence that you can work with AI tools, not for how quickly you write boilerplate. A TCS enterprise AI study found only 10 per cent of firms let AI agents act on their own, so demand is for people who can design, check and run these systems. New entry-level work is appearing in checking AI output and curating training data, and the Economic Survey 2025-26, as summarised by Indian Masterminds, notes that startups curating training data have not yet appeared at scale in India.

05.What to watch, and what not to over-read

Three things will show whether the squeeze narrows: the UGC-approved curriculum and whether the IITs and large state universities adopt it, FY27 campus hiring numbers from TCS and Infosys, and pricing. Persistent Systems' chief executive told Reuters that clients want the same work at 25 to 30 per cent lower prices, according to Livemint, and ICICI Direct estimates AI could cut traditional IT services revenue by 2 to 3 per cent a year. AI revenue has to grow faster than that to keep total revenue flat.

Two caveats. Nasscom's FY26 employment figure is a projection, and Nambiar did not put a number on entry-level job losses in the interview as published. Read the warning as a signal about direction, not a forecast with a date. The industry remains a net job creator and the ladder stands. What is changing is the height of its first rung.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly did the Nasscom president say about freshers?
In an interview with Nikkei Asia published on 6 November 2026, Rajesh Nambiar said fresh graduates could struggle to find technology jobs because AI is automating entry-level coding and software testing. He said he would not sugarcoat the concern, and that students risk graduating with obsolete skills if computer science curricula do not change quickly.

Does this mean engineering jobs in India are disappearing?
No. Nasscom still projects about 6 million direct tech jobs in FY26 and a net addition of 1,35,000, so the sector is growing, just slowly. The change is at the entry point: the routine coding and testing work that used to be a fresher's first job is being absorbed by AI tools, while demand rises for people who can build, check and run AI systems.

What can a final-year student do right now?
Build evidence that you can work with AI rather than only without it: a small portfolio of real projects, an internship or certification, and demonstrable skill in reviewing and correcting AI-generated code. Cybersecurity, AI implementation and data work, including Indian-language data, are where hiring demand is strongest in Nasscom's own list of growth areas.
