KarmAi Launches: AI Trade Intelligence for Indian Businesses from ₹99 a Month

TL;DR

  • Gurgaon-based KARM BABA Ventures launched KarmAi on September 17 — an AI platform that puts trade intelligence in one place for Indian SMBs
  • It bundles buyer credit checks, ECGC data, LC-verified bank lists, live freight and port data, and daily commodity prices
  • Plans run from ₹99 to ₹1,499 a month, with pitch deck, PDF and image generators built in
  • The company says 3,300+ enterprises were onboarded during testing, before launch day

Every business owner in trade knows the 3 a.m. question: is this buyer actually good for the money? Gurgaon-based KARM BABA Ventures thinks KarmAi, its new AI platform that went live on September 17 at karmai.io, can answer it in seconds instead of weeks — and it's pricing the answer at ₹99 a month to start.

01.What is KarmAi?

KarmAi is an AI platform built under the company's M.A.N.A.V. Mission, aimed squarely at trade-heavy sectors — agro commodities, textiles, and exports, where one bad buyer or one badly-timed shipment can wipe out a quarter's margins. Founder and CEO Maanav Dahuja frames it as a simple belief: every business, whatever its size, deserves the intelligence that large enterprises take for granted. Yogesh Mehta, co-founder and CTO, says the focus is on being genuinely useful rather than another AI tool with a long feature list.

What caught my attention is the target audience. Most AI launches this month chase developers or enterprises with budgets. KarmAi is chasing the proprietor in Ludhiana exporting garments, or the trader in Indore moving soybean — the people for whom a credit report or freight dashboard was previously either unaffordable or simply out of reach.

02.What's actually inside

The platform's foundation is the data that used to be locked inside expensive enterprise tools. Buyer credit information lets you check a buyer's real-time standing before you commit, not after. ECGC buyer data lets you screen buyers against export credit records to gauge risk upfront. LC-backed bank data and an LC accepted banks list show which banks stand behind a buyer's Letter of Credit — and which will actually honour it before you ship.

Then there's the logistics layer: live freight rates and port movement data so shipping runs on facts instead of phone calls, latest export prices so you negotiate with knowledge, and daily commodity market updates so you price at the right moment. Corporate profile access rounds it off for due diligence before you sign anything.

03.Intelligence plus output tools

Insight alone doesn't close deals, so KarmAi adds tools that turn it into output: an image generator for product and campaign visuals, a PDF generator for ready-to-share documents, and a pitch deck generator that makes an idea investor-ready without a design team. There are weekly updates and B2B growth tips on top.

The pitch deck generator is the one I'd bet on. Putting together a decent deck is exactly the kind of task a small exporter pays a freelancer ₹5,000–15,000 for, and it's the kind of thing AI now does well. It's the same pattern we've seen this month as global AI tools get serious about rupee pricing for India — the technology is finally meeting the market where it is. And if you missed it, we covered Jio Haptik's Solo AI launch for Indian SMBs earlier, which is chasing a similar customer with a different playbook.

04.Pricing and early traction

Plans start at ₹99 and scale to ₹1,499 a month — built for growth, as the company puts it, not gatekeeping. The traction claim is worth noting: KARM BABA says it onboarded 3,300+ enterprises during testing, and that usage is already shaping the product. The group's operations reportedly span India, Kenya, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, Turkey, Iraq, and Canada, which explains the heavy export focus.

A word of caution, though: this is a launch announcement, so treat the claims as the company's own. Before you base a big credit decision on any single tool, cross-check with your bank and your own due diligence. AI platforms are excellent at pulling data together; the judgement on whether to ship a container worth of goods still belongs to you.

05.Why this launch matters more than it looks

Here's the thing nobody's talking about: the interesting part of KarmAi isn't the AI. It's the consolidation. A small exporter today juggles a customs broker, a freight forwarder, an ECGC agent, a bank relationship manager, and a commodity news subscription — five sources of truth, none of which talk to each other. If KarmAi genuinely pulls that into one screen for the price of a family dinner, that's the real story, AI or not.
