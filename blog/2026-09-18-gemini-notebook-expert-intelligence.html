Gemini Notebook Expert Intelligence: Google's AI Now Reads the Books You Buy

TL;DR

  • Gemini Notebook can now use ebooks you've bought on Google Play Books as AI sources — with 100,000+ titles from major publishers
  • As of September 17, the feature is live for all Google Workspace customers, Workspace Individual subscribers and personal Google accounts
  • New study features: voice chat in ~100 languages, lecture recording and multiple quiz formats
  • Eligible college students in 140+ markets — including India — can get a year of Google AI Plus free

If you've ever bought an ebook, read three chapters, and then quietly abandoned it on your phone — Google has news for you. Gemini Notebook Expert Intelligence, announced for all Workspace customers and personal accounts on September 17, lets you drop books you actually own into an AI notebook and grill them with questions. Combined with the study features Google added this week — voice chat, lecture recording, and a free year of Google AI for students in over 140 markets — this is quietly one of the most practical AI updates of the month. Here's the full picture.

01.What is Expert Intelligence in Gemini Notebook?

Expert Intelligence is a cross-Google initiative that connects trusted, paid content to Google's AI products — and Gemini Notebook is the first stop. In plain terms: you can now add select ebooks you've purchased from Google Play Books directly into a Gemini Notebook as a source. Once a book is in, you can ask questions about it and get answers grounded in the actual text, not in whatever the model half-remembers from the internet. You can also turn the book into infographics, Audio Overviews (those podcast-style summaries), quizzes and more.

The launch catalogue is serious: more than 100,000 books from publishers including Penguin Random House, Macmillan, Bloomsbury, De Gruyter Brill, Johns Hopkins University Press and O'Reilly Media. To check whether a book you own qualifies, open its detail page on Play Books and look for the "Tools" badge — if Gemini Notebook is listed there, you're good to go.

02.Made for studying, not just skimming

Here's where it gets genuinely useful for students and working professionals. The real power isn't the book alone — it's combining it with your own material. Google's own example: a student uploads their class syllabus and lecture notes alongside a purchased textbook, then asks Gemini Notebook to generate an Audio Overview and practice quizzes for exam prep. A manager can consult a book on giving difficult feedback while drafting the actual conversation with their team.

Google also worked with 15 bestselling authors — including Steven Pinker, Michael Pollan and Jennifer Wallace — to build curated "Featured Notebooks" that add extra sources and artifacts around their books. Honestly, what surprised me most is that publishers agreed to this at all. That's usually the industry that treats AI like a housebreaker.

By the way, if you want to get dramatically more out of any AI assistant without paying for ten different subscriptions, our GPT-6 Astra — Zero to Hero Guide has full chapters on prompting properly and optimising costs — the same discipline applies whether you're using Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude.

03.The rules Google set — and why publishers signed up

The reason publishers are on board is that Google built in ownership controls. To interact with a book in Gemini Notebook, you must own it through Google Play Books. If someone shares a notebook containing that book with you, you'll see it listed as a source — but you can't read the text or query it until you buy your own copy. There are limits: only select English-language ebooks are eligible for now, and the free-book promotion Google ran is restricted to US accounts. Google says Expert Intelligence will spread to the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search soon, with sources like third-party subscriptions, business research reports and textbooks planned as well.

04.Now live for Workspace — and personal accounts

The September 17 update is what makes this news rather than a footnote: Expert Intelligence is now available to all Google Workspace customers, Workspace Individual subscribers, and users with personal Google accounts who have Gemini Notebook, Google Play and Google Books enabled. Admins control the toggles, and organisations on Workspace for Education Plus can even buy books for groups of students directly. If you're waiting for this in India — it works with a personal Google account, so no workspace required. (We covered Google's bigger Gemini 3.8 Live launch in detail earlier this week.)

05.The student upgrade that slipped under the radar

In the same week, Google announced a batch of study features for Gemini Notebook rolling out over the next few weeks: voice chat with your notes in nearly 100 languages, lecture recording in the mobile apps, new quiz formats (multiple-choice, multiple-select, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank), and Short Video Overviews in 80+ languages — one-minute animated explainers you can share with classmates.

And the part Indian students should actually care about: eligible college students in the US get Google AI Pro free for a year, while students in more than 140 other markets — India included — get Google AI Plus free for 12 months. Google AI Pro normally runs $19.99 (roughly ₹1,900) a month, so this is not a token discount. Check your Google account's student offers page to see if you qualify.

Key Takeaways

  • Gemini Notebook now accepts 100,000+ ebooks from Play Books as AI-queryable sources — but you must own the book
  • Live since September 17 for all Workspace customers and personal Google accounts, not just paid tiers
  • Combine a book with your own syllabus, notes and files — that's where the real study value is
  • New: voice chat (~100 languages), lecture recording, more quiz formats, Short Video Overviews
  • Students in India can get Google AI Plus free for 12 months under the student offer

If you're a student in India, this week quietly removed two of your biggest excuses — "AI tools are expensive" (a free year of Google AI Plus) and "AI doesn't know my syllabus" (add your notes, your lectures and your textbook into one notebook). If you're a professional, the same pattern applies to your domain: combine a trusted book with your own documents and you get answers with citations instead of hallucinations.
