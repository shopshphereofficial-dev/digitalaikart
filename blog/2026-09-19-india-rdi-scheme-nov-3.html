India's ₹1 Lakh Crore RDI Scheme: November 3 Launch, and Why AI Startups Should Care

TL;DR

  • PM Modi will launch the ₹1 lakh crore Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) scheme on November 3
  • It offers long-term, low or zero-interest loans and equity to startups and large companies working on AI, robotics, quantum, biotech and semiconductors
  • ₹20,000 crore is already budgeted, with an ANRF-led Deep-Tech Fund of Funds alongside
  • For the first time in 2025-26, private R&D spending in India exceeded government spending — the scheme is built to push that further

Most AI funding news these days comes from San Francisco. This one comes from New Delhi, and it's arguably more relevant to your next startup or research project than anything OpenAI announced this month. The ₹1 lakh crore RDI scheme — India's Research, Development and Innovation fund — is set for its official launch by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on November 3, according to reports published today. Here's what it is, who gets the money, and why I think this is one of the most under-discussed AI stories of the week.

01.What the RDI scheme actually is

The Union Cabinet approved the scheme back on July 1. The idea is simple: India's private sector invests far too little in R&D, and deep-tech ventures — the kind that take years and serious capital before they earn a rupee — struggle to find patient money here. The RDI scheme attacks that with a ₹1 lakh crore corpus designed to build a private-sector-led research ecosystem, with a particular focus on AI and deep tech. It's structured in two tiers, managed by the Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF) and the Department of Science and Technology (DST), with ₹20,000 crore already budgeted and an ANRF-led Deep-Tech Fund of Funds to pull in venture capital alongside.

02.How the money actually reaches you

This is the part founders should read twice. The scheme provides long-term, low or zero-interest loans, or equity — not grants you spend three years writing proposals for. Union Minister Dr Jitendra Singh spelt out the eligibility logic at a recent industry event: the scheme supports transformative projects at Technology Readiness Level 4 and above, through long-term financing, equity support for startups, and dedicated deep-tech funding mechanisms. In plain English: if you've moved beyond pure lab research and have a working prototype heading toward a real product — an AI model with a commercial use case, a robotics system, a quantum sensing device — the money is designed for you. Both startups and large companies can apply.

03.Why November 3 matters

A launch date is a signal. Coming just weeks after SEMICON India 2026 pulled in ₹1 lakh crore-plus in investment commitments for the semiconductor ecosystem (we wrote about the chip design startup angle earlier), the November 3 launch tells you the government wants R&D momentum to carry into winter, not fade after the summit photo-ops. It also lands right before the US midterms and the usual year-end funding slowdown — a window where Indian startups hunting capital historically come up empty. If you're building in AI right now, mark the date and watch for the application guidelines that follow.

04.The quiet milestone nobody noticed

Here's the thing nobody's talking about. A lead essay in The Hindu this week pointed out that private R&D spending in India exceeded government spending for the first time in 2025-26. For a country where "R&D budget" has meant "government budget" since independence, that's a genuine inflection point. The same essay noted that members of the India Deep Tech Alliance have committed more than $2.5 billion to deep tech. The RDI scheme isn't creating this shift out of thin air — it's riding a wave that's already building, and adding cheap, patient capital on top of it.

05.Deep tech is already delivering

Skeptics will say India's deep-tech push is all announcements and no outcomes. The counter-evidence is stacking up. Pixxel — founded by two BITS Pilani alumni — has six hyperspectral imaging satellites in orbit with a full constellation planned. Skyroot and Agnikul are pushing reusable launch vehicles out of Hyderabad and Chennai. ImmunoACT, incubated at IIT Bombay with the Tata Memorial Centre, developed NexCAR19, India's first indigenous CAR-T cell therapy, delivered at roughly a tenth of typical treatment costs. Remidio in Bengaluru fights preventable blindness with smartphone-based AI retinal imaging. This is what patient capital plus engineering talent looks like when it compounds — and it's exactly the pipeline the RDI scheme wants to widen.

Key Takeaways

  • Launch date: November 3, by PM Modi — watch for application guidelines after
  • ₹1 lakh crore corpus; ₹20,000 crore already budgeted; two-tier structure via ANRF and DST
  • Long-term, low or zero-interest loans and equity for TRL 4+ projects in AI and deep tech
  • Startups and large companies both qualify
  • Private R&D spending already crossed government spending for the first time in 2025-26

What This Means for You

If you're a student, this changes your calculus: deep-tech and AI research in India just became a career with institutional money behind it, not just a passion project — a natural companion to the one-crore AI skilling push we covered earlier. If you're a founder, November 3 is your cue to get your TRL documentation and financials in order — the teams that understand the scheme's structure early will be the ones funded first. And if you're an entrepreneur still figuring out what to build, funding is never the hard part; the idea and execution are.
