Union Alpha: The Stealth AI Model That Rivals GPT-6 Astra

#UnionAlpha#StealthStartup#AIModels#ModelWars

TL;DR

  • A mystery model called Union Alpha appeared on OpenRouter with no developer name — and benchmarked at the same level as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1
  • Its free trial was planned for a week but crashed after one day under 1 billion tokens per minute of demand
  • The model was then unmasked as unbiased.ai's Pareto 26.9, with an official release due October 10, 2026
  • Paid pricing is $2.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens — under a quarter of Astra's original price

This week gave us the most entertaining AI story in months. On September 16, a model called Union Alpha quietly appeared on OpenRouter, the AI model marketplace, listed as a "stealth model" — no developer name, no company, no marketing. It's a multimodal model built for research, coding, and agent workflows with a 262,144-token context window, and early benchmark scores put it at the same level as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1. Then things got chaotic, in the best way.

01.A top-tier model with no name tag

Stealth drops are an old trick in the AI world — labs sometimes release unnamed models to get honest feedback before a big launch. But a stealth model claiming Astra-level performance, offered free for a week on trial? That got everyone's attention fast. Union Alpha was pitched as delivering state-of-the-art results across a wide range of tasks, and the early numbers did the talking: scores comparable to the two flagship models of the moment. If you missed why those two matter, we covered GPT-6 Astra's Critical cybersecurity rating earlier, and its arrival on Microsoft's Azure Foundry too.

02.The internet broke the free trial

Developers piled in immediately. At peak, Union Alpha's official account reported it was processing 1 billion tokens per minute — and half-jokingly posted, "If anyone has spare GPUs lying around, you know where to find us." AWS stepped in and helped triple the processing capacity overnight. It still wasn't enough. Demand hit billions of tokens per minute, speeds dropped to unusable levels, and the team pulled the plug on the free trial after just one day of the planned week, switching to paid access.

Honestly, what surprised me most wasn't the demand itself — it's what the demand signals. Tens of thousands of developers were ready to route real workloads through an unverified, anonymous model within hours. When something claims Astra-class performance for free, the AI crowd moves like it's a flash sale.

03.The unmasking: Pareto 26.9

A day after the launch, the mystery ended. Union Alpha is Pareto 26.9, the new model from a company called unbiased.ai. Their explanation for the anonymity is actually sensible: they wanted genuine user reactions without brand bias, wanted to understand the challenges that come with scaling up, and wanted to fold all of it into the official release, which is scheduled for October 10, 2026. Sneaking a real stress test in before a launch is clever; doing it with the whole industry watching was the lucky accident.

04.Here's the number that matters: the price

Now that it's paid, Union Alpha costs $2.50 per million input tokens, $0.25 per million cached input tokens, and $7.50 per million output tokens — which the team says is less than a quarter of Astra's original price. For comparison, GPT-6 Astra on Microsoft's Foundry runs $10 per million input and $50 per million output on short context, rising further on long context. In rupee terms, that's roughly ₹650-₹700 per million output tokens from Union Alpha versus several times that for Astra. The model is also available through Cloudflare's AI platform, which should help with the capacity drama.

A caveat worth stating plainly: "benchmarks comparable to Astra" is not the same as "replaces Astra in production." We don't yet have independent, third-party evaluations of Pareto 26.9, and one viral week doesn't tell you how a model behaves on your specific workload. Treat it as a promising, cheaper option to test — not a verdict.

05.Why Indian devs and startups should care

If you're building AI products in India, input costs decide whether your unit economics survive. A capable model at a quarter of frontier pricing changes what's viable — a customer-support bot that was too expensive to run at $50 per million output tokens might make sense at $7.50. This is also exactly why cost optimization deserves the same attention as prompting: it's the skill that turns AI from an expensive demo into a business. Key Takeaways

  • Union Alpha = unbiased.ai's Pareto 26.9, officially launching October 10, 2026
  • It benchmarked at GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 level during its stealth run
  • Free trial collapsed under ~1 billion tokens per minute of demand; AWS tripled capacity overnight
  • Pricing: $2.50 in / $0.25 cached / $7.50 out per million tokens — under a quarter of Astra's price
  • No independent evaluations yet — promising, but test before you trust it in production
