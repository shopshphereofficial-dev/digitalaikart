India's First AI-Made Biopic Is Here — And It's About Sir Chhotu Ram

#AIFilm#IndianCinema#OTT#CreativeAI

TL;DR

  • Regional OTT platform STAGE is producing India's first biopic made using artificial intelligence — on farmer leader Sir Chhotu Ram
  • AI is being used across the whole filmmaking process: visualisation, character generation, voice, background score and 3D scene creation
  • The goal is to change the economics of filmmaking for stories and cultures that conventional production is too expensive for
  • It's part of a growing AI slate at STAGE that already includes "Daud", "Kailasham", "12vi Wala Pyaar" and "The Last Promise"

Bollywood loves a big-budget biopic, but nobody greenlights a film about a Haryana farmer leader from the 1900s — the math simply doesn't work. That's exactly why this week's announcement caught my attention. On Friday, regional-language OTT platform STAGE said it is producing India's first AI-made biopic, on Sir Chhotu Ram, the pre-Independence farmer leader and social reformer popularly known as Dada Chhoturam. And when they say "AI-made," they mean the entire pipeline — not just cleanup work in post-production.

01.Who was Sir Chhotu Ram — and why an AI biopic?

Sir Chhotu Ram, born on November 24, 1881, is regarded as a messiah of peasants in the region. He fought for farmers' rights during British rule and was instrumental in getting pro-farmer laws enacted in the pre-Independence era. His story, as STAGE's co-founder and chief content officer Parveen Singhal put it, "carries the struggles, aspirations and dignity of millions of farmers."

Here's the thing nobody's talking about though: this is precisely the kind of subject that traditional studios would never touch. A period film set in early 20th-century rural Punjab needs sets, costumes, crowds, historical accuracy — crores of rupees before a single song is shot. STAGE's bet is that AI collapses that cost. As Parveen Singhal said, "AI gave us the creative possibility to recreate a world that would otherwise require very significant production resources."

02.AI across the whole filmmaking process

What makes this different from the usual "AI in movies" stories is the scope. Most studios use AI for dubbing, subtitling or a bit of post-production polish. STAGE says it is using AI for visualisation, character generation, voice, background score and 3D scene creation — essentially rebuilding the period world of Sir Chhotu Ram rather than filming it. The company calls it an experiment in using AI across the filmmaking process rather than at its edges.

If you've been following this space with us, you know the building blocks have been arriving all year — from the first fully AI-generated film we covered earlier this week, to Hindi voice AI that now costs 98% less. STAGE is doing something more ambitious than either: combining all of it into a biopic about a real historical figure, with the cultural depth intact.

03.The economics nobody can ignore

The uncomfortable truth of Indian cinema is that it is brutally language-hierarchical. A Hindi film gets multiplexes; a Haryanvi film fights for scraps. STAGE co-founder and CEO Vinay Singhal was blunt about it: "We believe AI can fundamentally change the economics of filmmaking, especially for stories and cultures that have historically been underserved because conventional production is expensive." His vision, in his words, is "not to use AI simply to make existing filmmaking faster; it is to rethink how films can be created from the ground up."

That's not just talk. STAGE was founded in 2019 with a focus on Haryanvi content and has since expanded into Rajasthani, Bhojpuri, Gujarati, Marathi, Bengali and Hindi markets. A growing slate of its titles is already being developed with AI production tools — "Daud", "Kailasham", "12vi Wala Pyaar" and "The Last Promise", with the "Jai Hanuman" series also part of its AI-driven digital storytelling experiments. If AI cuts production costs enough, every one of those languages gets a shot at stories that would earlier have been "too expensive" to tell.

This is also why I keep telling anyone who'll listen that the biggest AI opportunity isn't in the labs — it's in applying these tools to underserved markets. 04.The obvious caveats

Let's not pretend this is a solved problem. An AI-made biopic about a real freedom-era figure raises questions studios don't face with fiction: How accurate is an AI-reconstructed face? Who owns the likeness and the legacy? Will historians get a say? STAGE's own framing — "technology expanding what storytellers can attempt" rather than replacing them — is the right instinct, but the industry will have to build the norms as it goes. And if the output looks synthetic or the emotional core doesn't land, no amount of cost savings will save it. The first attempt probably won't be perfect. It just needs to prove the concept works at all.
