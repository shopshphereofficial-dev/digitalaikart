Hyderabad Police SETU AI Kiosk: Complaints in 9 Languages

#HyderabadPolice#SETU#AIKiosk#CitizenServices#AIGovernance

TL;DR

  • Hyderabad Police have launched SETU, an AI-powered kiosk that records complaints in nine languages
  • It's a pilot at Mehdipatnam Police Station, with phased expansion planned if it works
  • Citizens get instant acknowledgement and digital tracking of what happens to their grievance
  • The AI is a "digital receptionist" — FIR decisions still rest entirely with police officers

Walking into a police station to file a complaint is, for most of us, one of those experiences we'd rather avoid — the queues, the forms, the awkwardness of explaining your problem to a stranger in a formal setting. Hyderabad Police just launched something aimed squarely at that anxiety: SETU, an AI-powered kiosk that records complaints in nine languages, issues an instant acknowledgement, and digitally tracks what action follows.

01.What the SETU AI kiosk actually does

SETU has been introduced as a pilot project at Mehdipatnam Police Station. The idea is simple: a person walks in, explains their grievance in whatever language they're comfortable in, and the AI system converts it into a digital record on the spot. The complainant gets an acknowledgement immediately, along with an indication of what the next step is likely to be.

Police Commissioner V C Sajjanar said the goal is to make the first interaction with a police station easier and to cut delays at the reception counter. If the pilot succeeds, the plan is to expand SETU to other police stations across the commissionerate in phases. Each complaint also gets a digital tracking mechanism, so senior officers can see what happened after a grievance was submitted — and whether it's still pending.

Why nine languages matters so much here: Hyderabad is a city where a complainant might be most comfortable in Telugu, Hindi, Urdu or any of several other Indian languages. Anyone who has struggled to formally describe an incident in a language that isn't their strongest will get why this is more than a tech demo. India's language diversity is exactly where AI has the most to offer citizens — we saw the same logic earlier in BodhanAI's open models for 22 Indian languages.

02.What the AI is — and importantly, isn't — doing

Here's the part I appreciate most about how this has been framed. SETU is not an AI police officer. It's closer to a digital receptionist and transcription assistant: it listens, records, handles language differences, and places the complaint into a workflow that officers then examine. Whether an FIR should be registered, what offences apply, how the investigation proceeds — those legal decisions remain firmly with human police officers.

That boundary is the right one. The awkward conversations around AI in government tend to start when systems quietly take on decisions they were never designed for. Removing administrative friction? Yes, absolutely. Automating criminal judgment? No. Hyderabad seems to have drawn that line clearly, at least on paper.

03.Hyderabad Police have been quietly building an AI toolkit

SETU isn't coming out of nowhere — Hyderabad Police have been testing AI in citizen-facing policing all year. In January, they launched C-Mitra, an AI-supported virtual helpdesk for cybercrime victims; in its first ten days it helped facilitate more than 100 FIRs and contacted around 1,000 victims through the 1930 cybercrime helpline. In May, they launched AI-CopWriter, an app that records, transcribes and translates complaints in 10 Indian languages in real time. There's also the SOCEYE system for monitoring harmful online content.

The bigger backdrop: the Union Home Ministry said in March that all 17,798 police stations in India now use the CCTNS digital network for FIRs and chargesheets. The plumbing is digital already — SETU is the next logical layer, putting AI right where an ordinary citizen first meets the system. It's also part of a broader pattern of Indian institutions deploying AI directly in the field — from the Army's battlefield AI push to this kiosk at a police reception desk.

04.The hard questions the pilot must answer

Honestly, what surprised me most is that the biggest feature isn't the AI at all — it's the tracking. Citizens rarely know whether a written grievance was formally recorded, transferred, or quietly forgotten. A digital acknowledgement at the start and officer-visible progress until resolution creates a clearer audit trail than any reception desk ever offered.

But the pilot will have to earn trust on three practical fronts: transcription accuracy (a mis-recorded complaint is worse than a slow one), personal data protection, and a clear way to correct errors before they enter a police workflow. These are solvable problems, but they decide whether SETU spreads or stalls. Key Takeaways

  • SETU kiosk piloted at Mehdipatnam Police Station, Hyderabad — records complaints in nine languages
  • Instant acknowledgement plus digital tracking of action on every grievance
  • AI handles reception and transcription only — FIR and investigation decisions stay with officers
  • Builds on C-Mitra (100+ FIRs facilitated in first 10 days) and AI-CopWriter (10 languages)
  • Success depends on transcription accuracy, data protection and error correction

What This Means for You

If you live in Hyderabad and SETU reaches your police station, filing a complaint could genuinely get easier — especially if language has ever been a barrier for you or your family. One practical tip from the reporting: always review what the kiosk recorded before you leave, and make sure you have your digital acknowledgement in hand.
