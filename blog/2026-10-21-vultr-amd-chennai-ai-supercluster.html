Vultr to Build India's Largest AMD AI Supercluster in Chennai

#AI#AIHardware#IndiaAI#DataCentres

TL;DR

  • Vultr announced on 21 October 2026 that it will deploy AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs in a new 20 MW supercluster in Chennai.
  • It will be India's largest AMD Instinct deployment and one of the world's largest AMD AI superclusters, alongside Vultr's flagship site in Springfield, Ohio.
  • The cluster is aimed at AI labs, foundation-model builders, startups, enterprises and academic institutions, and runs on AMD's open ROCm software stack.
  • Vultr plans more capacity in 2027 with AMD MI455X GPUs and the Helios rack-scale architecture.

01.What Vultr and AMD announced

Vultr, a US cloud infrastructure company, announced on 21 October 2026 that it will deploy AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs in a new 20 megawatt (MW) supercluster in Chennai. The company says it will be the country's largest AMD Instinct deployment and will give it two of the world's largest AMD AI infrastructure sites, alongside its flagship supercluster in Springfield, Ohio. That Ohio campus is a 50 MW site where Vultr has said it will add more than 24,000 MI355X GPUs, so the 20 MW Chennai build places it among the company's largest. Vultr describes itself as the world's largest privately held cloud infrastructure company and was valued at 3.5 billion dollars in a December 2024 funding round.

The MI355X is AMD's current data-centre GPU for AI work, with 288 GB of HBM3E memory and up to 8 terabytes per second of memory bandwidth on each chip. Vultr says that memory capacity lets customers handle larger models with fewer GPUs. The Chennai cluster will run on AMD's ROCm open software stack, which supports widely used AI frameworks, libraries and models, giving developers more flexibility in how they build and deploy.

02.Why Chennai, and who it is for

Vultr says the cluster is meant for AI labs and foundation-model builders, AI-native startups, enterprises, and research and academic institutions. It will support large language model and foundation model training, fine-tuning and high-throughput inference, the work that is hardest to do in India without renting capacity abroad.

The company frames the Chennai build around data sovereignty. Enterprises deploying AI at scale need infrastructure they can access locally to meet data sovereignty, data residency and latency requirements, said J.J. Kardwell, CEO of Vultr. Jaya Jagadish, Country Head and SVP Engineering at AMD India, said India has the talent and ideas to build the next generation of AI, and that access to compute at this scale will help turn more of those ideas into reality. Both statements come from Vultr's announcement of the deployment.

03.How it fits India's AI compute picture

India already runs a public compute pool under the IndiaAI Mission, approved in March 2024 with an outlay of 10,371.92 crore rupees. As of February 2026 the government said more than 38,000 GPUs were available through its compute portal at about 65 rupees an hour, which it described as roughly a third of the global average. In October the Centre said it would add 10,000 more AI compute units, and C-DAC's tender for them opens technical bids on 30 October 2026, with 3,000 units reserved for government and public-interest work. The mission does not own the hardware: empanelled private cloud firms buy the GPUs, and the government pays part of the bill when an approved user rents them, usually 40 percent.

The Chennai cluster is private capacity, not part of that pool, but it points at the same demand. India's foundation-model teams, including the twelve shortlisted under the IndiaAI Mission, and a fast-growing set of AI startups all need compute that sits inside the country. Vultr is betting that data-residency rules, security concerns and latency will push more of that demand toward local, privately run infrastructure.

04.What it means for Indian users and businesses

For a startup or an enterprise, the practical effect of in-country capacity is simpler compliance and faster responses: data does not have to leave India to be processed, and inference runs closer to users. For researchers and students, an extra option matters when the public pool is booked or their category does not fit.

The caveat is that this is a commercial deployment, priced by a private provider rather than subsidised. Access terms and rates will be set by Vultr. It adds choice and capacity, but it does not replace the subsidised public pool that students and academics depend on.

05.What comes next

Vultr says it will add more capacity in 2027, including AMD Instinct MI455X GPUs and AMD's Helios rack-scale architecture, and it already lists 34 cloud data-centre regions across 17 countries. The Chennai build fits a wider shift, in which governments and companies move large-scale AI compute closer to where data is created instead of concentrating it in a handful of overseas regions.

For India, the test will be whether that capacity is actually used and priced within reach of the teams building here. On Vultr's own framing, the point of the Chennai site is to make training, fine-tuning and inference possible on Indian soil.

Frequently asked questions

What did Vultr announce in Chennai?
Vultr said on 21 October 2026 that it will deploy AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs in a new 20 MW AI supercluster in Chennai, describing it as India's largest AMD Instinct deployment and one of the world's largest AMD AI superclusters.

What are AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs?
They are AMD's current data-centre GPUs built for AI and high-performance computing, each with 288 GB of HBM3E memory and up to 8 TB per second of memory bandwidth. Vultr says the extra memory lets customers run larger models on fewer GPUs.

Does this replace the IndiaAI Mission's compute pool?
No. The Chennai cluster is private, commercial capacity run by Vultr. The IndiaAI Mission's public pool, which offers subsidised GPU time to startups, researchers and students, continues separately.
