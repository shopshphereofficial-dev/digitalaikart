Odysseus: The Fall — the First Fully AI-Generated Film Is Out

TL;DR

  • Fountain 0 has released Odysseus: The Fall, a 150-minute film where every scene and image is AI-generated
  • It was made by one person — Ash Koosha — working part-time over four months, for a mid-five-figure budget
  • It's streaming now at watch.fountain0.com for $9.99 (roughly ₹850) to rent, with the soundtrack on Spotify
  • Critics who saw earlier footage were brutal, calling it "AI slop" — the debate matters more than the film itself

Odysseus: The Fall is out, and depending on who you ask, it's either a milestone or a warning sign. Released on September 16 by Fountain 0 — a studio that calls itself "the leading AI movie studio" — it's the first completely AI-generated film produced at the level of a big-budget Hollywood movie. Every scene and every image in it was generated by AI. It runs 150 minutes. And the entire thing is credited to one human being. If you've been wondering when AI-generated films would stop being two-minute tech demos, the answer appears to be: this week.

01.What exactly got released

The film is a retelling of Homer's Odyssey — specifically, the fractured memory of a drowning man in his final minutes, a voyage that doubles as a trial where every monster wears his own handwriting. That's a genuinely striking premise, honestly. It's now streaming at watch.fountain0.com alongside Fountain 0's earlier film Dreams of Violets, available to rent at $9.99 — call it roughly ₹850 in India. The soundtrack also landed on Spotify today, and the lead song is an interpretation of the Seikilos Epitaph, the ancient Greek song regarded as the oldest written musical composition for which both lyrics and melody survive. Someone at Fountain 0 clearly has taste.

02.One person, four months, part-time

Here's the detail that stopped me in my tracks: the entire film is a one-person creation. Ash Koosha — writer, director, producer, and co-founder of Fountain 0 — made it working part-time over four months. No actors, no sets, no cameras. According to The Hollywood Reporter's earlier coverage, the production used the Kling AI video generator for imagery, Google's Nano Banana for core frames, Claude for language-related editing and Gemini for project research, with Fountain 0's own tech handling actor blocking and world modelling. The budget was reportedly in the mid-five figures. Compare that to Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey — the film this one cheekily sailed in behind this summer — which was produced for around $250 million. Two films on the same epic, separated by roughly four orders of magnitude in cost.

03.Is it actually any good?

Let's be honest with each other: probably not Nolan-level, and the critics who saw the earlier footage did not hold back. The Verge called Odysseus: The Fall and its marketing "AI slop," pointing to the over-glossy aesthetic and an uncanny stiffness in how the AI characters move and speak. Fountain 0's own executive chairman Tom Rogers has acknowledged that with AI video generation still being nascent, they expect feedback that the Nolan film is the superior adaptation. I'll give them this much — releasing your film while openly admitting the competition is better is a refreshingly honest marketing strategy. Still, whether it's good art and whether it's a significant technological moment are two different questions, and only one of them is really in doubt.

04.Why this matters beyond the hype

Set aside the quality debate for a second. The claim Fountain 0 is making is about economics: that its software minimises token expense so drastically that full-length, high-quality film production costs collapse to levels no AI company has come close to. Rogers puts it baldly — that 10,000 films of the quality of Odysseus: The Fall could now be created for the same cost as one Nolan film. That number is marketing, sure, but the direction is real. We've already watched AI absorb writing, image generation, and presentation-making — Anthropic literally just launched Claude Docs and Slides, which we broke down earlier this week. Full-length video was the last fortress. It's now being stormed, however clumsily.

Key Takeaways

  • Odysseus: The Fall released September 16 on watch.fountain0.com — 150 minutes, every frame AI-generated, $9.99 to rent
  • One creator (Ash Koosha) made it part-time over four months on a mid-five-figure budget
  • Built with Kling, Google Nano Banana, Claude and Gemini plus Fountain 0's proprietary pipeline
  • Critics remain sceptical — the "AI slop" criticism is fair, but the cost collapse is the real story
  • The one-person creative studio is no longer theoretical

What This Means for You

Here's what nobody's telling Indian creators: you don't need Fountain 0's budget or Nolan's to benefit from this shift. The stack that made this film — an AI video model, a language model for editing, and a stubborn solo creator — is available to anyone reading this. A YouTuber in Indore, a Tamil indie animator, a wedding-filmmaker in Delhi — the cost of making something ambitious just fell through the floor. No, AI films won't replace human ones (the backlash proves audiences know the difference). But the middle ground — AI-assisted content, indie films, regional stories that no producer would fund — just opened up.
