Amazon Alexa+ Is Now Live in India: Free Early Access, Hindi and Hinglish Support

TL;DR

  • Alexa+, Amazon's generative AI voice assistant, launched in India today — free for everyone during Early Access
  • It understands English, Hindi and mixed Hinglish, switching languages even mid-sentence
  • After Early Access it stays free for Prime members, but costs ₹2,000/month for everyone else
  • It runs on 70+ AI models via Amazon Bedrock and understands desi phrases like "dhai baje" and "bhanja"

If you've had an Echo speaker sitting in your kitchen for years, today is the day it gets a serious brain transplant. Amazon has launched Alexa+ in India — its generative AI-powered personal assistant — and the Alexa+ India launch is the biggest upgrade the assistant has ever received here. It's conversational, it's personalised, and most importantly for us, it's authentically Indian. Amazon is offering it free under Early Access on eligible Echo devices, Fire TV and the Alexa app starting today.

01.What exactly is Alexa+?

The old Alexa was basically a very obedient command-taker. You said a specific thing, it did that specific thing. Alexa+ is different — it's built on generative AI, so you can talk to it the way you'd talk to a person. You don't have to repeat the "Alexa" wake word after every request, you can switch topics mid-conversation, and you can even mix Hindi and English in the same sentence without confusing it. A conversation started on your Echo Show can continue on the Alexa app with the context intact.

Teena Sidana, Country Manager for Alexa at Amazon India, said Indian households are "multi-generational, multilingual, and beautifully complex" — and Alexa+ was designed for exactly that complexity, letting each family member interact the way that feels most natural to them.

02.The pricing: free now, ₹2,000/month later

Here's the part everyone's asking about. During Early Access, Alexa+ is free for all customers. Once that period ends, it stays free only for Prime members. If you're on Prime Lite, Prime Shopping Edition, or you don't have Prime at all, the standalone subscription will cost ₹2,000 per month — that's ₹24,000 a year, which is more than an annual Prime membership itself.

Honestly, that price tells you two things. One: running generative AI at this scale is genuinely expensive. Two: Amazon is using Alexa+ to push people into the full Prime ecosystem. You can check if your device qualifies at amazon.in/alexaplus, or get immediate access by buying a new eligible Echo or Fire TV device.

03.Hindi, Hinglish and "dhai baje" — built for Indian homes

This is where Alexa+ gets genuinely impressive. Amazon has trained it on Indian-specific phrases that don't translate directly into English — it understands "dhai baje" (half past two), "ek paav", and family relationship terms like bhanja, bhateeji, jethani and devar. Anyone who has tried explaining these words to a foreign voice assistant knows how big a deal this is.

Much of this work — speech recognition tuned for Indian homes and models for handling code-mixed Hinglish — came from Amazon's teams in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Chennai. The assistant has also been tuned for Bollywood references, cricket and Indian festivals. More Indian languages beyond Hindi and Hinglish are on the way, though Amazon hasn't given a timeline yet.

04.Under the hood: 70+ AI models on Bedrock

Technically, Alexa+ isn't one model — it's built on Amazon's Bedrock platform and routes requests across more than 70 large language models, including ones from Amazon and Anthropic. Think of it as a traffic cop that sends your question to whichever AI model handles it best. This multi-model approach is the same direction the whole industry is moving in, and it's why voice assistants suddenly feel ten times smarter than they did a year ago.

It's an interesting contrast with Sarvam AI's Indus app, which we covered recently — India's homegrown, voice-first answer to ChatGPT. And on the global voice AI front, Google's Gemini 3.8 Live launch, which we wrote about in detail earlier, shows how fierce this voice-first competition has become.

05.What it can actually do

Beyond conversation, Alexa+ can control smart home devices from brands including Atomberg, Qubo, Dreame and Wipro using natural language — and set up routines through voice alone. On the shopping side, it can build shopping lists, track Amazon.in deliveries, alert you about price drops, compare product features and summarise verified reviews.

For entertainment, it works with Amazon Music, Spotify, Apple Music, JioSaavn, Prime Video and Netflix — though your existing subscriptions to those services still apply. Support for EazyDiner, Swiggy, District by Zomato, MakeMyTrip, Home Triangle and TripAdvisor is coming soon, which would make Alexa+ a genuine everyday assistant rather than a novelty.

06.Should you try it today?

Yes — mainly because it's free right now, and there's no downside to kicking the tyres. If you own an eligible Echo or Fire TV device, just check amazon.in/alexaplus. The Early Access period is the best window to figure out whether an AI assistant actually fits into your daily routine before deciding if it's worth ₹2,000 a month.

My suggestion: test it with the messiest, most Hinglish requests you can think of. Ask it to set an alarm for "dhai baje" or to explain something like a family member would. That's where the real difference between old Alexa and Alexa+ shows up — not in demo videos, but in your own living room.

Key Takeaways

  • Alexa+ launched in India on September 16 — free for all during Early Access on eligible Echo, Fire TV and the Alexa app
  • Supports English, Hindi and code-mixed Hinglish, with more Indian languages planned
  • After Early Access: free for Prime members, ₹2,000/month standalone for everyone else
  • Built on Amazon Bedrock, routing across 70+ LLMs including Amazon and Anthropic models
  • Understands desi phrases like "dhai baje", "bhanja" and "jethani"; Swiggy, MakeMyTrip and Zomato integrations coming soon

What This Means for You

For most Indian households, this is the first time a genuinely capable generative AI assistant arrives on a device you already own — no new purchase, no technical setup. If you're a Prime member, you're getting a serious AI upgrade baked into your subscription. If you're not, ₹2,000 a month is a real decision, and the Early Access window is your chance to evaluate it properly.
