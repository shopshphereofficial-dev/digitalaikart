India's New ₹1,000 Crore Deeptech Fund: Asiana-JCC Explained

TL;DR

  • Asiana Fund and Taiwan's JC Capital have launched a ₹1,000 crore fund for Indian deeptech startups
  • The corpus is ₹600 crore plus a ₹400 crore greenshoe, targeting 12-15 startups at Series A and B stages
  • Focus areas: semiconductors, robotics, advanced manufacturing, materials, aerospace and defence
  • The twist: portfolio startups get direct access to Taiwan's semiconductor and electronics ecosystem

Most deeptech funding news in India is about software AI. This one is different. On Friday, Asiana Fund — the investment platform of the Dani Family Office — and Taiwan-based venture firm JC Capital announced the Asiana-JCC Advanced Manufacturing & Innovation Fund, a ₹1,000 crore war chest aimed squarely at Indian startups building hard things: chips, robots, and defence tech. And it lands at exactly the right moment, with Semicon 2.0 in full swing and global capital hunting for India's hardware winners.

01.How the ₹1,000 crore is structured

The fund comprises a primary corpus of ₹600 crore and a greenshoe option of ₹400 crore, which lets the managers raise more if deal flow justifies it. The sponsors from both Asiana Fund and JC Capital are committing more than 20% of the corpus themselves — a healthy skin-in-the-game signal that this isn't a quick fee-collection exercise. It plans to build a portfolio of 12 to 15 startups, primarily at the Series A and Series B stages, with a portion reserved for follow-on investments into winners.

02.Where the money will go

The sectors read like a national priority list: advanced manufacturing and robotics, semiconductors and computing, materials and sustainability, and aerospace and defence. The initial deal pipeline is even more specific — compound semiconductors, motion-control systems for EVs and robotics, secure silicon, smart grids, robotic vision, and optoelectronics. If you're an engineer in any of these fields wondering whether India has patient capital for deep work, this fund is your answer for the next few years.

03.The Taiwan connection is the real story

Here's the part nobody's talking about enough: this isn't just money. JC Capital's investors are more than 80% corporate limited partners across the US, Japan, Taiwan and Malaysia. The fund explicitly plans to connect Indian startups with Taiwan's legendary semiconductor and electronics ecosystem — technology transfers, supply chain introductions, manufacturing scale-up support and routes to international markets. For a country trying to move from assembling chips to designing them, that bridge matters as much as the rupees.

It also complements what the government is already doing. IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said at Semicon India 2026 that global companies have committed around ₹1 lakh crore under the Semicon 2.0 initiative. We covered that push in detail when Semicon 2.0 launched, and this fund is the private-capital mirror of the same bet.

04.Who's behind it

Asiana Fund is the investment platform of Asian Paints co-promoter Jalaj Dani, who also chairs aerospace startup EndureAir and robotics company Addverb. Since 2017 it has deployed more than ₹700 crore across 11 private equity investments. JC Capital, founded in 2019, has invested over ₹1,000 crore across 30 startups, with a portfolio spanning semiconductors, AI and clean technology. So both sides have actually written cheques in this territory before — this is not a first-time experiment.

05.Why now?

The timing lines up with three things happening at once. First, the India Semiconductor Mission and PLI schemes have made deep hardware a policy priority. Second, AI is creating ravenous demand for compute, sensors and power infrastructure — every data centre announcement needs chips, cooling and materials. And third, global investors want a China+1 hedge, and India is the obvious candidate. Deeptech is capital-hungry and slow, which is exactly why dedicated funds with patient corporate backing are needed rather than typical three-year VC cycles.

Key Takeaways

  • ₹1,000 crore fund (₹600 crore corpus + ₹400 crore greenshoe) from Asiana Fund and JC Capital
  • Targets 12-15 Indian startups at Series A/B in semiconductors, robotics, aerospace and defence
  • Sponsors are committing 20%+ of the corpus themselves
  • Taiwan ecosystem access — supply chains, tech transfer, manufacturing scale-up — is the differentiator
  • Complements Semicon 2.0's ₹1 lakh crore in committed global investment
