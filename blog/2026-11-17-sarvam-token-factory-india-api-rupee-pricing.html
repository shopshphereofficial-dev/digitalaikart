Sarvam Token Factory: one API, rupee pricing, Indian GPUs

#Sarvam#AI#India#Developers

TL;DR

  • Sarvam AI has opened Token Factory to developers and businesses, with one API key for its own models and global open-weight ones.
  • Models run on Sarvam's GPU clusters in India, and the bill is in rupees rather than dollars.
  • Introductory pricing for Sarvam 105B is Rs 10 per million input tokens and Rs 25 per million output tokens, before GST.
  • The API is OpenAI-compatible, so switching often means changing the endpoint and the model name.

01.What Sarvam opened

Sarvam AI has opened its Token Factory platform to developers and businesses, India Today reported on 17 November 2026. The platform puts Sarvam's own models and a set of global open-weight models behind a single API, and it runs the work on GPU clusters located in India.

The idea will be familiar to anyone who has used OpenRouter: pick a model, call one endpoint, pay for the tokens you consume. Sarvam's version adds a local angle. The company is not forwarding prompts to servers run by the model makers abroad. It hosts the models itself, says it operates India's largest cluster of Nvidia B200 GPUs, and is expanding capacity for more models and heavier demand.

02.One key, one endpoint, many models

The practical detail that matters most is that the API is OpenAI-compatible. A developer who already has an application wired to OpenAI's API can point it at Sarvam by changing the endpoint and choosing a model, and keep using the same Sarvam API key, software development kit and credits across every model in the catalogue.

The launch catalogue lists Sarvam 105B alongside GLM 5.3, GLM 5.3 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1 Pro and Google's Gemma 4 31B. Sarvam's wider catalogue also names Qwen 3.8 27B, an embedding model and its Prism decision model. The pitch is that a team can route a cheap model to bulk background work, a stronger one to coding, and a Sarvam model to Indian-language tasks, without signing three separate contracts or standing up three separate deployments. Switching back is the same change in reverse, which matters when a cheaper model turns out to be good enough for a job you had budgeted for a large one.

03.Why hosting in India is the selling point

Where a model runs is no longer a detail that only infrastructure teams care about. Banks, insurers, hospitals and government departments in India increasingly ask vendors where data is processed before they sign. Sarvam says running the models on its own infrastructure in the country gives businesses greater control over that question and can cut response times for users here.

It is worth being precise about what this does and does not settle. Hosting a model in India changes where a prompt is processed. It is not a compliance certificate, and it does not by itself answer every obligation a regulated business carries. What it does give a developer is one less thing to explain in a procurement review, and a latency profile that does not include a round trip to another continent. For a support bot answering a customer in Coimbatore, that difference is felt in the seconds the customer waits.

04.The price list, in rupees

Unlike most AI services, which quote in dollars, Sarvam has published its Token Factory prices in rupees. Sarvam 105B costs Rs 10 per million input tokens and Rs 25 per million output tokens at introductory pricing, which makes output 2.5 times the price of input. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash comes in two versions: a Standard option meant for background tasks at Rs 14 per million input tokens and Rs 55 per million output tokens, and an Interactive option for coding and anything needing faster replies at Rs 25 and Rs 105 respectively.

For scale, Kimi K3.1 Pro is listed at Rs 200 per million input tokens and Rs 950 per million output tokens, so the gap between the cheapest and the strongest model in the catalogue is wide. All of these prices exclude GST, and Sarvam says the rates for other models vary.

05.What it changes for an Indian team

For a small studio or a solo developer, the useful part is the shape of the bill. Rupee billing removes a currency conversion and a card that may or may not clear an international charge, and per-token pricing means a prototype can cost a few hundred rupees rather than a monthly minimum. Being able to swap models by changing a string means a team can test an idea on a small open-weight model and move it to a bigger one only when the results justify the spend.

For students and researchers, the more interesting lesson is architectural rather than commercial: the model is becoming a swappable component, and the durable work is in the data, the evaluation and the workflow around it. Sarvam's Token Factory does not settle which model is best for your task. It makes that question cheaper to answer, which is a reasonable thing to ship on a Tuesday.
