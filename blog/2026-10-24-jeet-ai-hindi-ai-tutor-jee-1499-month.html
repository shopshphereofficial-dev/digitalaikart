JEET AI Launches Hindi AI Tutor for JEE at ₹1,499 a Month

#AIEducation#JEE#AITutor#EdTech

TL;DR

  • JEET AI has launched a Hindi AI tutor for JEE and Foundation courses starting at ₹1,499 a month.
  • It teaches concepts on a virtual board, tests understanding and adapts to each student.
  • The company positions it as a cheaper alternative to coaching that can cost lakhs of rupees.
  • NEET and CBSE board courses, plus Tamil and Kannada, are planned in the coming months.

01.What has launched

JEET AI has introduced an AI tutor that it describes as India's first to actually teach a subject rather than simply answer questions. The product was announced on 22 October 2026 and is aimed at school and competitive-exam students. It starts at ₹1,499 per month and is available in Hindi, with the company saying it wants a great personal teacher to be within reach of every student.

The company says the tutor behaves like a teacher sitting beside the student. It teaches a concept, writes and annotates on a virtual board, plays relevant multimedia, and uses interactive simulations to explain difficult ideas. In chemistry, for example, it can illustrate a reaction, explain the science behind it, and pause to check whether the student has followed before moving on. The whole lesson is meant to feel like a guided class rather than a search box that waits for a question.

02.Why it matters for Indian students and families

For years, students preparing for exams like JEE have depended on three things: coaching centres, recorded lectures and free videos. Teaching a large batch makes truly personal attention almost impossible, and one-to-one tutoring has stayed expensive enough to be out of reach for most families. JEET AI is selling the promise of that personal attention at a fraction of the usual cost, and pitching it as a single platform instead of a pile of separate tools.

The company points out that conventional coaching programmes can cost lakhs of rupees, often without individual attention. Its pitch is that a dedicated AI teacher can now reach families nationwide at ₹1,499 a month, and that students in smaller towns should not be left out because of income, location or language. If it works as described, it could change how middle-class families weigh the cost of exam preparation.

03.How the tutor works

Unlike a chatbot that waits for a question, the tutor takes the lead. It explains a topic step by step, asks questions and tests the student throughout a lesson, and changes its explanations based on how well the student is doing. Students can interrupt at any point, ask unlimited questions and request an explanation again without hesitation or judgment, which the company says removes the fear many students feel about asking a doubt twice.

Lessons end with targeted practice and assessments designed to spot learning gaps, so a student can see which concepts need more work. JEET AI says it has built the product on more than fifteen years of content, teaching material and education research from Extramarks Education, the company led by its founder Ritvik Kulshrestha. That background in school content is what the company is leaning on to argue that this is teaching, not just conversation.

04.Courses, languages and what comes next

JEET AI has started with JEE and Foundation courses taught in Hindi. Support for other Indian languages, including Tamil and Kannada, is planned in the coming months. The company also plans to add NEET preparation, CBSE board preparation and other courses over the coming weeks and months, building out from engineering entrance exams towards a broader school syllabus.

That rollout matters because India's exam-preparation market is huge and deeply multilingual. Starting with Hindi covers a very large group of students, but the real test will be whether the tutor teaches as well in Tamil, Kannada and other languages, and whether its content is accurate for each syllabus. Students and parents will also want to know how the monthly price compares with the free AI tools they already use for doubts.

05.The bigger picture

JEET AI is one of a growing number of Indian efforts to use AI in education. Over the past month, several states have announced AI training for lakhs of students, and institutions have launched free AI tutors and AI labs. The idea running through all of them is the same: use AI to give every student some of the attention that only the most privileged could once buy. AI is already part of how many Indian students study, from doubt-solving apps to free online tutors, and what is new here is the attempt to package a full teaching experience into a monthly subscription.

Whether that promise holds will depend on results, not announcements. Families will judge the tutor on how well it explains difficult topics, how accurately it marks practice, and whether it genuinely improves exam performance at a price they can afford. For students, the more useful question is simpler: does it help them understand a chapter they were stuck on, better than what they have now.
