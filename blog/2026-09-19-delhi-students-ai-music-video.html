Delhi Students Made an AI Music Video in 10 Days

TL;DR

  • A Delhi team of multimedia students produced a complete birthday music video for PM Modi using 2D/3D design, animation and generative AI — in just 10 days
  • There was no singer, no dancers and no actors — the entire video is computer generated
  • The students insist it wasn't "one prompt and done": real skill in animation, camera angles and composition was needed to direct the AI
  • Delhi now has a four-year degree course in design and animation skills at DSEU, launched with MAAC

There was no singer in a recording studio, no dancers rehearsing at Connaught Place, no actors facing a camera. Yet a group of multimedia students in Delhi produced a full-fledged music video — visuals, music, everything — using 2D and 3D design, animation, motion graphics and generative AI. The occasion: Prime Minister Narendra Modi's birthday. The timeline: ten days.

01.How the AI music video came together

The video was released by the Delhi government as part of its 'Sewa Sankalp Abhiyan', and created by students associated with Maya Academy of Advanced Creativity (MAAC). The project began casually — during discussions between the academy and minister Ashish Sood, the idea of a birthday song came up, and the team was given roughly fifteen days' notice, leaving about ten working days to deliver. The only brief: develop a birthday wish from a Gen Z point of view, with complete creative liberty on everything else.

Four students — Adarsh Kumar Mishra, Mahtab Alam, Ayush Sharma and Subhadeep Das, mentored by Ravi Sharma — handled the design, content, lyrics, visuals and video. The software stack ran from Premiere Pro, Photoshop, After Effects and Audition to the AI tools layered on top. It was one of two songs released on the occasion; the other was prepared and performed by government school students. The launch happened at the Secretariat in the presence of Chief Minister Rekha Gupta, minister Ashish Sood and the students.

02.The myth-busting part nobody expected

Here's the detail I found most interesting — and it's the opposite of the "AI will do everything" narrative. The students were categorical: this was not a case of typing a prompt and getting a finished video. They needed to know 2D and 3D design themselves to give the AI the right prompts, and they had to understand animation, camera angles, composition and lighting to generate footage that actually looked professional. "People think using generative AI means using a prompt that will give it all to you, but that is not the case," the team said.

If you've been reading our coverage of AI creativity in India — like STAGE OTT's AI-made biopic or Pocket FM's AI fiction engine — you'll notice the same pattern: AI collapses the cost of production, but the humans who direct it well are the ones with genuine craft. The tool is only as good as the hand guiding it.

03.Why this matters for creative careers

A video like this once needed a budget, a crew, a shoot schedule and weeks of post-production. A four-person student team with laptops just replaced all of that. That's simultaneously exciting and terrifying if you're studying design or media — the entry barrier to producing studio-quality work has crashed, but so has the value of knowing only the mechanical parts of the job.

Delhi's education system is responding in parallel: the state has launched its first four-year degree course in design and animation skills at the Delhi Skill and Entrepreneurship University (DSEU), in partnership with MAAC. The message is clear — learn the craft and the AI together, not one or the other.

Key Takeaways

  • Delhi students built a complete AI-generated music video for PM Modi's birthday in about 10 days, with zero performers
  • The team used 2D/3D design, animation and motion graphics alongside generative AI tools
  • Real design skill was essential — the AI amplified their craft rather than replacing it
  • Delhi Skill and Entrepreneurship University now offers a four-year design and animation degree with MAAC
  • The barrier to studio-quality production has collapsed for small teams and students
