IndiaAI LLM Funding: Evaluations Reach Final Stage

TL;DR

  • Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw says evaluation of LLM applications under the IndiaAI Mission is in its final stage
  • Funding for selected teams will be awarded in the coming weeks
  • Around eight homegrown firms are expected to receive incentives, including subsidised GPU compute, under MeitY's ₹1,500 crore scheme
  • The scheme drew roughly 500 applications; BharatGen, the IIT-led consortium, is a likely beneficiary

While Silicon Valley argues about slowing AI down, India is quietly speeding up its own AI pipeline. Union IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw announced this week that the evaluation of large language model (LLM) applications under the IndiaAI Mission is in its final stage — and that funding for the selected teams will be handed out in the coming weeks. For India's homegrown AI builders, this is the moment two years of policy work starts turning into actual money.

01.What Vaishnaw actually announced

Speaking about the LLM development track of the IndiaAI Mission, Vaishnaw said the evaluation of LLM applications is in its last leg, with funding awards planned over the coming weeks. He also pointed to AI adoption picking up across education, healthcare and industry as evidence that the mission's groundwork is starting to pay off. It's a short statement, but the timing matters: the last-mile evaluation means the shortlists are essentially done, and the cheque-writing is next.

02.Who's in line for the IndiaAI money

Here's the context from earlier reporting. Under MeitY's ₹1,500 crore scheme for indigenous foundational models, around eight homegrown firms are poised to receive incentives — primarily subsidised GPU compute rather than plain cash grants. The scheme drew roughly 500 applications, which gives you a sense of how many Indian teams are seriously building models. Among the likely beneficiaries is BharatGen, the consortium of IIT researchers building India's own foundational models. Earlier rounds of the mission already backed Sarvam AI, Gnani.ai, Soket Labs and Gan.ai — some of the names now becoming familiar beyond India's AI circles. Sarvam, for instance, launched its Indus chat app for Indian-language users earlier this year, which we covered in detail a few days back.

03.The bigger IndiaAI Mission picture

The LLM funding is one piece of a much larger puzzle. The IndiaAI Mission was approved in March 2024 with an outlay of ₹10,371.92 crore, aiming to build an end-to-end AI ecosystem — compute, datasets, indigenous models, startups, skills and safety. On compute, the numbers have genuinely overshot expectations: against an initial target of 10,000 GPUs, the mission has deployed over 38,000, with a goal of 100,000 by December 2026. Eligible users can get up to 40% subsidy on cloud AI compute. And in February this year, the government unveiled three state-backed models — Sarvam's LLM, the multilingual BharatGen, and Gnani's voice model — all required to keep citizen data inside the country. That sovereignty requirement is why institutions like the NTA are now using Indian GPUs for exam translations, a story we explored earlier this week.

04.Why this matters for Indian builders

What surprised me most is how under-discussed this programme is among the very people it could help most. Ask around in a college hostel or a coworking space in Bengaluru, and everyone knows about ChatGPT's latest feature — but very few know that the government is about to fund a handful of Indian teams to build foundational models, or that subsidised compute exists at all. If you're a student, researcher or startup in this space, the next few weeks are worth watching: the teams that get funded will be hiring, and the ecosystems around them — data annotation, evaluation, tooling, Indic-language datasets — will grow. For professionals, it's a reminder that "AI skills" in India increasingly means skills relevant to Indian-language, Indian-infrastructure problems, not just copying what works in California. Key Takeaways

  • LLM application evaluations under the IndiaAI Mission are in the final stage; funding awards come in the next few weeks
  • About eight homegrown firms are expected to get incentives under the ₹1,500 crore scheme, out of ~500 applications
  • BharatGen (IIT-led consortium) is a likely beneficiary; Sarvam, Gnani, Soket and Gan.ai were backed in earlier rounds
  • The wider mission is worth ₹10,371.92 crore, with 38,000+ GPUs deployed against a 10,000 target and 40% compute subsidies
