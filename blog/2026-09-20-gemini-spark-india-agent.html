Gemini Spark in India: Google's 24/7 AI Agent Explained

#GeminiSpark#GoogleAI#AIAgents#GeminiIndia#Gemini

TL;DR

  • Google is rolling out Gemini Spark in India for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers — a 24/7 background AI agent, reportedly powered by a Gemini Flash model
  • Spark runs on Google's cloud and keeps working even when your device is offline — no prompt-babysitting required
  • It connects natively with Gmail, Docs and other Workspace apps to handle multi-step tasks, schedules and reservations
  • Globally, Spark has expanded fast — from a US-only Ultra beta to AI Pro subscribers in 160+ countries

Here's a sentence that would have sounded like science fiction two years ago: Google now offers an AI assistant in India that works for you around the clock, on Google's own servers, even while your phone is switched off. Gemini Spark — the company's always-on AI agent — is now rolling out to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in India, and it marks a genuine shift from chatbots you talk to, to agents that simply get things done.

01.What exactly is Gemini Spark?

Spark isn't another chatbot skin. It's a 24/7 background agent: you assign it a job — draft replies, manage your schedule, track a reservation, run a multi-step workflow — and it executes autonomously on secure cloud infrastructure. Because it runs on Google's side rather than your device, it keeps chugging along even when your phone is offline or you're asleep. Reports say it's powered by a Gemini Flash model, which is Google's efficiency-focused line — fast enough for constant background work without costing Google a fortune in compute.

02.What it can actually do for you

The killer feature for most people will be the native Workspace integration. Spark connects to Gmail, Docs and other Google apps to complete multi-step tasks: triaging your inbox, managing calendars, tracking bookings and automating routine workflows — all without you watching over its shoulder or re-prompting every thirty seconds. That's the fundamental difference from regular Gemini: you stop directing and start delegating. Anyone who has spent an evening going back and forth with a chatbot to plan a trip knows exactly how tedious "assisted" can feel. Spark is Google's answer to that fatigue.

03.How to get it in India

You'll need a paid Google AI subscription — Spark is available on the AI Pro and AI Ultra plans. Internationally, AI Pro sits at $19.99 a month (roughly ₹1,700, though India pricing typically lands lower) with Ultra tiers at $99.99 and above. Globally, Spark launched at I/O 2026 as a US-only, Ultra-only beta and expanded within weeks to AI Pro subscribers in over 160 countries — India included in that wave. Notably, it still isn't available in the EEA, UK, Switzerland or Nigeria, so India getting it early is a small but real signal of how seriously Google rates this market.

04.The bigger picture: 2026 is the year of agents

Sure, you could dismiss Spark as a subscription perk. But zoom out: every major lab is now shipping agents. Meta launched Muse, its personal AI agent that books and buys for you — we covered its breakout launch when it hit Macs last week. Anthropic has Claude Cowork, Amazon has shopping agents, and OpenAI keeps pushing agentic features across ChatGPT. Indian players are in the race too — ixigo's Smart Lock, the AI agent that hunts cheap flights for you, is one of my favourite homegrown examples. Google's move with Spark is arguably the deepest integration of the lot, because most of us already live inside Gmail, Calendar and Docs — no new app to download, no habits to change.

05.The honest caveats

Now the part I'd tell a friend over chai. An always-on agent with access to your email and documents is a genuine privacy trade-off — you're trusting Google's cloud to act on your behalf, continuously. These are early days for agents too: rival products have had publicised stumbles, with test reports of agents stalling, uploading data users didn't authorise, and finding ways around guardrails. None of that is a reason to avoid Spark — but it is a reason to start small. Give it low-stakes tasks first (draft the email, summarise the thread), and don't hand it your most sensitive workflows on day one. For a solid grounding in how far you should let agents go, our GPT-6 Astra guide's chapters on AI agents and safety cover exactly where to draw those lines.

06.What to watch next

Three things will tell you whether Spark becomes truly useful in India: whether agentic browsing (currently a US-first feature that lets Spark fill forms and complete checkouts in Chrome) reaches us; whether Indian-language support goes beyond chat into agent tasks; and whether Google bundles Spark into Workspace business plans here, which would put it in every office that runs Gmail. Google has been laying India-specific groundwork for a while — Gemini models now run inside India for data-residency reasons, which matters to any enterprise handing an agent its documents. My bet? Within a year, "I'll ask my agent to do it" will be as normal in Indian offices as "I'll ping you on WhatsApp" is today.

Key Takeaways

  • Gemini Spark is Google's always-on AI agent, now rolling out to AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in India
  • It runs on Google's cloud — continuing tasks even when your device is offline
  • Native Gmail and Docs integration handles multi-step tasks, schedules and reservations
  • Went from US-only beta to 160+ countries in months — India is part of the early wave
  • Agents are the defining AI trend of 2026: Meta Muse, Claude Cowork and now Spark

What This Means for You

If you're a professional drowning in email and calendar admin, Spark is the first agent that fits your existing workflow rather than demanding a new one — worth trying on the Pro plan for a month. For students and freelancers, the lesson is bigger: the skill of the next decade is delegating well to AI, and that starts with understanding how agents think, where they fail, and how to instruct them precisely.
