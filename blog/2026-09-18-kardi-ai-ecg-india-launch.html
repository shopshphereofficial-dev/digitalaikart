Kardi Ai Comes to India: AI-Powered Long-Term ECG Heart Monitoring, Explained

TL;DR

  • Czech healthtech company Kardi Ai is launching its AI-powered long-term ECG monitoring system in India, with the official debut on September 26 in Hyderabad
  • A wearable ECG chest strap plus AI analysis tracks your heart rhythm for days while you live normally — catching intermittent problems that a short ECG or 24-hour Holter misses
  • It's prescription-grade, holds an Indian MD-15 licence and EU MDR Class IIa certification — not a fitness smartwatch
  • The India entry comes via Hyderabad-based Eurasia Medical Technologies, and matters in a country where heart disease is the leading cause of death

Here's a statistic that should worry every Indian family: cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in India, and around 220 million Indians live with hypertension. Yet many dangerous heart-rhythm problems are intermittent — they come and go, showing up at 3 AM and vanishing by the time you reach the clinic. That's exactly the gap Kardi Ai, a Czech healthtech company, is trying to close as it prepares to launch its AI-powered long-term ECG monitoring technology in India on September 26 in Hyderabad.

01.What is Kardi Ai and how does the ECG monitoring work?

The system has three parts: a lightweight wearable ECG chest strap, mobile connectivity, and AI-supported ECG analysis. Strap it on, go about your day — office, walk, sleep — and it continuously monitors your heart rhythm for long stretches. The AI combs through that extended recording to flag abnormalities that a 10-minute ECG at the clinic would almost certainly miss.

The official announcement was made on Friday at FTCCI in Hyderabad by Dr. Ramana Jagarlamudi (Director – International Operations & Authorized Representative – India, Kardi Ai) and Dr. Raghavendra Rao (Scientific Adviser & Co-Founder, Eurasia Medical Technologies LLP). The launch event itself — themed "From signal to insight for healthier lives" — is scheduled for September 26 at the Daspalla Hotel, Jubilee Hills, Hyderabad, and will bring together cardiologists, electrophysiologists and hospital leaders for clinical sessions on arrhythmia detection and remote cardiac care.

02.Why India needs long-term heart monitoring

The diagnostic math is brutal. A standard ECG captures a few minutes of heart activity. A Holter monitor, the traditional next step, covers 24–48 hours. But arrhythmias — irregular heartbeats that raise stroke risk — often strike unpredictably, maybe once a week. Doctors call these intermittent abnormalities, and catching one is partly luck.

Long-term monitoring changes the odds: the longer the window, the more likely a fleeting rhythm problem actually gets recorded. And because the analysis is AI-supported, thousands of hours of heart data can be screened quickly instead of being reviewed manually, with physicians focusing on what matters. India's doctor-to-patient ratio makes that efficiency not a luxury but a necessity — it's the same logic behind the AIIMS mammogram-reading AI we covered earlier, where AI acts as a tireless second doctor.

03.Prescription-grade, not a fitness gadget

This is the part worth underlining, because your smartwatch also claims to check your heart. Kardi Ai's device is being introduced in India as a prescription-grade medical technology rather than a consumer fitness product. It has secured an MD-15 licence from the Government of India for import and sale, and it is CE marked under the EU medical-device framework as an EU MDR Class IIa product.

In plain terms: it's regulated medical equipment intended to assist physicians, not replace their clinical judgment. The India launch is being facilitated by Hyderabad-based Eurasia Medical Technologies LLP, Kardi Ai's Indian arm — another example of the city quietly becoming a serious healthtech hub.

04.The bigger picture: AI and remote cardiac care in India

Here's the thing nobody's talking about enough: a single launch like this fits into a much larger shift. With 220 million hypertensive Indians and heart disease as our top killer, remote monitoring could genuinely change outcomes — especially for patients in tier-2 and tier-3 cities who can't shuttle to a metro hospital every time they feel a flutter. Combined with efforts like JanAI's plan to put AI within reach of rural India, and AI healthcare emerging as one of the biggest startup opportunities in the country, the tools for early detection are finally arriving at population scale.
