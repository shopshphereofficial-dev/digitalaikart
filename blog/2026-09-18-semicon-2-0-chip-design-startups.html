Semicon 2.0: India Targets 200 Chip Design Startups

#Semicon2#ChipDesign#IndiaSemiconductors#Semiconductors

TL;DR

  • Semicon 2.0 aims to create at least 200 chip design startups and companies in India, up from about 105 attempts under Semicon 1.0
  • The mission has a ₹1.27 lakh crore outlay, and roughly ₹1 lakh crore in investment commitments have already landed since launch
  • Eligible semiconductor startups and MSMEs can get up to ₹15 crore in seed funding, plus equity co-investment support
  • India trained 70,000 chip design engineers in four years and now wants 1 lakh factory-floor technicians on top

If you dismissed Semicon 2.0 as just another scheme with a big number attached, Union IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw's address at SEMICON India 2026 in New Delhi this week was a proper reality check. Speaking at the inaugural session of the event — which Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated on Thursday, and which we covered in detail earlier — Vaishnaw laid out exactly how India plans to turn chip design from a niche career into a startup-heavy industry. The headline target: at least 200 chip design startups and companies under Semicon 2.0, backed by real money.

01.Why chip design is pillar number one

Semicon 2.0 rests on six pillars: design, machines and materials, fabrication units, advanced packaging, R&D, and talent. Notice which one comes first. Under Semicon 1.0, more than 105 startups attempted chip design, and about 20 of them secured venture capital funding worth around ₹800 crore. "That was just the beginning. This time our ambition is much bigger," Vaishnaw said, adding that the government wants startups designing chips "across the spectrum of semiconductor requirements."

Here's the thing nobody's talking about: chip design is the least capital-hungry entry point into semiconductors. A fab costs tens of thousands of crores; a design team costs salaries, EDA tool licences, and patience. That's why this pillar is where students and small teams can realistically compete — and why the government is pushing it first.

02.Follow the money — all ₹1.27 lakh crore of it

The numbers are genuinely large. Semicon 2.0 was approved in July 2026 with a total outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore, and Vaishnaw revealed that investment commitments of roughly ₹1 lakh crore have already come in since launch — from players in capital equipment, materials, specialty gases, and advanced packaging, to be deployed over the next two to three years. For context, Semicon 1.0 was approved in December 2021 with ₹76,000 crore, and ended up approving twelve manufacturing units with over ₹1.64 lakh crore of cumulative investment.

For startups and MSMEs specifically, the scheme offers up to ₹15 crore in seed funding, equity co-investment support for VC- or PE-backed companies, and deployment-linked incentives for semiconductor IPs, chips, and SoCs launched after the scheme was announced. Big silicon wafer fabs with at least ₹20,000 crore investment qualify for support of up to 40% of eligible capex.

03.The talent pipeline is ahead of schedule

This was the part that surprised me most. Against a Semicon 1.0 target of training 85,000 design engineers over ten years, India has already trained 70,000 in just four years. More than 400 universities now have access to the latest EDA tools from Cadence, Siemens, and Synopsys, and the Semiconductor Laboratory in Mohali is being used to tape out chips designed by students — yes, actual student-designed silicon. Under Semicon 2.0, the government additionally wants to develop 1 lakh skilled technicians, cleanroom personnel, and factory-floor talent for the upcoming fabs.

04.Why the AI crowd should care

You might run an AI blog, an agency, or a data-science team and wonder what chips have to do with you. Plenty. Every AI model you use — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, all of them — runs on silicon, and India's semiconductor demand is expected to reach $110 billion by FY30 and over $200 billion by FY35. Yet between FY17 and FY25 the country imported nearly $150 billion worth of semiconductor products, with imports growing at 23% a year. If that trend continues, annual imports could hit $240 billion by 2035. A domestic chip ecosystem is compute sovereignty — the same instinct behind the IndiaAI Mission's GPU push.

05.Six pillars, one philosophy

Vaishnaw recalled guidance from the Prime Minister at the start of the programme five years ago: "Don't think short term, think long term. Don't think fab, think ecosystem." That philosophy shaped the six pillars — from machines and materials (capital equipment, chemicals, gases) to more display, memory, silicon, compound, and logic fabs, to scaling advanced packaging on the back of a 3D packaging facility coming up in Odisha, industry-driven applied R&D with academia, and the talent push. The mission is openly aimed at Viksit Bharat by 2047, which tells you the time horizon the government is working on.

Key Takeaways

  • Semicon 2.0 targets at least 200 chip design startups and companies, up from 105 attempts in phase one
  • Total outlay: ₹1.27 lakh crore; around ₹1 lakh crore in investment commitments already secured
  • Startups and MSMEs can access up to ₹15 crore in seed funding plus equity co-investment support
  • 70,000 design engineers trained in four years; 400+ universities now have EDA tool access
  • India's chip demand could cross $200 billion by FY35 — domestic design capacity is the hedge
